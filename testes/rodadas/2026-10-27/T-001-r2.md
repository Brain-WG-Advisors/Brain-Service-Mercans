## Resposta

**Short answer:** the severance amounts go under CRA **0054**, split into E (exempt portion) and I (excess), and spread across the 12 months before termination. Unused vacation goes under **0006 / I**. This means the answer we gave in SR-81 in April was wrong on two points.

**What the TGSS CRA manual (updated 1 July 2026) says**

- **Unused vacation paid at termination: CRA 0006, I only.** The concept covers payments for vacation that was paid but not taken when the employment contract ends. It is reported only in the month of payment and settled via L13. If the follow-on vacation period runs into the next month, the amounts are split between the two months. A fraction of a day goes under CRA 0001 instead.
- **Severance for dismissal or termination: CRA 0054, I and E** (§2, CRA 0051–0054):
  - The exempt portion must be reported as E even when there is no included portion. Any portion that is part of the contribution base is reported as I.
  - Both the E and I amounts are prorated over the twelve months before the month of the event.
  - The exempt cap is the maximum set by sector rules or the collective agreement, or otherwise by the Estatuto de los Trabajadores. So the SS exempt cap can differ from the IRPF exempt amount.
- **Employer's payment in lieu of notice (falta de preaviso): CRA 0054, I.** Because the manual treats it as more salary than indemnity, it must be declared as included. This is the change introduced in the 01/07/2026 version.

**Correction to the April SR-81 reply:**

1. We said 58184, 58186 and 58188 are all reported as 0054/E in the termination month, and that 58197/58198 are not reported in CRA. The manual's 12-month proration applies to CRA itself, not only to BASES. That makes the prorated pair 58197/58198 the correct CRA lines, which matches Lily's original mapping. Reporting 58184, 58186 or 58188 as a lump sum in the termination month as well would double-report the indemnity.
2. 58188 (Severance Final Taxable Excess) cannot be E. Any excess above the obligatory amount is included in the base, so it must be I.
