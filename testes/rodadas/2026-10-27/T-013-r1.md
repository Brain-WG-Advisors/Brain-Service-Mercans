## Resposta

**Verdict: all of them are taxable. None qualifies under art. 206 num. 1 or num. 2. They are ordinary labor income, subject to withholding, and eligible only for the general 25% exemption (art. 206 num. 10).**

| Payment | Art. 206 exempt? | Withholding |
|---|---|---|
| Common-illness incapacity, days 1–2 (employer) and day 3+ (EPS) | No | Yes |
| Work-related (ARL) temporary incapacity | No (current DIAN doctrine, see note) | Yes |
| Maternity leave | No | Yes |
| Paternity leave | No | Yes |
| Shared/flexible parental leave (Ley 2114/2021) | No | Yes |

**Basis**

- **DIAN Oficio 027408 (100208221-002582) of 2 Nov 2019, points 7.1–7.4.** Art. 206 num. 1 refers to indemnities for work accidents or illness, so incapacity payments for common illness and work accidents are not exempt, because they are not indemnities. Num. 2 refers to indemnities protecting maternity, not maternity leave, so maternity leave is not exempt. Paternity leave is not exempt for the same reason, and because none of these is exempt, withholding must be applied to these payments.
- **Consejo de Estado, Sección Cuarta, exp. 22620 (2019).** The court held that num. 2 does not cover maternity-leave payments. It covers the indemnity for labor protection of maternity regulated in art. 239 CST, which is the 60-day indemnity for dismissing a pregnant or postpartum worker without authorization.
- **Oficio 23438 of 2015 / Oficio 000321 of 2015.** These hold that incapacity payments substitute for salary while the worker is off work due to certified illness, and they revoked the earlier 1998, 2008 and 2014 doctrine that had treated incapacity payments as exempt.

**Note on ARL incapacity:** DIAN's doctrine is internally inconsistent here. Oficio 23438/2015 said num. 1 covers indemnities for work accidents and the economic benefits for incapacity caused by illness or accident of occupational origin, within the limits of parágrafo 1. The later Oficio 027408/2019 (point 7.1) treats incapacity from work accidents as not exempt and makes no distinction by payer. The 2019 position is the current one.

**Parental leave is my inference.** I found no DIAN pronouncement specifically on the Ley 2114/2021 shared or flexible leave. It is a *licencia*, not an *indemnización*, so the 2019 reasoning applies directly.

**What is exempt under num. 1 and num. 2:** only true indemnities. That means the ARL lump sum for permanent partial disability and the art. 239 CST dismissal indemnity. Both are capped by art. 206 par. 1, under which the exemption in numerals 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 applies only to the amounts corresponding to the legal minimum under labor law; any excess is not exempt.

**2026 depuration (art. 388 ET):** the 25% exemption is capped at 790 UVT per year, which is $41,375,460 at the 2026 UVT of $52,374. It sits inside the 40% global limit. DIAN concluded in Concepto 11383 of 2024 that the 1,340 UVT cap applies to determining net taxable income in the return, not to withholding, which is governed by art. 388 and its 40% limit only.
