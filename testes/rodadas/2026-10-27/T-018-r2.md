## Resposta

**Short answer:** don't model Irish PAYE as a static progressive bracket table on gross pay. Model it as a **two-band calculation with employee-specific parameters, run on a cumulative year-to-date basis**, with separate branches for Week 1/Month 1 and Emergency.

**Why a bracket table doesn't fit**

The 20% band limit (the Standard Rate Cut-Off Point, SRCOP) and the tax credits are not country constants. They differ by employee and arrive on the Revenue Payroll Notification (RPN). Revenue's own guidance says the amounts of personal tax credits and rate band applied in any pay period are decided by the relevant tax basis, and income may be taxed on a cumulative basis, Week 1 basis or emergency basis. Only the 20% and 40% rates belong in a country-level table.

**Recommended reg structure**

*Employee-level inputs, from the RPN:* tax basis, SRCOP, tax credits, and pay/tax to date.

*Country-level effective-dated parameters:* the rates (20%/40%) and the emergency-basis values.

*Cumulative basis (the default):* under this basis, an employer must apply tax credits, tax cut-off points and USC cut-off points up to and including the week in which the pay day falls.

```
CumPay     = TaxablePay_YTD (incl. current period)
CumSRCOP   = SRCOP cumulated to current period no.
CumCredits = Credits cumulated to current period no.
GrossTax   = 20% × min(CumPay, CumSRCOP) + 40% × max(0, CumPay − CumSRCOP)
NetTaxYTD  = max(0, GrossTax − CumCredits)
PeriodTax  = NetTaxYTD − TaxDeducted_YTD   // may be negative = refund
```

For fortnightly and four-weekly payrolls, map the period to Revenue's cumulative week number. For example, for fortnightly pay, payment in week 1 or 2 uses cumulative values at week 2, and week 3 or 4 uses week 4.

*Week 1/Month 1 basis:* this is the same formula using single-period values with no accumulation. Here neither pay, tax credits, nor the standard rate cut off point nor USC cut off points are accumulated, and refunds are not triggered, as refunds only occur in a cumulative basis. Floor PeriodTax at 0.

*Emergency basis:* this needs its own branch. It is used when no RPN is available. The employer taxes income at the standard rate until week 4, and from week 5 onwards income is taxed at the higher rate with no tax credits. It is not a band calculation.

*Basis linkage:* PAYE and USC are on the same basis. The basis applied to PAYE and USC deductions is always tied, so drive both regs from one basis indicator.

**Default values for test cases only**

These values should not be hard-coded per employee, since the RPN governs. For 2026, the standard rate band is €44,000 for single/widowed, €53,000 for a married couple with one income and €88,000 for a married couple with two incomes, where the increase is capped at the lower of €37,500 or the lower earner's income. Under Budget 2027, announced 6 October 2026, these become €46,500 / €55,500 / €93,000. The rates stay at 20% and 40%, and personal and PAYE credits go from €2,000 to €2,125. The 2027 figures still depend on the Finance Act being enacted.

The Week 53 edge case (pay dates on 30 or 31 December) is covered in Employers' Guide to PAYE para. 14.3 and needs its own handling.
