Premium rate and contributions

Paid Leave makes time for some of life's most important moments. Like many insurance plans, Paid Leave is funded by premiums paid by employers and employees.

Overview

What are premiums?

Like many insurance plans, Paid Leave is funded by premiums shared by employers and employees. The premium rate is a percentage of an employee's wages.  

For large employers who already offered private paid leave plans, the move to a state plan often results in cost savings. And for small employers, Minnesota Paid Leave makes a critical employee benefit affordable when it might not otherwise be. 

Premium rate

For 2026 and 2027, the Paid Leave premium rate is 0.88%. This rate covers Family Leave (0.27%) and Medical Leave (0.61%).  

Small employers pay less. The Paid Leave small employer rate is 0.66%. 

The premiums are split between employers and employees. Under the Paid Leave law, employers can collect up to 0.44% from employees, or employers can choose to cover more. Premiums are collected by the state from employers.

How was this calculated?

Premiums Provided By This Calculator Are Unofficial

Premium calculations provided by this calculator are unofficial and are only intended to provide an estimate for premium contributions.

How this calculation works:

To determine the total contribution, the payroll total entered above is multiplied by the premium rate of 0.88%. This value is then divided to give the total contribution amount for an employer's reported employee count by week, month, quarter, or year. The calculator displays the total contribution owed by the employer for their reported employee count, and the total contributed by all of their employees.

This calculator assumes premium contributions are split 50% between employers and employees. Employers may choose to cover up to 100% of the total premium for employees, but may not collect more than 50% from employees.

Small employers pay a reduced premium rate under the Paid Leave law. This calculator finds the average employee wage and compares it to the criteria for the small employer rate: the number of employees must be 30 or fewer, and the average employee wage must be less than 150% of the statewide average annual wage. If an employer qualifies, the reduced premium rate is applied. The maximum contribution from employees in this case is the same as an employee of a large employer.

More about Paid Leave premiums

How premiums work

Premium rates are set to ensure Paid Leave can cover payments and serve Minnesotans who need it. The 0.88% premium rate for 2026 and 2027 covers Medical Leave (0.61%) and Family Leave (0.27%).

Premiums are capped at the Old-Age, Survivors, and Disability Insurance (OASDI) limit. This is the same wage base used by the Social Security program. For Paid Leave, this means wages up to $185,000 are subject to premiums.

Small employer premium rate

Paid Leave small employers pay a reduced premium rate of 0.66%. To qualify as a small employer, you must meet limits on how many people you employ and how much you pay in wages.  

You must employ 30 or fewer workers in each quarter. You must also pay an average wage of no more than 150% of the statewide average wage, currently $27,745.88 per quarter. Paid Leave calculates your average wage by dividing wages from your highest-paid quarter by your highest employee count. These numbers may come from different quarters.   

Employer and employee contributions

Employers can collect up to 0.44% of wages from employees to cover their portion of the premium. Employers can choose to cover more of the premium for some or all of their employees.

Premium rate adjustments

The premium rate is set annually by July 31 for the following year. The premium is based on how the program is running, and best budgeting practices to keep the fund at a healthy level. The rate is informed by an independent actuarial study. The premium rate cannot be more than 1.1% as set in Minnesota's Paid Leave law. 

Pay your Paid Leave premiums

Employers submit Paid Leave premium payments.  

Premiums are due quarterly. The first premiums were due April 30, 2026. Log in to your Employer Account at ui.mn.gov to pay your premiums. 

How do I pay Paid Leave premiums? 
  1. Log in to your Employer Account at ui.mn.gov
  2. On your home page, select Paid Leave payment information
  3. Select Paid Leave payments and account summary
  4. Click Make Paid Leave Payment 

If you have a UI balance, you must pay that first. Follow the prompts to make a UI payment, and then you will be able to pay your Paid Leave premium. For detailed, step-by-step payment instructions, visit Make a Paid Leave premium payment on the UI website. 

When are future premium payments due?

Paid Leave premiums and wage detail reports are due on the same schedule each quarter. You can submit both of them through your Employer Account at ui.mn.gov. 

Quarter Paid Leave premiums and wage detail report due 
1st Quarter (January 1 – March 31) April 30 
2nd Quarter (April 1 – June 30) July 31 
3rd Quarter (July 1 – September 30) October 31 
4th Quarter (October 1 – December 31) January 31 

Note: If the due date falls on a weekend or a legal holiday, it is extended to the next business day.
