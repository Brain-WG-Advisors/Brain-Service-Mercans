# Module 9: Termination & Irregular Payments

*Objective: To detail calculation and tax rules for non-standard payments.*

## 9.1 Termination Payments

### What are the different types of termination payments?

A Colombian termination settlement (*liquidación de prestaciones sociales*) is made up of two families of
payment: **accrued entitlements**, which are always due whatever the reason for termination, and
**indemnities**, which are due only in defined circumstances.

**Family A — accrued entitlements. Always payable, whatever the reason for termination, including
dismissal for cause and employee resignation.**

| # | Payment | Basis | Legal source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Salary to the termination date | `(S + T) × days ÷ 30` | Labour Code art. 127 |
| A2 | **Severance savings** (*cesantías*) | One month's salary per year of service and pro rata for a fraction of a year | Labour Code art. 249 |
| A3 | **Interest on severance savings** | 12% per year on the balance, proportional to the elapsed part of the year | Law 52 of 1975 art. 1 |
| A4 | **Service bonus** (*prima de servicios*) | 15 days per semester, pro rata to the termination date | Labour Code art. 306 |
| A5 | **Unused vacation** | 15 working days per year of service, pro rata | Labour Code arts. 186, 189 |
| A6 | Overtime, premiums and commissions earned but unpaid | As earned | Labour Code arts. 127, 134 |
| A7 | Work clothing owed but not delivered | Value of the undelivered items | Labour Code arts. 230, 232 |

**Family B — indemnities and conditional payments.**

| # | Payment | When due | Legal source |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Indemnity for dismissal without just cause** | Employer terminates without a just cause listed in art. 62 | Labour Code art. 64 (as amended by Law 789 of 2002 art. 28) |
| B2 | **Late-payment penalty** (*indemnización moratoria*) | Salaries and statutory benefits unpaid at termination | Labour Code art. 65 (as amended by Law 789 of 2002 art. 29) |
| B3 | **Penalty for non-deposit of severance savings** | The employer failed to deposit severance savings to the fund by 14 February | Law 50 of 1990 art. 99 num. 3 |
| B4 | **Penalty for non-payment of severance interest** | Interest unpaid in January | Law 52 of 1975 art. 1 num. 3 |
| B5 | **Indemnity for dismissal during protected status** | Dismissal of a pregnant or nursing employee, an employee on certified incapacity, an employee with a disability, or an employee with trade-union immunity — requires prior authorisation from a labour inspector or a judge | Labour Code art. 239; Law 361 of 1997 art. 26 |
| B6 | **Payment in lieu of notice** | Only where the contract or a collective agreement provides for it — **there is no statutory notice period for the employer** | Contractual |
| B7 | **Negotiated settlement / mutual-termination bonus** | By agreement | Contractual |
| B8 | **Pension bonus** (*bono pensional*) | On transfer between pension regimes; not an employer payroll payment | Law 100 of 1993 |

**Not a termination payment:** the employee's own 30-day notice on resignation from an indefinite-term
contract (Labour Code art. 47, as substituted by Law 2466 of 2025 art. 5). That article expressly forbids
any penalty on an employee who omits the notice: *"In no case may a penalty be agreed for an employee
who omits the notice described here."*

### How do termination rules and calculations differ by residential status and employer type?

**By residence status: the labour-law entitlements are identical; only the tax treatment differs.**

| Aspect | Resident | Non-resident |
|---|---|---|
| Severance savings, interest, service bonus, vacation | **Identical** — the Labour Code applies to every employee working in Colombia, whatever their nationality or tax residence (Labour Code art. 2) | **Identical** |
| Dismissal indemnity | Identical | Identical |
| **Tax on severance savings and interest** | Exempt in full where the average monthly income of the last 6 months ≤ 350 tax units (COP 18,330,900); tapered above | **Flat 20%** on the gross under Tax Code art. 408 — the art. 206 num. 4 exemption does not attach to a non-resident's flat withholding |
| **Tax on the dismissal indemnity** | Fully taxable within the ordinary monthly base; art. 383 progressive | **Flat 20%** |
| **Tax on accrued vacation and service bonus** | Ordinary treatment | **Flat 20%** |
| Social security on the settlement | Vacation is contributory; the other accrued entitlements and the indemnity are not | **Identical** |

**By employer type.**

| Employer type | Difference |
|---|---|
| **Legal person, income-tax filer** | Standard. Qualifies for the Tax Code art. 114-1 exemption |
| **Natural-person employer with 2 or more employees** | Standard. Qualifies for the exemption |
| **Natural-person employer with fewer than 2 employees** | **No exemption** — art. 114-1 inciso 2 excludes them: *"The foregoing shall not apply to natural persons who employ fewer than two employees"* |
| **Taxpayer at the special rate of Tax Code art. 240 par. 8** | **No exemption** — art. 114-1 par. 6, added by Law 2133 of 2021 art. 31 |
| **Public higher-education institutions** | No apprenticeship training levy — art. 114-1 par. 5 |
| **Temporary employment agency** | The agency is the employer of the assigned worker and owes the full settlement. Law 2466 of 2025 art. 45 adds that where the statutory limits on agency use are breached, the **user undertaking is treated as the true employer** and the agency as a mere intermediary, on a prior judicial declaration |
| **Contractors and subcontractors** | Labour Code art. 34, as substituted by Law 2466 of 2025 art. 44 — the principal is jointly and severally liable for the contractor's labour obligations where the work is within the principal's ordinary line of business |

**By contract type — this changes the indemnity, not the accrued entitlements.**

| Contract type | Indemnity for dismissal without just cause |
|---|---|
| **Indefinite term** | Tiered by salary and seniority — see the table below |
| **Fixed term** (maximum **4 years** under Labour Code art. 46 as substituted by Law 2466 of 2025 art. 6) | **The salaries corresponding to the remaining term** |
| **Specific work or task** (*obra o labor*) | The remaining term of the work, **never less than 15 days** |
| **Apprenticeship** | A special fixed-term labour contract governed by the Labour Code (art. 81, as substituted by Law 2466 of 2025 art. 21); in the practical or dual phase the apprentice is entitled to *"all the benefits, allowances and other rights proper to an employment contract"* |

### Detail the calculation and tax withholding rules for unused leave paid out on termination.

**Calculation.**

```
Vacation payout = vacation base × days of service since the last settlement ÷ 720

where vacation base = the ordinary salary being earned, EXCLUDING
                      rest-day work and supplementary overtime hours only
                      (the night premium and commissions stay IN)
                      and EXCLUDING the transport allowance
Variable salary     -> average of the immediately preceding year (art. 192 num. 2)
```

The 720 divisor derives from 15 working days per 360 days of service expressed in monthly-salary terms:
15 ÷ 30 = 0.5 months per 360 days.

**Tax withholding.**

| Aspect | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Taxable?** | **Yes, fully.** Vacation compensation is ordinary taxable employment income. There is no exemption for it, and it does **not** carry the art. 206 num. 4 severance-savings exemption |
| **Which base?** | The ordinary monthly withholding base of the termination month, under Procedure 1 or Procedure 2 |
| **Separate table read?** | **No.** Only the statutory minimum service bonus gets a separate read, and only under Procedure 1 |
| **25% exempt-income deduction?** | **Yes**, it participates in the ordinary depuration within the 40% and 790-unit caps |
| **Social security** | **Contributory.** Vacation is **paid rest, not a statutory benefit**, so the payout enters the **contribution base** and the **payroll-levy base**. This is the single most frequently missed line on a Colombian termination settlement |
| **Non-resident** | Flat 20% on the gross under art. 408 |

### Describe the rules for statutory severance/redundancy payments.

Colombia has **no redundancy regime as such**. There is no economic-dismissal procedure, no collective
redundancy consultation for the ordinary case, and no separate redundancy payment. A dismissal for
economic reasons is simply a **dismissal without just cause**, compensated by the art. 64 indemnity.

**What Colombia does have instead is severance *savings* — a different instrument entirely.**

**(a) Severance savings (*auxilio de cesantía*) — Labour Code art. 249.**

> *"Every employer is obliged to pay its employees … on termination of the employment contract, as
> severance savings (auxilio de cesantía), **one month of salary for each year of service and proportionally for any fraction of a
> year**."*

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Entitlement** | One month's salary per year of service, pro rata for a fraction |
| **Base** | **Labour Code art. 253:** the **last monthly salary**, provided it has not varied in the last **3 months**; otherwise, and for variable salaries, the **average of the last year of service**, or of the whole period if shorter. The base includes the **transport allowance** |
| **Annual regime** (Law 50 of 1990 art. 99, for all employees hired from 1 January 1991) | Liquidated at **31 December** each year and **deposited to the employee's chosen fund before 15 February** (i.e. by 14 February). **Late deposit penalty: one day's salary for each day of delay** |
| **Monthly advance deposit — new** | **Law 2466 of 2025 art. 64** adds a paragraph to Law 50 of 1990 art. 99 permitting the employer to deposit **8.33% of the base monthly** as an advance deposit. This is **not** a partial payment of severance savings — the employee can access the money only in the cases the law allows or on termination |
| **Monthly interest — new** | The same article permits the parties to **agree in writing to pay the interest monthly**, at **1% of the base** |
| **Direct payment to the employee** | **Labour Code art. 57 par. 4**, added by Law 2466 of 2025 art. 15: where the employer proves direct payment to the employee, that payment is **discharging** and **no non-deposit penalty applies**, unless it is shown that the payment was not made freely or was not applied to a legally authorised purpose. **Affiliation to a severance-savings fund nonetheless remains compulsory** |
| **Partial payments** | **Labour Code art. 254** prohibits partial payment before termination except in the expressly authorised cases (housing acquisition, improvement or debt release; education of the employee, spouse or children), on pain of losing the sums paid |
| **Loss of the right — art. 250** | Delictual acts against the employer or the employer's relatives; intentional grave material damage to buildings, works, machinery or raw materials; disclosure of technical or commercial secrets causing grave prejudice. The employer may withhold payment until a court decides |
| **Pre-1991 regime** | Employees hired before 1 January 1991 who did not migrate remain on the retrospective annual-adjustment regime. **Reported as a separate line on the information return** |
| **Construction workers — art. 310** | Special regime: **3 days' salary per completed month** |
| **All-inclusive salary** | Absorbed |

**(b) Interest on severance savings — Law 52 of 1975 art. 1.**

> *"… shall recognise and pay interest of **12% per annum** on the balances that, at **31 December** of each
> year, or on the dates of the employee's departure or of a partial settlement of severance savings, the employee holds to their credit
> in respect of severance savings. … The interest … must be paid **in the month of January** of the following year … or
> on the date of the employee's departure … in an amount proportional to the part of the year elapsed. … If the
> employer fails to pay the employee the interest established here … it must pay the wage earner, by way
> of **indemnity and on one occasion only**, the amount [equal to the interest owed]."*

The interest is paid **to the employee directly**, not to the fund — unlike the principal.

**(c) Indemnity for dismissal without just cause — Labour Code art. 64.**

| Employee's salary | ≤ 1 year of service | Each subsequent year and pro rata |
|---|---|---|
| **Below 10 minimum wages** (< COP 17,509,050) | **30 days'** salary | **+20 days'** salary |
| **10 minimum wages or above** | **20 days'** salary | **+15 days'** salary |

Transitional paragraph: employees with **10 or more years** of continuous service at the entry into force
of Law 789 of 2002 retain the earlier Law 50 of 1990 art. 6 literals b), c) and d) scale.

**(d) The social-security clearance condition — Labour Code art. 65 par. 1.** An easily missed gate:

> *"In order to proceed with the termination of the employment contract …, the employer must inform the
> employee in writing, at the last registered address, **within the sixty (60) days following the
> termination**, of the payment status of the social security and payroll-levy contributions on the
> salaries of the **last three months** preceding the termination, attaching the payment receipts …
> **If the employer does not prove payment of those contributions, the termination of the contract shall have no
> effect.** However, the employer may pay the contributions during the following sixty (60) days,
> with default interest."*

**(e) Statement of cause — Labour Code art. 66.** The terminating party must state the cause **at the
moment of termination**; different causes may not validly be alleged afterwards.

### Provide the statutory formula for any tax-free portion.

**The tax-free portion attaches to the severance savings and their interest, and to nothing else.**

**Tax Code art. 206 num. 4:**

```
Determine A = the average MONTHLY income over the LAST SIX MONTHS of employment,
              expressed in tax units (1 unit = COP 52,374 in 2026)

If A <= 350 tax units (COP 18,330,900):
    severance savings and interest are EXEMPT IN FULL

Otherwise apply the taper:
```

| Average monthly income | Untaxed portion | 2026 band boundary (COP) |
|---|---|---|
| 350 – 410 tax units | **90%** | 18,330,900 – 21,473,340 |
| 410 – 470 tax units | **80%** | 21,473,340 – 24,615,780 |
| 470 – 530 tax units | **60%** | 24,615,780 – 27,758,220 |
| 530 – 590 tax units | **40%** | 27,758,220 – 30,900,660 |
| 590 – 650 tax units | **20%** | 30,900,660 – 34,043,100 |
| From 650 tax units | **0%** | From 34,043,100 |

**No other termination payment carries a tax-free portion.**

| Payment | Tax-free portion |
|---|---|
| Severance savings and interest | **Yes** — the taper above |
| Service bonus | **None** |
| Unused vacation | **None** |
| Salary to the termination date | **None** |
| **Dismissal indemnity** | **None** in the general case. Tax Code art. 206 nums. 1–3 exempt accident, illness and maternity-protection indemnities, and art. 206 par. 1 limits even those to the statutory minimum amount: *"operates only on the amounts corresponding to the statutory minimum … the excess is not exempt"*. A dismissal indemnity under art. 64 is not within nums. 1–3 and is **fully taxable** |
| Late-payment penalty | **None** |
| Negotiated settlement bonus | **None** |

**Also excluded from the base entirely.** Severance savings and their interest are **excluded from the
monthly withholding base** under both Procedure 1 and Procedure 2 (Tax Code arts. 385 and 386). They are
not simply exempt — they never enter the monthly calculation at all, and the art. 206 num. 4 exemption is
applied when the payment itself is made. **Also, the 25% exemption of art. 206 num. 10 does not apply to
severance savings** (art. 206 par. 2).

### Deliverable: Detail the rules for any applicable concessional tax cap

**Not Applicable — Colombia has no concessional tax cap on termination payments.**

There is no equivalent of a lifetime or per-event cap on concessionally taxed termination payments, no
tax-free redundancy allowance and no reduced rate for termination income. The only concessional treatment
in the Colombian termination settlement is the **art. 206 num. 4 exemption on severance savings and their
interest**, which is:

- **income-tested**, not capped by amount — the test is the average monthly income of the last six months
  of employment, and the exemption tapers from 100% to 0% across the 350-to-650-tax-unit range;
- **not a lifetime allowance** — it applies to each termination on its own facts;
- **not extendable** to any other termination payment.

Three related caps exist and must not be confused with a concessional termination cap:

| Cap | What it limits | Applies to termination? |
|---|---|---|
| 790 tax units per year on the 25% exempt income (art. 206 num. 10) | The general labour-income exemption | Yes, to the ordinary monthly base of the termination month — but **not** to severance savings (art. 206 par. 2) |
| 1,340 tax units per year on combined deductions and exempt income (art. 336 num. 3) | The total of deductions and exemptions | Yes, to the ordinary base |
| 40% of the post-non-taxable-income figure (art. 388) | The same total | Yes, to the ordinary base |

**Also not applicable:** there is no averaging, no spreading, no top-slicing and no separate termination
schedule. The dismissal indemnity is taxed as ordinary employment income in the month it is paid.

### Deliverable: Provide a detailed worked example of a redundancy termination payout

**Facts.** Employee terminated **without just cause** on **31 August 2026** for economic reasons — in
Colombian law simply a dismissal without just cause, since no redundancy regime exists.

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Hire date | **1 March 2022** |
| Termination date | **31 August 2026** |
| Total service | 4 years and 6 months = **1,620 days** (360-day years) |
| Monthly salary | **COP 4,500,000**, unchanged for more than 3 months |
| Transport allowance | Not due — salary exceeds 2 minimum wages (COP 3,501,810) |
| Last severance-savings settlement | 31 December 2025 → **240 days** accrued in 2026 (1 Jan – 31 Aug) |
| Last service-bonus payment | 30 June 2026 → **60 days** accrued (1 Jul – 31 Aug) |
| Vacation | 15 days taken in 2024; **510 days** of service accrued since the last vacation settlement |
| Occupational risk | Class I, 0.522% |
| Employer | Legal person, income-tax filer |
| Withholding procedure | Procedure 1 |
| Average monthly income, last 6 months | COP 4,500,000 = **85.92 tax units** — well below 350 |

|---|---|---|

## 9.2 Irregular and Retrospective Payments

### Describe the specific tax calculation method required for irregular payments.

**Colombia has no special method for irregular payments.** There is no averaging, no spreading, no
top-slicing, no annualisation and no separate schedule. An irregular payment is added to the taxable
payments of the month in which it is paid, and the ordinary calculation runs.

**The four exceptions**, all of which are rules about *particular payments*, not about irregularity as such:

| Exception | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Statutory minimum service bonus, Procedure 1** | **Separate** read of the art. 383 table on the bonus alone (art. 385) |
| **Severance savings and interest** | **Excluded** from the monthly base under both procedures; art. 206 num. 4 exemption and taper applied on payment |
| **Procedure 2 generally** | The fixed percentage smooths irregular payments across the semester by construction — the payment raises the base the percentage is applied to but does not change the percentage until the next June or December recalculation |
| **Sub-30-day periods, Procedure 1** | Gross to 30 days, read the table **percentage**, apply that percentage to the actual payment (art. 385) |

**The consequence, stated plainly.** Under **Procedure 1** a large irregular payment in a single month
pushes the depurated base into a higher marginal band **for that month only**, producing a spike in
withholding. That is arithmetically correct — the employee did receive that income in that month — and it
is reconciled in the employee's own annual return, not by the employer. Under **Procedure 2** the same
payment produces a smaller spike, because the fixed percentage is unchanged.

**Two caps bite hardest on irregular payments**, and the engine must apply them monthly:

| Cap | Monthly figure 2026 |
|---|---|
| 790 tax units per year on the 25% exempt income | 65.8333 units = **COP 3,447,955** |
| 1,340 tax units per year on combined deductions and exempt income | 111.6667 units = **COP 5,848,430** |

A one-off payment large enough to make 25% of the depurated base exceed COP 3,447,955 will hit the first
cap — as demonstrated in the retention-bonus example in Module 6.3 and the termination example above.

**Social security on irregular payments.** An irregular payment that is *salary* enters the contribution
base of the month it is paid, subject to the **25-minimum-wage ceiling** and, where a non-salary pact
applies, to the **40% clamp**. A large one-off payment is therefore commonly the month in which the ceiling
or the clamp bites for the first time.

### How are backdated payments and retrospective salary adjustments handled?

**The controlling principle is that Colombian payroll is settled on a *payment* basis, not an accrual
basis, for tax and social security alike.** A retrospective payment is generally taxed and contributed in
the month it is **paid**, not in the months to which it relates.

| Aspect | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Withholding tax** | The arrears are added to the **taxable payments of the month of payment** and the ordinary calculation runs. There is **no** re-opening of prior months and **no** re-computation of prior withholding. Tax Code art. 383 applies to *"payments or credits to account"* — payments or credits — so the trigger is the payment |
| **Statutory benefits** | The arrears **retrospectively increase the base** for the service bonus, severance savings, interest and vacation for the periods to which they relate. These must be **recalculated and topped up**, because the statutory benefit is measured on the salary that was actually due |
| **Overtime and premiums** | A retrospective salary increase raises the ordinary hour value for the affected periods, so overtime and premiums already paid must be **topped up** |
| **Electronic payroll XML** | Reported through an **adjustment note** (`NominaIndividualDeAjuste`) where a validated document must be corrected, or within the current month's ordinary document where the arrears are simply a payment of the current month |
| **Form 350** | The arrears withholding is declared in the **month of payment**. No prior return is corrected |

**Social security is the exception that requires a prior-period correction.** Contributions follow the
**accrual** of the salary, because the contribution base is defined by the salary earned in the period.
Where a retrospective increase raises the base of past months, the employer must file a **correction
planilla** (type N) for those months, reporting paired records — an `A` group with the originally settled
values and a `C` group with the corrected values — and pay the additional contributions **with
late-payment interest**. The sum of the contribution bases and of the day counts in the `C` records must be
greater than or equal to those in the `A` records, which is precisely the under-declaration case a
retrospective increase produces. The correction may be filed only through the **same information operator**
that carried the original return.

**Practical sequence for a retrospective salary increase.**

```
1. Compute the arrears of BASIC SALARY for each affected month.
2. Recompute the ORDINARY HOUR VALUE for each affected month and top up
   overtime, the night premium and the rest-day premium.
   -> remember the 2026 divisor split: 220 to 14 July, 210 from 15 July.
3. Recompute the STATUTORY BENEFIT bases and top up:
      service bonus, severance savings, interest, vacation.
4. Pay the whole arrears amount in the CURRENT month.
5. TAX: add the arrears to the current month's taxable payments; run the
   ordinary calculation; declare on the current month's Form 350.
6. SOCIAL SECURITY: file a CORRECTION PLANILLA for each affected month,
   with paired A/C records, and pay the additional contributions with
   late-payment interest.
7. Report the arrears in the current month's electronic payroll document,
   or by adjustment note where a validated document must be corrected.
```

**Two 2026-specific retrospective triggers.** Both arise from mid-year statutory changes and both produce
genuine arrears if not applied on time:

1. The **rest-day premium step from 80% to 90% on 1 July 2026**. An employer that continued at 80% into
   July owes arrears on every rest-day hour worked from 1 July.
2. The **hourly divisor change from 220 to 210 on 15 July 2026**. An employer that continued at 220 owes
   arrears of approximately **4.8%** on every premium hour from 15 July.

### For arrears, how are contributions and deductions computed?

| Item | Computation |
|---|---|
| **Employee health 4%** | On the **corrected contribution base of each affected month**, not on the payment month. File a correction planilla and recover the employee's share from the arrears payment |
| **Employee pension 4%** | Same |
| **Solidarity pension fund** | Same. Note that arrears can **retrospectively trigger** the 4-minimum-wage threshold for a month in which it was not previously triggered, or move the employee into a higher subsistence band |
| **Employer pension 12%, health 8.5%, occupational risk** | On the corrected base of each affected month, with late-payment interest |
| **Payroll levies 4% / 2% / 3%** | On the corrected payroll-levy base of each affected month |
| **The exemption test** | Re-tested on the **corrected** month's earnings. Arrears can retrospectively push a month above 10 minimum wages, **retrospectively losing the exemption** for that month and creating a liability for employer health, the apprenticeship levy and the family welfare levy on the whole of that month's base |
| **Contribution-base ceiling** | Re-tested per affected month at 25 minimum wages. Arrears that would take a past month above the ceiling are capped for that month |
| **The 40% clamp** | Re-tested per affected month |
| **Withholding tax** | On the **payment month**, not the affected months |
| **Voluntary deductions** | Not recomputed retrospectively; applied in the payment month, subject to the minimum-wage floor |
| **Late-payment interest** | Payable on the additional contributions, computed from each original due date |

**The retrospective exemption trap deserves emphasis.** Because the Tax Code art. 114-1 test is *"menos de
diez (10) salarios mínimos"* applied **per employee, per month, on what the employee earns in that month**,
a retrospective increase that takes a past month's earnings from COP 17,000,000 to COP 17,600,000 loses the
exemption for that entire month — creating a retrospective liability of 8.5% + 2% + 3% on the whole
corrected base, roughly **COP 2.4 million** for that single month. An engine that applies arrears only
prospectively will not detect this.

### Deliverable: Provide a worked example of a bonus calculation

**Facts.** Resident employee on a monthly salary of **COP 6,000,000**, Withholding Procedure 1, occupational
risk class I, employer a legal person and income-tax filer. In **October 2026** the employee receives a
**performance bonus of COP 12,000,000**. The bonus is contractual and is **habitual** — paid every year —
so **no valid non-salary pact applies** and it is fully salary under Labour Code art. 127.

**Step 1 — Social security**

| Line | Computation | COP |
|---|---|---|
| Salary | | 6,000,000 |
| Performance bonus (salary — habitual, no non-salary pact) | | 12,000,000 |
| **Contribution base** | 6,000,000 + 12,000,000 | **18,000,000** |
| Floor / ceiling check | Above 1 minimum wage; below 25 minimum wages (43,772,625) | ok |
| 40% clamp | No non-salary payments — not engaged | n/a |
| Employee health 4% | 720,000 | 720,000 |
| Employee pension 4% | 720,000 | 720,000 |
| Solidarity fund — solidarity account 1% | Base ≥ 4 minimum wages (7,003,620) | 180,000 |
| Solidarity fund — subsistence account | Base 18,000,000 < 16 minimum wages (28,014,480) | 0 |
| **Total employee social security** | | **1,620,000** |

**Step 2 — The exemption test**

The employee's earnings this month are **COP 18,000,000**, which is **above 10 minimum wages
(COP 17,509,050)**. The **Tax Code art. 114-1 exemption is therefore LOST for October**, even though it
applies in every other month of the year.

| Concept | Rate | Base | COP | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pension | 12.0% | 18,000,000 | 2,160,000 | Never exonerated |
| **Health** | 8.5% | 18,000,000 | **1,530,000** | **Exemption lost this month** |
| Occupational risk, class I | 0.522% | 18,000,000 | 93,960 → **94,000** | |
| Family compensation fund | 4.0% | 18,000,000 | 720,000 | Never exonerated |
| **Apprenticeship training levy** | 2.0% | 18,000,000 | **360,000** | **Exemption lost** |
| **Family welfare levy** | 3.0% | 18,000,000 | **540,000** | **Exemption lost** |
| **Total employer contributions** | | | **5,404,000** |

*Comparison.* Had the bonus been COP 11,500,000 instead — total earnings COP 17,500,000, just below the
threshold — the employer contributions would have been pension 2,100,000 + occupational risk 91,400 +
family compensation fund 700,000 = **COP 2,891,400**. The extra COP 500,000 of bonus costs the employer an
additional **COP 2,512,600** in contributions. This is the exemption cliff, arriving through a bonus.

**Step 3 — Withholding tax, Procedure 1**

The performance bonus is **not** the statutory service bonus, so it gets **no separate table read**. It
enters the ordinary monthly base.

| Line | Computation | COP |
|---|---|---|
| (A) Total taxable payments | 6,000,000 + 12,000,000 | 18,000,000.00 |
| (B) Non-taxable: pension 4% + solidarity 1% + health 4% | | (1,620,000.00) |
| **(C) Subtotal** | | **16,380,000.00** |
| (D) Art. 387 deductions | None declared | 0.00 |
| (E) 25% exempt income | 25% × 16,380,000 = 4,095,000; monthly 790-unit cap = **3,447,955** → **capped** | (3,447,955.00) |
| (F) 40% cap check | 40% × 16,380,000 = 6,552,000 → 3,447,955 is within | ok |
| — 1,340-unit monthly cap | 5,848,430 → not reached | ok |
| **(G) Taxable base** | 16,380,000 − 3,447,955 | **12,932,045.00** |
| (H) In tax units | 12,932,045 ÷ 52,374 | **246.9173** |
| (I) Band | > 150 and ≤ 360 → marginal **28%** | |
| (I) Withholding, in tax units | (246.9173 − 150) × 28% + 10 = 27.1368 + 10 = **37.1368** | |
| **Withholding** | 37.1368 × 52,374 | **1,945,004.60** |

**Step 4 — Net pay for October 2026**

| Line | COP |
|---|---|
| Salary | 6,000,000.00 |
| Performance bonus | 12,000,000.00 |
| **Total gross earnings** | **18,000,000.00** |
| Less health 4% | (720,000.00) |
| Less pension 4% | (720,000.00) |
| Less solidarity pension fund 1% | (180,000.00) |
| Less withholding tax | (1,945,004.60) |
| **NET PAY** | **14,434,995.40** |

**Step 5 — Statutory benefit accrual on the bonus**

Because the bonus is habitual and therefore salary, it **enters the statutory-benefit base** for October:

| Concept | Rate | Base | COP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Service bonus | 8.3333% | 18,000,000 | 1,500,000.00 |
| Severance savings | 8.3333% | 18,000,000 | 1,500,000.00 |
| Interest on severance savings | 1.0000% | 18,000,000 | 180,000.00 |
| Vacation | 4.1667% | 18,000,000 | 750,000.00 |
| **Total accrual** | **21.8333%** | | **3,930,000.00** |

**Total employer cost of the October bonus month: 18,000,000 + 5,404,000 + 3,930,000 = COP 27,334,000** —
an employer cost factor of **1.5186** against gross, compared with about 1.38 in an ordinary month.

**The counterfactual worth showing.** Had the bonus been made **occasional and gratuitous**, or subject to
a **valid written non-salary pact** under Labour Code art. 128:

| Line | Habitual bonus (salary) | Non-salary pact |
|---|---|---|
| Total remuneration | 18,000,000 | 18,000,000 |
| 40% clamp — permitted non-salary | n/a | 40% × 18,000,000 = 7,200,000 |
| Excess forced back into the contribution base | n/a | 12,000,000 − 7,200,000 = **4,800,000** |
| **Contribution base** | **18,000,000** | **10,800,000** (6,000,000 + 4,800,000) |
| Employee contributions (9%) | 1,620,000 | 972,000 |
| Exemption test — month's earnings | 18,000,000 ≥ 10 minimum wages → **lost** | The test is on what the employee *devenga*, which includes the bonus → also **lost** |
| Statutory-benefit base | 18,000,000 | **6,000,000** — the bonus is out permanently |
| Statutory benefit accrual at 21.8333% | 3,930,000 | 1,310,000 |
| **Withholding tax** | **unchanged in structure** — the bonus is fully taxable either way; only the deductible contributions differ | 1,945,004.60 | **2,126,444.60** (lower contributions → higher taxable base) |

The comparison makes the Colombian structuring rule concrete: a non-salary pact is a **social-security and
statutory-benefit** optimisation, it **does not reduce tax** — indeed it slightly *increases* withholding,
because the lower contributions mean less non-taxable income — and it is **clamped at 40%**.
