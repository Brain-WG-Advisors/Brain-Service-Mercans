# Module 8: Statutory Leave Management

*Purpose: To define the rules for all statutory leave types.*

## 8.1 Leave Entitlements

### For each type of statutory leave, what are the eligibility criteria and accrual methodology?

| Leave type | Entitlement | Eligibility | Accrual | Paid by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Annual vacation** | **15 working days** per year of service | All employees, after one year of service; accrues from day one and is settled pro rata on termination | 1.25 working days per month; monthly cost accrual **4.1667%** | Employer |
| **Weekly rest** | Minimum **24 continuous hours**, paid | All employees | Weekly | Employer |
| **Public holidays** | **18 days** per year, paid | All employees | Calendar | Employer |
| **General-illness incapacity** | Up to **180 days**: 66.67% of salary for the first 90 days, **50%** for the remainder | Certified by the health insurer | Per event | **Employer days 1–2; health insurer from day 3** |
| **Occupational accident or disease incapacity** | **100%** of the contribution base | Certified as occupational | Per event | **Occupational-risk insurer from the day after the event** |
| **Maternity leave** | **18 weeks**, paid at the salary earned when the leave begins | All female employees | Per event | **Health insurer** |
| **Paternity leave** | **2 weeks**, paid | Fathers; the leave expands by one week per percentage point of structural-unemployment reduction, capped at **5 weeks** | Per event | **Health insurer** |
| **Shared parental leave** | The parents may freely distribute the **last 6 weeks** of the mother's leave | Both parents; the mother must take the first **12 weeks** | Per event | Health insurer |
| **Flexible parental leave** | Part-time exchange of the last 6 weeks | By agreement | Per event | Health insurer |
| **Bereavement leave** (*licencia de luto*) | **5 working days**, paid | Death of a spouse, permanent partner, or a relative within the second degree of consanguinity, first of affinity or first civil | Per event | Employer |
| **Voting leave** | Paid time off to vote | All employees | Per event | Employer |
| **Transitory official office** | Paid leave to hold a compulsory transitory public office | All employees | Per event | Employer |
| **Grave domestic calamity** | Paid leave; **now expressly reaching relatives to the third degree of consanguinity, second of affinity or first civil** | All employees | Per event | Employer |
| **Union commissions and colleague's funeral** | Paid leave, on due notice, provided absences do not prejudice the business | All employees | Per event | Employer |
| **Emergency and scheduled specialist medical appointments** | Paid leave, on notice with a prior certificate; **expressly including endometriosis diagnosis and treatment** under Law 2338 of 2023 | All employees | Per event | Employer |
| **School obligations as a guardian** | Paid leave where the school requires the employee's attendance | All employees | Per event | Employer |
| **Judicial, administrative and legal summonses** | Paid leave | All employees | Per event | Employer |
| **Bicycle-commuting rest day** | **One paid rest day per six months' work**, by agreement, where the employee certifies commuting by bicycle | By agreement | Per six months | Employer |
| **Carer's flexible schedule** | Not leave — a right to request flexible hours or remote work | Certified carers of dependants | — | — |
| **Unpaid leave** | By agreement | All employees | — | Unpaid |
| **Military service** | See below | — | — | — |

**Accrual methodology.**

- **Vacation** accrues continuously from the first day of service at 15 working days per 360 days of
  service, i.e. **1.25 working days per month**. The monthly employer cost accrual is 15 ÷ 360 =
  **4.1667%** of the vacation base.
- **Leave with a per-event trigger** (sickness, maternity, paternity, bereavement, calamity) does not
  accrue; it arises on the event. No balance is carried.
- **Public holidays and weekly rest** are calendar entitlements, not accruals.

**Vacation detail — Labour Code arts. 186–192.**

| Rule | Content |
|---|---|
| Art. 186 num. 1 | *"Employees who have rendered their services for one year are entitled to **fifteen (15) consecutive working days** of paid holiday."* **Working days, not calendar days** |
| Art. 186 num. 2 | **15 days per six months** for staff in tuberculosis-control establishments and in X-ray work |
| Art. 187 | The employer must set the period no later than the following year, giving **15 days' notice**; num. 3 requires a **special vacation register** recording the entry date, the start and end of each vacation and the remuneration paid |
| Art. 189 num. 1 (as amended by Law 1429 of 2010 art. 20) | Employer and employee may agree **in writing, at the employee's prior request**, to pay in money **up to half** the vacation |
| Art. 189 num. 3 | The base for the cash compensation is **the last salary earned** |
| Art. 190 | At least **6 continuous working days** per year must actually be taken and are **non-accumulable**; the remainder may be accumulated for up to **2 years**, or **4 years** for technical, specialised, trusted or management staff and for foreign nationals serving away from their family's residence |
| Art. 192 | During vacation the employee receives *"the **ordinary salary** they are earning on the day the holiday begins"*; **only** rest-day work and supplementary overtime hours are excluded. Variable salary → the average of the **immediately preceding year** |

### How are unpaid or partially paid leaves handled?

**Unpaid leave** (*licencia no remunerada*) is not a statutory entitlement. It arises by agreement between
employer and employee, or as a consequence of a lawful contract suspension under Labour Code art. 51
(force majeure, employer-authorised suspension of activities up to 120 days, the employee's compulsory
military service, disciplinary suspension, or a preventive detention).

| Aspect | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Salary** | Not payable for the unpaid days. Deduct `monthly salary × unpaid days ÷ 30` |
| **Transport allowance** | Not payable for the unpaid days |
| **Contribution return** | Reported with the **unpaid-leave change event (SLN)**. The contribution-return rate table shows health moving to **8.5% or 0%** and pension to **12% or 16%** depending on the variant, and the **payroll levies falling to 0%** |
| **Statutory-benefit accrual** | **Suspended.** Labour Code art. 53 provides that during suspension the employee's obligation to work and the employer's obligation to pay are suspended, but the time may be counted for severance-savings and pension purposes at the employer's option and is charged against vacation |
| **Vacation accrual** | Suspended for the period of unpaid leave |
| **Seniority** | Labour Code art. 53: the periods of suspension **may be counted** as service for severance savings and pensions, in which case the employer pays the corresponding contributions |
| **Withholding tax** | The month's taxable payments are lower; withholding follows automatically |

**Partially paid leave** in Colombia means, in substance, **sickness incapacity**:

| Period | Rate | Payer |
|---|---|---|
| General illness, days 1–2 | See the open item below | **Employer** |
| General illness, days 3–90 | **66.67%** (two thirds) of salary | Health insurer |
| General illness, days 91–180 | **50%** of salary | Health insurer |
| Occupational accident or disease, from the day after the event | **100%** of the contribution base | Occupational-risk insurer |

> **Open item — the rate for the employer's first two days.** Decree 2943 of 2013 art. 1, compiled at
> Decree 780 of 2016 art. 3.2.1.10, allocates *"the cash benefits corresponding to the first two (2)
days of incapacity arising from general illness"* to the employer, but does **not** state
> the rate. Labour Code art. 227 sets the *auxilio* at two thirds for the first 90 days, and market
> practice applies **66.67% for days 1–2** on that basis. A minority practice pays 100% for those two days.
> Mercans default: **66.67%**, configurable. Carried in the QA report.

> **Open item — the minimum-wage floor on incapacity payments.** Where 66.67% of the employee's salary
> falls below the minimum wage, market practice and settled administrative doctrine top the payment up to
> one minimum wage. The primary regulatory source for that floor was not retrieved this pass. Mercans
> default: apply the floor, configurable. Carried in the QA report.

### Which wage types are affected by unpaid/partially paid leave?

| Wage type | Unpaid leave | Sickness incapacity | Maternity / paternity | Occupational incapacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic salary | **Reduced** pro rata | Replaced by the incapacity benefit | Replaced by the maternity/paternity benefit | Replaced by the occupational benefit |
| Transport allowance | **Not payable** | Not payable — no travel occurs | Not payable | Not payable |
| Overtime, night and rest-day premiums | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| Commissions | Per the contract | Per the contract | Per the contract | Per the contract |
| **Contribution base** | Reduced; SLN change event, levies to 0% | **Maintained** — contributions continue on the base | **Maintained** | **Maintained** |
| Service bonus accrual | **Suspended** | **Continues** | **Continues** | **Continues** |
| Severance-savings accrual | Suspended, subject to the art. 53 option | **Continues** | **Continues** | **Continues** |
| Interest accrual | Suspended | Continues | Continues | Continues |
| **Vacation accrual** | **Suspended** | **Continues** — incapacity is not a break in service | **Continues** | **Continues** |
| Withholding tax | Follows the reduced base | The benefit is exempt within the statutory minimum (art. 206 nums. 1–2) | Exempt within the statutory minimum | Exempt within the statutory minimum |
| Seniority for indemnity purposes | Art. 53 option | Continues | Continues | Continues |

**The essential contrast:** **unpaid leave suspends accrual; paid or benefit-covered leave does not.**

### Are there any mandatory national military service leaves?

**Yes.** Compulsory military service exists in Colombia for men, under **Law 1861 of 2017** (which replaced
Law 48 of 1993).

| Aspect | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Contract effect** | Compulsory military service is a **cause of suspension** of the employment contract — Labour Code art. 51 num. 4: *"the employee being called up to perform military service"* |
| **Salary** | **Not payable** during the suspension. This is an unpaid statutory suspension, not paid leave |
| **Reinstatement** | Labour Code art. 51 num. 4 obliges the employer to **keep the post open** and to reinstate the employee on discharge. Law 1861 of 2017 art. 42 reinforces the guarantee |
| **Time to present** | The employee must present for reinstatement within **30 days** of discharge |
| **Seniority** | The period counts for seniority purposes on reinstatement |
| **Social security** | Contributions are suspended with the contract; the conscript is covered by the military health system during service |
| **Contribution return** | Reported as a suspension change event (SLN) for the affected periods, or as a termination and rehire where the service is long |
| **Reserve call-up / training** | Short-term reserve obligations are treated as leave for a compulsory transitory public office under Labour Code art. 57 num. 6 lit. b) — **paid** |

**Not a statutory leave in the accrual sense.** Military service does not accrue, does not carry a balance
and does not generate an employer payment. The engine must model it as a **long unpaid suspension with a
reinstatement guarantee**.

### Are any other kinds of leave paid by government authorities?

**Yes — this is a defining feature of Colombian leave.** The employer pays only a narrow set of short paid
absences; the substantial paid leaves are borne by the social-security institutions.

| Leave | Paid by | Rate | Employer's role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **General-illness incapacity, days 1–2** | **Employer** | 66.67% (see the open item) | Pays directly |
| **General-illness incapacity, from day 3** | **Health insurer (EPS)** | 66.67% to day 90; **50%** days 91–180 | Typically **advances** the payment through payroll and recovers from the insurer |
| **Maternity leave — 18 weeks** | **Health insurer** | Salary earned when the leave begins; variable salary → average of the last year | Advances and recovers |
| **Paternity leave — 2 weeks** | **Health insurer** | Full salary | Advances and recovers |
| **Shared and flexible parental leave** | **Health insurer** | Salary of whoever takes the leave | Advances and recovers |
| **Occupational accident or disease incapacity** | **Occupational-risk insurer (ARL)**, from the **day after** the event | **100%** of the contribution base | Advances and recovers |
| **Invalidity and survivor pensions** | Pension administrator | Per the pension rules | None |
| **Family subsidy** (*subsidio familiar*) | **Family compensation fund** | Per the fund's scale | Reports eligible dependants |
| **Bereavement, voting, calamity, medical appointments, school obligations, summonses, bicycle rest day** | **Employer** | Full salary | Pays directly |
| **Vacation** | **Employer** | Ordinary salary | Pays directly |

**The cash-flow mechanic.** Colombian employers customarily **advance the benefit through payroll** and
recover from the health or risk insurer, because the institution pays in arrears and the employee cannot
wait. The engine must therefore support a benefit line that is **paid to the employee but receivable from
the institution**, and must not treat it as an employer cost.

**Documentation for the paternity benefit is exacting.** Labour Code art. 236 par. 2 requires the **civil
birth registration** as the **only** valid supporting document, to be filed with the health insurer within
**30 days** of the birth. The benefit is recognised in proportion to the weeks the father contributed
during gestation.

### What are the payout rules for statutory leaves?

| Leave | Payout rule |
|---|---|
| **Vacation** | Paid at the **ordinary salary being earned on the day the leave begins**. **Excludes** rest-day work and supplementary overtime hours **only**; the night premium and commissions stay in. Variable salary → the average of the **immediately preceding year**. The **transport allowance is not part of the vacation base** — it compensates travel that does not occur during leave |
| **Cash compensation of vacation** | Up to **half** the entitlement, on the employee's **prior written request**, at **the last salary earned** (Labour Code art. 189 nums. 1 and 3) |
| **Public holidays and weekly rest** | One day's ordinary salary, *"but without any deduction being made for absence from work"* (art. 177). Where the agreed schedule does not require work on all working days of the week, the rest day is paid **in proportion to time worked** (art. 173 num. 5) |
| **General-illness incapacity** | 66.67% for days 1–90; 50% for days 91–180. Variable salary → the average of the year of service preceding the incapacity, or of the whole period if under a year (art. 228) |
| **Occupational incapacity** | 100% of the contribution base, from the day after the event |
| **Maternity and paternity** | Salary earned when the leave begins; variable salary → average of the last year |
| **Bereavement, voting, calamity, medical appointments, summonses** | Full ordinary salary; no deduction |

**A structural point on the paid rest day.** Labour Code art. 174 num. 2 provides that *"Every salary is
understood to include payment for rest on the days on which rest is legally mandatory and paid."* For
a monthly-salaried employee the paid rest day is **already inside** the monthly salary and is not a
separate payment. For a daily-rated employee it is a distinct payment. The engine must not double-pay the
monthly-salaried case.

### How are unused paid leaves treated upon termination?

| Leave | Treatment on termination |
|---|---|
| **Vacation** | **Paid out in full, in cash, in proportion to the time worked.** This is unconditional — it applies whatever the reason for termination, including dismissal for cause and employee resignation. The 6-day non-accumulable minimum of art. 190 does **not** bar the payout; it constrains in-service accumulation only |
| **Accumulated vacation from prior years** | Paid out in full |
| **Service bonus** | Paid pro rata for the semester worked to the termination date |
| **Severance savings** | Liquidated and paid to the employee on termination (not deposited to the fund) |
| **Interest on severance savings** | Paid to the employee, proportional to the elapsed part of the year |
| **Sickness incapacity in progress** | Continues to be paid by the health insurer to the extent of the certified incapacity; a dismissal during certified incapacity carries a separate protection-against-dismissal risk |
| **Public holidays, weekly rest, per-event leaves** | No balance; nothing to pay out |
| **Unpaid leave** | No balance |

**Vacation payout formula and worked example.**

```
Vacation payout = base salary (ex overtime and rest-day work)
                  x days of service in the accrual period
                  / 720
```

The 720 divisor derives from 15 working days per 360 days of service, expressed in monthly-salary terms:
15 ÷ 30 = 0.5 months of salary per 360 days, so `salary × days ÷ 720`.

**Example.** Employee on COP 2,400,000 (no overtime, no rest-day work), terminating on **31 August 2026**
with **510 days** of service since the last vacation settlement:

```
2,400,000 × 510 ÷ 720 = COP 1,700,000
```

**Tax on the payout.** Vacation compensation is ordinary taxable employment income within the monthly
withholding base. It also **enters the social-security contribution base and the payroll-levy base**,
because vacation is paid rest, not a statutory benefit — a point frequently missed on termination
settlements.

### Provide the calculation logic for each leave type where applicable.

Let `S` = monthly salary, `T` = transport allowance (COP 249,095 where due), `d` = days.

| Leave | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Vacation accrual** | `S_vacation_base × d ÷ 720`, where the base excludes overtime and rest-day work |
| **Vacation taken** | `S_vacation_base × working days of leave ÷ 30` |
| **Vacation cash compensation** | `last salary × days compensated ÷ 720`, maximum half the entitlement |
| **Vacation payout on termination** | `S_vacation_base × days of service ÷ 720` |
| **Sickness, days 1–2 (employer)** | `S ÷ 30 × 0.6667 × d`, floored at the minimum wage equivalent |
| **Sickness, days 3–90 (health insurer)** | `contribution base ÷ 30 × 0.6667 × d`, floored at the minimum wage equivalent |
| **Sickness, days 91–180 (health insurer)** | `contribution base ÷ 30 × 0.50 × d`, floored at the minimum wage equivalent |
| **Occupational incapacity** | `contribution base ÷ 30 × 1.00 × d`, from the day after the event |
| **Maternity, 18 weeks = 126 calendar days** | `S ÷ 30 × 126` |
| **Paternity, 2 weeks = 14 calendar days** | `S ÷ 30 × 14` |
| **Bereavement, 5 working days** | `S ÷ 30 × 5` |
| **Public holiday** | Already inside a monthly salary (art. 174 num. 2); `S ÷ 30 × 1` for a daily-rated employee |
| **Unpaid leave deduction** | `−(S + T) ÷ 30 × d` |

**Worked example — a mixed month. September 2026.** Employee on COP 2,400,000 plus the transport
allowance, who takes **5 working days of vacation**, is certified sick for **4 days** (days 1–2 employer,
days 3–4 health insurer), and works the remaining days.

| Line | Computation | COP |
|---|---|---|
| Salary for days worked (30 − 5 vacation − 4 sick = 21 days) | 2,400,000 × 21 ÷ 30 | 1,680,000.00 |
| Vacation pay, 5 working days | 2,400,000 × 5 ÷ 30 | 400,000.00 |
| Sickness, days 1–2, employer, 66.67% | 2,400,000 ÷ 30 × 0.6667 × 2 | 106,672.00 |
| Sickness, days 3–4, health insurer, 66.67% (advanced by the employer, recoverable) | 2,400,000 ÷ 30 × 0.6667 × 2 | 106,672.00 |
| Transport allowance, days actually worked and on vacation — **not** for sick days | 249,095 × 26 ÷ 30 | 215,882.33 |
| **Total gross earnings** | | **2,509,226.33** |
| **Contribution base** | Salary 1,680,000 + vacation 400,000 + sickness 213,344 (contributions continue during incapacity); transport allowance excluded | **2,293,344** |
| Employee health 4% | | 91,800 (up to the nearest 100) |
| Employee pension 4% | | 91,800 |
| Solidarity fund | Base < 4 minimum wages | 0 |
| **Net pay** | 2,509,226.33 − 183,600 | **2,325,626.33** |

Note the two behaviours the example demonstrates: **vacation pay is in the contribution base**, and
**contributions continue during incapacity**, while the transport allowance is excluded from the base and
is not paid for sick days.

### How does extended unpaid leave impact social security contributions, tax status, and accrual?

| Dimension | Effect of extended unpaid leave |
|---|---|
| **Employment relationship** | **Suspended**, not terminated. Labour Code art. 53: the employee's obligation to work and the employer's obligation to pay are suspended |
| **Health contribution** | Reported with the unpaid-leave change event (SLN). The contribution-return rate table shows the health rate moving to **8.5% or 0%** depending on the variant. In the 8.5% variant, the **employer bears the whole health contribution alone** to preserve the employee's health cover |
| **Pension contribution** | Rate of **12% or 16%** depending on the variant; a public-law employer may contribute 75% of the total rate |
| **Occupational risk** | Not payable — there is no exposure while the contract is suspended |
| **Payroll levies** | Fall to **0%** for the family compensation fund, the apprenticeship training levy and the family welfare levy |
| **Health cover for the employee** | Preserved where the employer maintains the health contribution. **Extended unpaid leave without contribution risks loss of health cover**, and the employer bears the consequence where the suspension was employer-initiated |
| **Tax status** | No change to residence or to the withholding procedure. Taxable payments are simply lower or nil for the period. Under **Procedure 2** the fixed percentage is unchanged, so a long unpaid absence produces mechanical over-withholding on resumption until the next semi-annual recalculation |
| **Vacation accrual** | **Suspended** |
| **Service-bonus accrual** | **Suspended** — art. 306 accrues on time worked |
| **Severance-savings accrual** | Suspended, **unless** the employer elects under art. 53 to count the period, in which case it pays the corresponding contributions |
| **Seniority for the dismissal indemnity** | Follows the same art. 53 election |
| **Return to work** | The employee resumes on the same terms. Where the suspension exceeded 120 days for an employer-authorised suspension of activities, the limit of art. 51 num. 3 has been breached and the suspension is unlawful |
| **Contribution-return mechanics** | The SLN change event carries the affected day count; the base is reduced pro rata for partial-month suspensions |

**Configuration rule.** Model extended unpaid leave as a **suspension with an accrual switch**, not as a
zero-pay month. The engine must (a) stop the four benefit accruals, (b) report the SLN change event with
the correct day count, (c) apply the SLN rate variant the client has elected, and (d) hold the art. 53
seniority election as a per-case flag.
