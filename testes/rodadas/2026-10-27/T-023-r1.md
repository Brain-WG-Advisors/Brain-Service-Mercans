## Resposta

**Default values for the GB Data Dictionary**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Employer contribution | **3%** of qualifying earnings |
| Employee contribution | **5%** of qualifying earnings, gross. This is 4% deducted plus 1% tax relief if the scheme uses relief at source. |
| Total minimum | **8%** |
| Qualifying earnings band (2026/27) | £6,240 – £50,270 per year |
| Earnings trigger (2026/27) | £10,000 per year |

**Statutory basis**

- **Rates:** Pensions Act 2008, **s.20(1)(b)–(c)**. The employer's contribution, however calculated, must be equal to or more than 3% of the amount of the jobholder's qualifying earnings in the relevant pay reference period, and the total amount of contributions paid by the jobholder and the employer, however calculated, must be equal to or more than 8% of those earnings.
- **Thresholds:** In the DWP *Review of the AE Earnings Trigger and Qualifying Earnings Band for 2026/27*, Table 1, the Secretary of State decided to keep the 2025/26 threshold values for the 2026/27 tax year. These are £10,000 trigger, £6,240 lower limit, £50,270 upper limit. TPR's "Earnings thresholds" page gives the per-pay-period figures, for example £520 / £833 monthly for the lower limit and the trigger, and £4,189 monthly for the upper limit.

**Things to note in the DD**

- **Tax relief method.** The 5% employee rate is a gross figure. Under a **net pay arrangement**, the full 5% is deducted before tax. Under **relief at source**, the payroll deducts 4% net, with 1% tax relief claimed from the government by the scheme provider.
- **Employee rate depends on the employer rate.** 8% is the total floor. If the employer pays more than 3%, the employee rate can go down by the same amount. If your employer chose to pay the full 8%, then you would not have to pay anything.
- **Extent.** The Pensions Act 2008 applies to England, Wales and Scotland. Northern Ireland has its own parallel Act, the Pensions (No. 2) Act (NI) 2008, s.20, which sets the same 3% and 8% rates.
