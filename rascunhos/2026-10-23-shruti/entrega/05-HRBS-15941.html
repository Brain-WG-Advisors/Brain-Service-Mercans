Hi @anaik,

For an employer-paid childcare subsidy paid once a year through payroll:

1. Tax: taxable. Salary income includes "各種補助費" (all kinds of subsidies) (Income Tax Act art. 14(1), Category 3), and no art. 4 exemption applies; the exemption for childcare subsidies covers only those paid by the government (MOF ruling 台財稅字第09700315650號). As non-monthly salary, 5% is withheld when the payment reaches NT$90,501 (2026 threshold); below that there is no withholding (Withholding Regulations for Salary Income arts. 6–7; MOF tax portal Q&A 1506). Either way it is reported in the annual withholding / non-withholding statement (Income Tax Act arts. 89(3) and 92).

2. Statutory insurance: LI, EI, Labour Pension and the NHI insured amount follow "wages" under Labor Standards Act art. 2(3) (LI Act art. 14 and Enforcement Rules art. 27; EI Act art. 40; Labor Pension Act art. 3; NHI Enforcement Rules art. 46). A discretionary, non-recurring welfare payment is not counted (LSA Enforcement Rules art. 10; CLA Order 勞保2字第0950114071號), so it does not change the insured salary bracket. If it becomes a fixed or contractual entitlement, it becomes wages.

3. Once-a-year payment: no NHI employee supplementary premium, as subsidies are not incentive bonuses (Supplementary Premium Regulations art. 3(1)(1); NHIA guide v115.1). The employer supplementary premium (2.11%) applies in the month paid (NHI Act art. 34).

4. Payroll: processing it through payroll as a separate one-off earning gives the correct withholding and annual reporting. The "refund application" is the employer's own claim for the Ministry of Labor childcare subsidy (育兒補貼, up to NT$10,000 per employee per year), filed at childcare.mol.gov.tw.

Regards,
