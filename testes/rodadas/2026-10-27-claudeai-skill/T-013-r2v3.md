## Resposta

**Short answer:** under current DIAN doctrine, only the ARL (occupational) incapacity is exempt under ET art. 206. Common-illness incapacity and maternity, paternity and parental leave pay are taxable labour income, subject to retención en la fuente, with only the general 25% renta exenta (num. 10) available.

**1. Common-illness incapacity (employer days 1–2, EPS from day 3): taxable**

DIAN held in Oficio 023438 of 2015 that the monetary auxilio for non-occupational illness is not expressly covered by art. 206 num. 1 and is therefore taxed. In its reading, num. 1 covers only indemnities for work accidents and the economic benefits for incapacity caused by occupational illness or accident. It doesn't matter who pays. Day 3 onward is an EPS benefit, but the employer usually advances it and recovers it, so the employer withholds as on any labour payment. The 25% num. 10 exemption applies, inside the 40% / 1.340 UVT combined limit.

One caveat: older doctrine (Concepto 006728 of 1998, cited in Oficio 000321 of 2015) treated general-illness incapacities recognised by the EPS as not subject to withholding. The 2015 line has since narrowed that, and taxing them is the current position.

**2. ARL / occupational incapacity: exempt, capped at the statutory minimum**

Oficio 036846 of 2015 links num. 1 with Decreto-Ley 1295 de 1994 art. 94 lit. a. That provision makes exempt the sums the ARL pays to cover the economic and health benefits of the occupational-risk system. DIAN then answered the specific question directly: the Ley 776 de 2002 art. 3 temporary-incapacity subsidy is to be considered in setting the exempt amount under num. 1. That subsidy is 100% of the IBC from the day after the accident.

Three points to apply in the engine:
- **Cap:** par. 1 limits the exemption to the "mínimo legal". DIAN reads this as the amount set by the labour rules. Anything paid above it, such as an employer top-up over the ARL subsidy, is taxed and subject to withholding under arts. 383 and 384.
- **Combined limit:** the exempt amount still counts toward the 40% / 1.340 UVT limit.
- **Ordering:** it is "other exempt income", so it comes out before the 25% is computed.

Commentators disagree. Gerencie, for example, argues ARL incapacities don't qualify because they are not an indemnity. DIAN's official doctrine is what binds an audit, though, so exempt is the defensible position. Tell clients it is contested.

**3. Maternity, paternity and shared parental leave: taxable**

Oficio 027408 of 2019, reiterated in Oficio 901191 of 2020, holds that num. 2 covers indemnities protecting maternity, not income from maternity and paternity leave, so these are not exempt. The consequence is that withholding must be applied to these payments. This follows from art. 369 num. 2, which only excludes from withholding payments that are exempt by special provision.

Num. 2 is generally read as covering the CST art. 239 indemnity for dismissing a protected worker without authorisation, which equals 60 days' salary. Licencia parental compartida is the mother's leave redistributed, so it follows the same taxable treatment. The 25% num. 10 exemption applies.
