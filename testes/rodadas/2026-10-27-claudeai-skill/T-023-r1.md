## Resposta

Set the defaults to **5% employee and 3% employer**, with the contribution basis set to **qualifying earnings**. That is the statutory minimum for automatic enrolment: **8% in total, of which the employer must pay at least 3%**. These rates have been unchanged since April 2019 and still apply in 2026-27 under the Pensions Act 2008 (per the GB pack's `01-ccg.md` §8.2 and `05-data-dictionary.md` §6.2).

Several points to build into the Data Dictionary alongside the two percentages:

1. **They are floors, not fixed values.** The field format is "% or GBP," and schemes often contribute more. The employer can pay more than 3% and the employee correspondingly less, as long as the employer pays at least 3% and the total reaches 8%. Make both fields overridable per scheme and per employee, and validate against the 3% employer floor and the 8% combined floor rather than against 5% for the employee alone.

2. **Pair the rates with the basis.** On the default qualifying-earnings basis, contributions are charged only on the band between **£6,240 and £50,270 a year**. The pro-rated monthly figures are £520.00 and £4,189.17. Both thresholds are unchanged for 2026-27 (DWP DEP2025-0866, 18 Dec 2025). The DD's contribution-basis enum also allows certified set 1, set 2 and set 3, and pensionable pay. Those bases have their own minimums, which the pack doesn't detail, so don't apply 5%/3% to them automatically.

3. **The tax relief method changes what payroll actually deducts.** The 5% employee figure includes tax relief.
   - Under **net pay**, payroll deducts the full 5% from gross pay. This reduces the PAYE base only.
   - Under **relief at source**, payroll deducts from net pay, and the scheme reclaims basic-rate relief from HMRC. In practice that means 4% deducted and 1% added by HMRC, so the 5% total is still met. The pack confirms the mechanism but not the 4%/1% split, which comes from general TPR guidance and should be confirmed with TPR.
   - Under **salary sacrifice**, the whole amount becomes an employer contribution.

4. **Related triggers that drive the default.** The rates apply to eligible jobholders: aged 22 to State Pension age and earning above the **£10,000** trigger. Workers who opt in must receive the employer contribution once they earn more than **£520 a month, £120 a week, or £480 over 4 weeks**.

The 2026-27 QA report has no open items on these values. The only forward change it flags is the **£2,000 a year cap on NIC relief for salary-sacrificed contributions from April 2029**, which doesn't affect the default percentages.
