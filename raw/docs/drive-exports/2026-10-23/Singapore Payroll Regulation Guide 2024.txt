Country Overview
Tax Year
The Fiscal year runs from January to December.
Residency        
The person is considered tax resident in Singapore if either of below is applicable:
1. Singapore Citizen or Singapore Permanent Resident (SPR); or
2. Foreigner who has stayed/worked in Singapore:
1. for at least 183 days in the previous calendar year; or
2. continuously for 3 consecutive years; or
3. for a continuous period straddling two calendar years (e.g. 03 Nov 2021 to 07 May 2022) and total period of stay is at least 183 days. *
*This does not apply to foreigners who are directors of a company, public entertainers or professionals. 
Employment types for foreigners
1. Work Permit (these are required for semi-skilled worker for jobs in construction, manufacturing, or the services sector)
2. S-Pass (mid-skilled employees)
3. Employment Pass (there are different types of employment passes such as long-term visit pass, dependent Pass, personalized employment pass etc.)
4. Passport (applicable for non-resident director only)

Personal Income Tax
Taxable income
The employer is not required to withhold taxes from employees. The employer prepares employment income records for submission to IRAS.
Benefits-in-kind
Employers are required to report taxable benefits in appendix 8A which are as follows.
* Accommodation rented by the employer – is calculated as per the annual (rental) value of the property including rent for furniture, less the rent paid by the employee.  
* Accommodation owned by the employer – is calculated as per AV of the property. For fully furnished 50% of AV is added, for partially furnished 40% of AV is added. 
If the accommodation is shared by no. of employees, or it is provided for no. of days in a year. Then prorate the cost. 
Example: = rent paid by employer is SGD 800 per month, the house is shared by employee A & B. It is given for 30 days to employee A.  
Value of benefit for employee A = (((800*12)/365)/2) * 30 => 394.52
* Utilities and housekeeping – are calculated as the amount incurred by the employer. 
* Driver benefit is valued at “Annual cost of driver x (private mileage / total mileage)”
* Hotel Accommodation – is calculated as the amount incurred by the employer, less the amount paid by the employee. 
* Home leave Passage or incidental benefits – is calculated as the full amount of leave passage provided by the employer.
* Free or subsidized loan to key person – is calculated as the amount of the interest borne by the employer. If it is available to all employees, then it is not taxed.
* Insurance premium – is calculated as the amount of premium borne by the employer.
* Education/Child education/care – is calculated as the amount borne by the employer.
* New Car – is calculated as “3/7 x [(car cost - PARF rebate)/10 + Maintenance cost]”
* Used car – is calculated as “3/7 x [(car cost - PARF rebate)/10 + Maintenance cost]”
* Leased car – is calculated as “3/7 x [car cost + Maintenance cost]”
* Renewed COE car – is calculated as “3/7 x [(car cost + PARF rebate)/10 + Maintenance cost]”
If the car benefit is provided for no. of days in a year, then we can prorate the cost. 
* Entrance/transfer fee and annual subscription to social or recreational clubs are valued at its cost. It is not taxed if the employer has corporate membership & the benefit is extended to all employees.
* Staff discount on assets such as vehicles, property, laptop, handphone etc. are taxed if the gain is above SGD 500.
* Non-monetary awards such as examination pass award, SPOT award, GEMS award, long service awards, retirement awards, most courteous employee etc. are taxed if the value is above SGD 200.
* Other benefits such as Christmas gift, Chinese New Year gift, Deepawali gift, birthday gift, wedding gift, parenthood gift, SG50 gift etc. are taxed if the value is above SGD 200.
* Employer SRS Contributions
The benefits which have administrative concessions are not taxed. Refer below link.

Social Security 
Central Provident Fund (CPF)
https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer
CPF is a key pillar of Singapore's social security system. It has four accounts. 
* Ordinary account (for retirement, housing, insurance, and investment)
* Medisave account (for hospitalization expenses and approved medical insurance)
* Special account (for old age & investment in retirement related financial products)
* Retirement account (at the age of 55 or above, CPF creates retirement account for monthly retirement payouts)
Who is subject to CPF?
As an employer, you’re required to pay CPF contributions for employees who are Singapore Citizens (SC) or Singapore Permanent Residents (SPR), and who are earning total wages of more than $50 per month. 
Who is not subject to CPF?
* Students
* Domestic workers (employed for 14 hours or less in a week, such as gardener, cook etc.)
* Foreigners
* Employees of the United Nations (UN) Organization, or any agency or institution of the United Nations Organization stationed in Singapore.
* Seamen who are Singapore Citizens employed in Swedish, Norwegian and Danish ships, and employed on specific terms and conditions.
https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/faq/employer-obligations/who-should-receive-cpf-contributions/who-is-exempted-from-receiving-cpf
Income subject to CPF
CPF contributions are computed on wages which are remuneration in money, including any bonus that is due or granted to a person in respect of his employment. The incomes are classified as ordinary wages, additional wages & total wages. 
https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/faq/employer-obligations/what-payments-attract-cpf-contributions/are-cpf-payable-on-supplementary-retirement-scheme-allowance


Points to be noted:
1. Employers’ contribution towards the SRS is subject to CPF.
2. Reimbursement for holiday expenses incurred by the employee and his dependents is subject to CPF.
3. NS Pay Treatment: 
There are two set-ups done by the employer.
1. The employer will pay it to the employee & claim from MOM. No special treatment for CPF purposes. NS pay is included as other regular wages in the CPF base.
2. The employer deducts the NS claim from the employee's salary. Employees will directly claim from MOM. In this case, one non-payable but subject to CPF wage type is to be added to CPF Ordinary wage base.
Income not subject to CPF
* Termination benefits such as retirement gratuity, retrenchment pay, ex-gratia payment not related to employment, compensation in lieu of notice, severance pay, compensation for loss of employment etc. Temporary lay-off benefits such as redundancy payment are not covered under the exemption. https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/faq/employer-obligations/what-payments-attract-cpf-contributions/are-cpf-payable-on-gratuity-and-ex-gratia-payments
* Reimbursements of expenses incurred on behalf of the employer.
* Reimbursement of dental treatment (employee, spouse & child)
* Reimbursement of medical treatment (employee, spouse & child)
* Reimbursement for handphone and pager expenses incurred for official purposes.
* Reimbursement for laundry expenses to uniformed employees.
* Reimbursement for course and examination fees as part of employee’s training program
* Bursary or scholarship awards  
* Benefits in kind (e.g., rent payment directly to third party, payment for handphone directly to third party, shares, non-cash gift vouchers)
* Finders’ introduction/ referral fees
* Awards and incentives in kind (e.g., token gifts)
* Maternity subsidies which are directly paid to service providers.
* Meal reimbursement for staying beyond working hours or on non-working days.
* Cash prizes (not related to work i.e., dinner & dance, early bird draws, lucky draws etc.) 
* Gifts in kind (e.g., marriage or childbirth) (Ang bao / red packet are not covered as these are money)
* Restrictive covenant payment given after the employee has left.
* Death benefits 
* Donation on bereavements.
* Program allowance given to employees for attending training after working hours.
* Employment assistance payment (EAP) given to employee if employer is unable to offer re-employment when your employee reaches retirement age (62-67)
* Employment grant provided by govt.
* Director Fee

CPF contributions are not payable on LSA that is paid with at least 5 years of service and subsequent LSA for each 5 years of service (i.e. 5, 10, 15 and so on), up to the ordinary wages (OW) of the month in which LSA is paid. If there is no OW in that month, full LSA is subjected to CPF. If there are multiple LSA given to employees, only the first LSA granted in each 5-year period can qualify for exemption. CPF on additional wages (AW) paid after termination. 
For AW payable on or after 1 January 2024, The CPF shall be payable on the AW if the employee is a SC/ SPR on both the last date of employment and the date of payment of AW. 
Example: An employee’s last date of employment is 30th June 2023. AW was payable on 1st January 2024.
* If the employee is a SC/SPR on both 30th June 2023 and 1st January 2024, CPF contributions are payable for the month & year in which AW is paid.
* If the employee is not a SC/SPR on either 30th June 2023 or 1st January 2024 or on both dates, CPF contributions are not payable on AW.

Classification into Ordinary & Additional Wages
Wages which are not classified as OW will be AW for the month. For wages to be classified as Ordinary Wages (OW) for the month, it must satisfy both conditions below: 
* The wages are due or granted wholly or exclusively in respect of an employee’s employment during that month; and 
* The wages for that month are payable by the 14th of the following month. 


Ordinary wages have a ceiling at SGD 6,800 per month per employer. Additional wages ceiling is to be computed for each employee based upon total wage ceiling at SGD 102,000. Ordinary wages ceiling is set at SGD 7,400 for 2025 and SGD 8,000 from 2026 onwards. The total wage ceiling is 17 times OW 6000, which comes as 102,000.

	Calculation Logic
1. Determine the citizenship status of an employee. If it is SC/SPR, then CPF is to be computed, else not. Find SPR year in case of SPR.
2. Determine the age of the employee.
3. Determine the ordinary wages, additional wages for the month which are to be considered for CPF. 
Determine ordinary wages subject to CPF: If ordinary wages are above 6800, consider 6800 as ordinary wages to be subject to CPF.
4. Determine additional wages subject to CPF as follows:
   1. In other than termination or December, determine additional wage ceiling for the current month as follows:
      1. Estimated annual AW ceiling = [(Maximum total wage subject to CPF i.e., 102000) – (YTD of ordinary wage subject to CPF + Current month OW subject to CPF + Estimated OW for remaining month in the year as per current month OW subject to CPF)] 
      2. Take the YTD of AW subject to CPF.  
      3. Find the difference of annual estimated AW ceiling & AW already subject to CPF in the year as computed in “ii” above.
      4. The AW ceiling for the month would be “0” if the difference in “iii” is negative.
      5. Compare actual AW for the month with AW ceiling for month. The lower of the two values is considered as AW subject to CPF for the current month.
      6. If the actual AW is more than AW subject to CPF in the current month, store the difference of actual AW & AW subject to CPF as AW not subject to CPF. 
   2. In termination month or in December month after computing the AW for the current month. Reconcile AW as follows:
      1. Annual AW ceiling = [(Maximum total wage subject to CPF i.e., 102000) – (YTD of ordinary wage subject to CPF + Current month OW subject to CPF)]
      2. Take the YTD of AW and Current month AW.
      3. Take the YTD of AW subject to CPF and current month AW subject to CPF.
      4. If (iii) is lower than (i), then additional wage ceiling balance for reconciliation would be difference of (i) & (iii),
      5. Minimum of AW not subject to CPF in past months & (iv) is the AW subject to CPF for previous months in last month.
      6. If (iii) is greater than (i). Then compute the excess AW subject to CPF.
      7. If (vii) is true, then identify the month in which YTD of AW subject to CPF has crossed (i). We need these values for the IR8S report.
      8. Find the difference of YTD value of month identified in (viii) & (i). This is the excess wage value. We need these values for the IR8S report.
5. Find the percentage from the rate table for total, employee & employer as per total wage subject to CPF [(point 3) + (point 4)], age, SPR Year of the employee.
6. Total contribution: Apply the percentage on total percentage on total wage subject to CPF. Round to nearest SGD.  
7. Employee contribution: Apply the employee percentage on total wage subject to CPF. Drop the cents.
8. Employer contribution: Subtract employee contribution after rounding from total contribution.
9. Compute employee contribution & employer contribution on OW subject to CPF & AW subject to CPF separately also as per respective percentage for report purposes. 
10. Compute employee & employer CPF on excess/short additional wages as per respective month percentage.

The total contributions for the calendar month should be rounded off to the nearest dollar. An amount of 50 cents should be regarded as an additional dollar. The cents should be dropped for the employee's share of CPF contributions.

Rounding Logic: Add up the SDL calculated for each employee and then round the total amount down to the nearest dollar. It is remitted along with CPF.
https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/faq/employer-obligations/skills-development-levy/how-do-i-calculate-skills-development-levy

Example1: 
Ordinary wages are SGD 7,500 & additional wages are SGD 1,000.
Gross wages are more than SGD 4,500, hence SDL = 4500 * 0.25% = SGD 11.25


Example2:
Ordinary wages are SGD 700 & additional wages are SGD 50.
Gross wages are lower than SGD 800, hence SDL = 800 * 0.25% = SGD 2.00


Foreign Worker Levy (FWL)
The FWL is a monthly levy that employers are liable to pay for each foreign employee (Work Permit or S Pass holders) hired. The levy rate depends on the employer’s industry and the quota (foreigners to SC/SPR ratio). It is computed by MOM & directly debited. Calculation & reporting in payroll is not required.
https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-permit-for-foreign-worker/foreign-worker-levy/paying-the-levy
However, an internal report can be prepared to evaluate the FWL. Rates & logic of calculation are available in the link below.
https://www.mom.gov.sg/-/media/mom/documents/services-forms/passes/guide_on_comp_of_company_quota_balance.pdf

It is not payable if CPF is not payable for Students/Intern.

Additional Medisave Contributions (AMCS) – 
For SC/SPR, additional contributions to the Medisave account can be made of any amount up to SGD 2,730 per employee per year. The employer can have different plans such as 
* minimum 1% contribution of monthly wages (minimum contribution amount as SGD 16 for OW & AW respectively) However, the maximum contribution is restricted to SGD 2,730 per employee per year.
https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/schemes-for-employers-and-employees/portable-medical-benefits
* minimum 2% contribution of monthly wages (minimum contribution amount as SGD 50 for OW & AW respectively), the maximum OW are SGD 7,000, maximum AW are SGD 35,000. It will result in a cumulative monthly contribution of SGD 2,380. And if an employee is available till December, SGD 350 is credited to the Medisave account.

For SC/SPR, employer can do Voluntary CPF contributions can be done, of any amount up to CPF annual limit (SGD 37,740 i.e.,102,000 * 37%) less mandatory CPF contributions. Any excess contribution over SGD 37,740 shall be refunded by CPFB without interest.  


Another scenario is where VCPF is made by employer:
for the employees seconded to overseas, Employers may do VCPF for them, as they do not receive mandatory CPF contributions.

CPF Submission number (CSN) for Mandatory & Voluntary Contributions 
Employers must apply for a separate CSN for mandatory contributions & voluntary contributions. i.e., one CSN for mandatory CPF, SDL, FWL, SHG contributions & another CSN for VCPF, AMCS, SRS. 

Multiple CSN for mandatory CPF will be considered later as part of CR.
