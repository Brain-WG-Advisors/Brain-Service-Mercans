## Resposta

Under **GVK Art. 63(1)(3)**, the deduction is limited in three layers. The first layer applies to the premium itself, and the other two are caps on the total.

**1. Eligible portion of the premium (applied first, per policy).** For policies with an insurance or pension company resident and headquartered in Türkiye, the deductible base is 50% of premiums the employee pays for savings-type life insurance (birikim primli hayat sigortası) covering themselves, their spouse and minor children, and 100% of premiums for personal insurance policies such as death, accident, health, sickness, disability, unemployment, maternity, birth and education. "Minor child" means a child under 18, or under 25 if in education.

**2. Monthly cap: 15% of the wage. This cap is not cumulative.** The total deductible premiums may not exceed 15% of the wage earned in the month the premium is paid. The wage used for this test is the gross total of recurring taxable payments made for the employee's service, such as salary, premiums, bonuses, social benefits and raises. The cap is tested month by month. Headroom you don't use in one month does not carry into later months, and any excess over the cap in a month is lost.

**3. Annual cap: the gross annual minimum wage. This cap is cumulative.** The total deducted during the year may not exceed the annual gross minimum wage, which for calendar year 2025 is TRY 312,066. Any change to the minimum wage during the year is taken into account. In practice, payroll tracks the year-to-date total of premiums deducted. Each month's deduction is the lower of the 15% amount and the remaining annual headroom.

**Shared pool with employer BES contributions.** Employer contributions to the private pension system (BES) under GVK Art. 40(1)(9) use the same two caps. The combined total of employer BES contributions and Art. 63(1)(3) premiums may not exceed 15% of the monthly wage or the annual minimum wage (TRY 312,066 for 2025). If the combined amount exceeds the limit, the parties may decide whether the deduction goes first to the wage tax base or to commercial income, as long as nothing is deducted twice. BES contributions paid by the employee or the employer can never be deducted from the taxable wage base.

**Timing rules that affect the caps:**
- To be deducted from wages, a premium must relate to the month of the wage and be paid by the end of that month.
- Premiums paid in advance for later months cannot be deducted in the month of payment, even if they would fit within the 15% limit.
- If a premium is paid as a lump sum, it is divided by the number of months it covers that remain, counting from the month the receipt is given to the employer. Each monthly portion is then deducted subject to the monthly cap and the annual minimum-wage cap.
- Any amount that cannot be deducted cannot be carried forward to the next year.
- Premiums deducted through payroll during the year cannot be deducted again on the employee's annual income tax return.

**Employer-paid premiums.** These are a benefit to the employee. They must be grossed up and included in the monthly payroll as wage, and they are then deductible under the same 15% and annual minimum-wage limits.

Source: GİB, *Gelir Vergisi Matrahından İndirilecek Hayat/Şahıs Sigorta Primleri* (Yayın No. 597, February 2026), sections 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 and 11, which apply GVK Art. 63(1)(3) and Art. 40(1)(9). The worked example in section 7 (Örnek 2) shows the monthly cap applied first and then the year-to-date check against TRY 312,066.
