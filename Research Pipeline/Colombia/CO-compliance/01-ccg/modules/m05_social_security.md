# Module 5: Social Security & Pension Systems

*Purpose: To detail the rules for all mandatory social security and pension schemes.*

> **Wage-caliber declaration (control B2).** Every social-security floor, ceiling and trigger in Colombia
> derives from **one single published figure — the statutory minimum monthly wage (SMMLV)** — and from no
> average-wage series of any kind. Colombia's statistics office publishes average and median wage series,
> and the minimum-wage decree itself cites a living-wage study, consumer-price inflation, total-factor
> productivity, labour productivity per hour and per employed person, and gross-domestic-product growth —
> but **none of these enters any payroll parameter**. They are inputs to the annual political negotiation
> that fixes the minimum wage, not calibers in their own right. There is therefore **no possibility of
> mixing calibers in Colombian payroll**, and the reconciliation is trivial and exact:
>
> | Parameter | Derivation | 2026 value (COP) |
> |---|---|---|
> | Contribution-base floor | 1 × 1,750,905 | 1,750,905 |
> | Solidarity-fund trigger | 4 × 1,750,905 | 7,003,620 |
> | Payroll-levy exemption cut-off | 10 × 1,750,905 | 17,509,050 |
> | All-inclusive salary floor | 13 × 1,750,905 | 22,761,765 |
> | Subsistence sub-account first band | 16 × 1,750,905 | 28,014,480 |
> | Subsistence sub-account top band | 20 × 1,750,905 | 35,018,100 |
> | **Contribution-base ceiling** | **25 × 1,750,905** | **43,772,625** |
>
> The **only other calibration unit in Colombian payroll is the tax unit (UVT), COP 52,374 for 2026**, and
> it indexes **tax parameters exclusively** — never a social-security parameter. The two units are never
> mixed. The statutory transport allowance of COP 249,095 is a fixed peso amount, not a caliber, and
> indexes nothing.

## 5.1 Contribution Types and Rates

### List all mandatory social security and pension contributions for both employer and employee.

Colombia's integrated social-protection system has **three insurance branches** and **three payroll
levies**, plus one employee-only solidarity surcharge. All seven are collected through a single monthly
integrated contribution return.

| # | Contribution | Employee | Employer | Total | Base | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Health** | **4.0%** | **8.5%** | 12.5% | Contribution base | 25 minimum wages |
| 2 | **Pension** | **4.0%** | **12.0%** | 16.0% | Contribution base | 25 minimum wages |
| 3 | **Solidarity pension fund — solidarity account** | **1.0%** | — | 1.0% | Contribution base, if ≥ 4 minimum wages | 25 minimum wages |
| 4 | **Solidarity pension fund — subsistence account** | **0.2%–1.0%** | — | 0.2%–1.0% | Contribution base, if ≥ 16 minimum wages | 25 minimum wages |
| 5 | **Occupational risk** | — | **0.348%–8.7%** | Same | Contribution base | 25 minimum wages |
| 6 | **Family compensation fund** | — | **4.0%** | 4.0% | Payroll-levy base | **None** |
| 7 | **Apprenticeship training levy (SENA)** | — | **2.0%** | 2.0% | Payroll-levy base | **None** |
| 8 | **Family welfare levy (ICBF)** | — | **3.0%** | 3.0% | Payroll-levy base | **None** |

**Total standard employee burden:** 8.0%, rising to 10.0% at the top of the solidarity-fund scale.
**Total standard employer burden:** 12% + 8.5% + occupational risk + 9% levies, before the exemption.

**Three of the eight are exonerated for lower-paid employees.** Tax Code art. 114-1 exonerates **employer
health 8.5%, the apprenticeship training levy 2% and the family welfare levy 3%** for employees earning
**less than 10 minimum wages**. Pension, occupational risk and the **family compensation fund 4% are never
exonerated**. See Module 5.3.

> **Newest-law check — the 2024 pension reform is NOT the operative law for FY2026 (control C2).**
> Colombia enacted a complete replacement of its pension architecture, **Law 2381 of 2024** ("establishing
> the Integral Social Protection System for Old Age, Invalidity and Death from Common Causes"), which
> replaces the two competing regimes of Law 100 of 1993 with a **pillar system**. It is the newest pension
> law on the statute book and it is the reason this guide states its pension basis explicitly. **It does
> not govern any 2026 payroll period**, and the rates in the table above are the Law 100 of 1993 rates as
> amended by Law 797 of 2003. The judicial history that produces that result:

The Court reviewed **procedure only**, not substance |

> must apply Law 100 of 1993 as amended by Law 797 of 2003 — the rates, bands, floor and ceiling set out
> in this module. **No part of Law 2381 of 2024 may be configured as live for 2026.** The reform is a
> 2027 change-control item, and because judgment C-264 of 2026 was handed down on 25 August 2026 the
> parameters below must be re-verified before the April 2027 cut-over.
>
> **What will change on 1 April 2027, and what will not.** Recorded here so the 2027 change is scoped, not
> rediscovered:

> |---|---|---|
> | Total pension contribution | **16%** of the contribution base | **16%** of the contribution base — **unchanged** (art. 20) |
> | Employer / employee split | **12% / 4%** | Expressed as **75% / 25% of the total contribution**, which yields the same **12% / 4%** — **unchanged** (art. 20) |
> | Contribution-base ceiling | **25 minimum wages** | **25 minimum wages** — **unchanged** (art. 22) |
> | Contribution base definition | Monthly salary per Labour Code art. 127 | Monthly salary per Labour Code art. 127 — **unchanged** (art. 22) |
> | **Solidarity fund surcharge** | **1.0%** at ≥ 4 minimum wages, **plus** a banded **0.2%–1.0%** subsistence surcharge from ≥ 16 minimum wages; **1.0%–2.0%** in total | **Replaced by a single five-band scale on the whole contribution base: 1.5%** (≥ 4 and < 7), **1.8%** (≥ 7 and < 11), **2.5%** (≥ 11 and < 19), **2.8%** (≥ 19 and ≤ 20), **3.0%** (> 20 minimum wages) — art. 20 nums. 1–5. **This is the one employee-facing deduction that changes** |
> | Destination of the 16% | Split by the employee's chosen regime — public average-premium regime or a private individual-account fund | **Routed by income level**: the average-premium component takes the contributions on the base from 1 up to **2.3 minimum wages**, and the complementary individual-savings component takes the part above that (art. 19). **Caveat: art. 19 literal k is one of the nine provisions returned to the Chamber**, so the routing detail must be re-read after the cured debate |

>
> The employer's collection duty is unchanged in substance: the employer pays its own share and withholds
> the employee's share at the moment of payment (Law 2381 of 2024 art. 21, matching Law 100 of 1993
> art. 22).

### Are contributions managed at a national or sub-national level?

**National.** There is no departmental or municipal social-security contribution, no regional rate
variation and no sub-national scheme. All rates, bases, floors and ceilings above apply uniformly across
the country.

Three points of institutional, not rate, variation:

| Point | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Occupational-risk class** | Varies by **work centre**, not by geography. A company with two work centres legitimately carries two risk classes and two rates. Where a person performs several occupations, they contribute at the **highest** risk class (Decree 1072 of 2015 art. 2.2.4.2.5.6) |
| **Family compensation fund** | Organised by department; the receiving institution code differs, the 4% rate does not |
| **Small-business levy scales** | Law 1429 of 2010 provides a graduated scale for qualifying micro and small enterprises, with a special regime for **Amazonas, Guainía and Vaupés**. Visible in the contribution-return rate tables as contributor classes C and D. Not applicable to standard corporate payroll |

**Collection is national but intermediated.** The employer never transacts directly with the government
for social security. Contributions are self-assessed and paid through a **licensed private information
operator** (*operador de información*) which files the integrated contribution return and distributes the
money to the health insurer, pension administrator, occupational-risk insurer, family compensation fund,
apprenticeship service and family welfare institute. The audit counterparty is the **UGPP**, which
cross-matches tax-authority data against filed contribution returns.

## 5.1.1 Contribution Basis Definition

### For each mandatory contribution, provide the statutory definition of the earnings base.

**Health, pension, solidarity fund and occupational risk — the contribution base (*Ingreso Base de
Cotización*, IBC).**

Law 100 of 1993 art. 18, as substituted by **Law 797 of 2003 art. 5**, verbatim highlights:

> *"The limit of the contribution base shall be **twenty-five (25) legal monthly minimum wages**
> in force, for public- and private-sector employees."*
> *"Contributions for employees whose remuneration is agreed under the integral-salary arrangement
> shall be calculated on **70%** of that salary."*
> *"In no case may the contribution base be **lower than one legal monthly minimum wage** in
> force."*
> Par. 1: where the employee has two or more employers, contributions are made **proportionally** to each
> remuneration and accumulate up to the legal ceiling; *"contributions to the health
> system must be made on the same base"*.

Health uses the **same base** as pension — Law 100 art. 204 par. 1 — and occupational risk likewise
(Decree 1072 of 2015 art. 2.2.4.3.3). The content of the base is the set of payments that are *salary*
under Labour Code arts. 127 and 128, clamped by Law 1393 of 2010 art. 30.

**Family compensation fund, apprenticeship training levy and family welfare levy — the payroll-levy base
(*nómina mensual de salarios*).**

Law 21 of 1982 art. 17, verbatim:

> *"… the monthly salary payroll means the **totality of the payments made in respect of the
> various constituent elements of salary** within the meaning of the labour law, whatever their
> designation, and in addition those made for statutory and collectively agreed or
> contractual **paid rest periods**."*
> Foreign-currency payments are included, converted at the official rate on the **last day of the month**.

### Clarify the legal definitions of any specific terms used.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Contribution base (IBC)** | The monthly sum of salary-constituting payments, after the 40% clamp, the 70% all-inclusive rule, the 1-minimum-wage floor and the 25-minimum-wage ceiling, rounded up to the whole peso |
| **Payroll-levy base** | The total of all salary elements **plus statutory and contractual paid rest days**, with **no ceiling** |
| **Salary** (*salario*) | Labour Code art. 127 — everything received in money or in kind as direct consideration for the service, whatever its name |
| **Non-salary** (*no constitutivo de salario*) | Labour Code art. 128 — occasional gratuities, profit participation, sums given to enable the work, the statutory benefits, and expressly-pacted benefits and allowances |
| **All-inclusive salary** (*salario integral*) | Labour Code art. 132 num. 2 — a written salary above 13 minimum wages compensating benefits, premiums and allowances in advance, except vacation |
| **Statutory benefits** (*prestaciones sociales*) | Titles VIII–IX of the Labour Code — the service bonus, severance savings, interest on severance savings, work clothing, sickness assistance |
| **Solidarity account** (*subcuenta de solidaridad*) | The solidarity pension fund sub-account subsidising contributions of low-income and informal workers |
| **Subsistence account** (*subcuenta de subsistencia*) | The solidarity pension fund sub-account funding the non-contributory old-age benefit |
| **Public defined-benefit regime** (*Régimen de Prima Media*, RPM) | The pay-as-you-go pension regime administered by Colpensiones |
| **Private individual-account regime** (*Régimen de Ahorro Individual con Solidaridad*, RAIS) | The funded regime administered by private pension administrators |
| **Occupational risk class** | I to V, assigned to the work centre by economic activity under Decree 768 of 2022 |
| **Exemption** (*exoneración*) | The Tax Code art. 114-1 relief from employer health, apprenticeship-training and family-welfare contributions for employees below 10 minimum wages |
| **Change event** (*novedad*) | A within-month event reported on the contribution return that alters the base or the contributory days |

## 5.2 Calculation Logic and Edge Cases

### What is the step-by-step calculation logic for all types of social security contributions?

```
STEP 1  Build the CONTRIBUTION BASE
        1.1  Sum salary-constituting payments of the month (Labour Code arts. 127/128)
             - transport and connectivity allowances EXCLUDED
             - service bonus, severance savings and interest EXCLUDED
             - vacation pay INCLUDED
        1.2  Apply the 40% clamp: if non-salary payments > 40% of total remuneration,
             add the excess back into the base   (Law 1393 of 2010 art. 30)
        1.3  All-inclusive salary: base = 70% of the salary
        1.4  Floor: base >= 1 minimum wage, pro-rated to contributory days
        1.5  Ceiling: base <= 25 minimum wages = 43,772,625
        1.6  Round the base UP to the nearest whole peso

STEP 2  Build the PAYROLL-LEVY BASE (separate, no ceiling)
        2.1  Sum all salary elements PLUS statutory and contractual paid rest days
        2.2  All-inclusive salary: base = 70%
        2.3  No floor, no ceiling
        2.4  Round UP to the nearest whole peso

STEP 3  EMPLOYEE deductions on the contribution base
        Health         4%
        Pension        4%    (0% for an employed old-age pensioner)
        Solidarity fund 1% if base >= 4 minimum wages
        Subsistence    0.2%-1.0% if base >= 16 minimum wages, by band
        Round EACH amount UP to the nearest multiple of 100

STEP 4  Evaluate the EXEMPTION (Tax Code art. 114-1)
        exempt := employer_is_income_tax_filing_legal_person
                  AND month_earnings < 10 x minimum wage      # STRICTLY less than
        (a natural-person employer qualifies only if it employs 2 or more workers)

STEP 5  EMPLOYER contributions
        Pension                     12%   of contribution base            always
        Health                      8.5%  of contribution base            0 if exempt
        Occupational risk           class rate of contribution base       always
        Family compensation fund    4%    of payroll-levy base            always
        Apprenticeship training     2%    of payroll-levy base            0 if exempt
        Family welfare              3%    of payroll-levy base            0 if exempt
        Round EACH amount UP to the nearest multiple of 100

STEP 6  Report contributory DAYS per subsystem and any change events
```

### Are contributions based on actual or standard salary?

**Actual salary**, with four statutory adjustments — the floor, the ceiling, the 70% all-inclusive rule and
the 40% clamp. Colombia has **no notional, banded or standard contribution salary**; there is no class
system and no scheduled contribution wage.

Two consequences worth stating explicitly:

1. A variable-pay employee has a **different contribution base every month**, and can legitimately cross
   the 4-minimum-wage solidarity trigger, the 10-minimum-wage exemption line and the 25-minimum-wage
   ceiling in different directions in successive months.
2. Where actual salary falls below the floor — a partial month, a low-paid part-time employee — the base is
   raised to **1 minimum wage**, pro-rated to the contributory days where a valid change event reduces
   them. The employee still contributes 4% + 4% of that raised base even though the salary was lower.

**A special case: the apprentice.** Decree 0223 of 5 March 2026 art. 2.2.6.3.3.4 par. 2 provides that
*"in any event the contribution base must be [at] one (1) legal monthly minimum wage in
force"* and that the employee-side deductions are computed on that base. So an apprentice in the
classroom phase, receiving 75% of the minimum wage, nonetheless contributes on a **full** minimum wage.

### Describe rules for eligibility, joining month, termination month, mid-period hires, mid-period terminations, and mid-period salary changes.

**Eligibility.** Affiliation to health, pension and occupational risk is **compulsory from the first day of
the employment relationship**, with no waiting period, no minimum hours and no minimum earnings. Two
timing rules matter:

- Occupational-risk **coverage starts the calendar day after affiliation** (Decree 1072 of 2015
  art. 2.2.4.2.5.4), and a new hire must be reported to the risk insurer **no later than the business day
  following** the start date (art. 2.2.4.3.6). An employee who starts work before affiliation is uninsured,
  and the employer bears the occupational-risk benefit directly.
- Health and pension affiliation must be effective from the start date.

**Joining month.** Contributions are due for the days actually worked, with a *hire* change event carrying
the contributory day count. The base is the actual earnings for those days, floored at the pro-rated
minimum wage. The full monthly rates apply — there is no reduced first-month rate.

**Termination month.** Contributions are due to the termination date, with a *termination* change event.
Termination settlement components are treated as follows:

| Component | In the contribution base? |
|---|---|
| Salary to the termination date | **Yes** |
| Accrued vacation paid in cash | **Yes** — paid rest |
| Accrued service bonus | No |
| Accrued severance savings and interest | No |
| Dismissal indemnity | **No** |
| Payment in lieu of notice | No |

**Mid-period hire and termination in the same month.** Both change events are reported; the base is the
earnings of the days actually worked.

**Mid-period salary change.** Split the month at the effective date, prorate each rate over its own days,
sum to the monthly base, and report a *salary variation* change event. Where an increase takes the employee
across the 10-minimum-wage line **for that month's total earnings**, the exemption is lost for the whole
month — the test is on the month, not on the segment.

**Within-month change events the engine must support** (contribution-return mnemonics, verified from
Resolution 2388 of 2016 Technical Annex 2):

| Event | Meaning | Effect on contributions |
|---|---|---|
| ING | Hire | Contributory days from the hire date |
| RET | Termination | Contributory days to the termination date |
| TDE / TAE | Transfer from / to a health insurer | Institution code changes mid-month |
| TDP / TAP | Transfer from / to a pension administrator | Institution code changes mid-month |
| VSP / VST | Permanent / transitory salary variation | Base recomputed |
| **SLN** | Suspension or unpaid leave | Health rate moves to 8.5% or 0% and pension to 12% or 16% depending on the variant; the payroll levies fall to **0%** |
| IGE | General-illness incapacity | Contributions continue on the base |
| LMA | Maternity or paternity leave | Contributions continue on the base |
| VAC / LR | Vacation or paid leave | Contributions continue; vacation pay is in the base |
| IRL | Occupational accident or disease incapacity | Contributions continue |
| AVP | Voluntary pension contribution | Additional voluntary amount |
| VCT | Change of work centre | Occupational-risk class may change |

### Do different payment frequencies affect contribution rates or parameters?

**No.** Rates, floors, ceilings and triggers are all **monthly** parameters, independent of how often the
employee is paid. A weekly-paid employee and a monthly-paid employee on the same annual salary contribute
identically.

| Aspect | Effect of frequency |
|---|---|
| Rates | None |
| Floor and ceiling | None — both are monthly |
| Solidarity-fund trigger | None — tested on the **monthly** base |
| Exemption test | None — tested on the **monthly** earnings |
| Filing frequency | None — the contribution return is monthly regardless |
| Contributory days | Reported per subsystem for the month, whatever the number of payments |

The only frequency-sensitive interaction is the five-payment weekly month discussed in Module 1.3 — which
changes the amount of the monthly base, not any parameter.

### Detail variations based on employee age or gender.

**Age.**

| Age or status | Variation |
|---|---|
| 15–17, lawfully employed | No rate variation. Restricted hours (6 per day, 30 per week, daytime only) reduce the base |
| 18 and above | Standard rates |
| Employed old-age pensioner | **Pension contribution ceases entirely** — neither the 4% employee nor the 12% employer share is due. Health, occupational risk and the levies continue. Worker sub-type `01` |
| Employee in a high-risk activity | Additional employer pension contribution and reduced retirement age (Decree-Law 2090 of 2003) — see the open item in Module 5.1.1 |
| Retirement age (57 women / 62 men, public regime) | Affects entitlement, not the rate. Reaching the age does not by itself stop the contribution; **being a pensioner in active service** does |

**Gender: no variation of any kind.** No gender-differentiated rate, base, floor, ceiling or trigger exists
in any of the eight contributions.

### Is there a minimum number of working days required for contributions?

**No.** There is no minimum-days, minimum-hours or minimum-earnings qualifying condition. **One day of
employment generates a contribution obligation** for that day, with the base floored at the pro-rated
minimum wage.

Three refinements:

1. **The floor is pro-rated to contributory days**, not applied in full to a part-month. An employee hired
   on the 20th contributes on 11 days at the pro-rated floor, not on a full minimum wage.
2. **Unpaid leave (change event SLN)** reduces the contributory days. The contribution-return rate table
   shows that in this case health moves to 8.5% or 0% and pension to 12% or 16% depending on the variant,
   and the payroll levies fall to **0%**.
3. **Part-time contributions.** Law 2466 of 2025 art. 34 permits **micro-enterprises and households** to
   pay social security on a part-time basis, computed in weeks and settled at each month end, and permits
   proportional contributions where monthly income is below the minimum wage. Access requires commercial
   registration, accounting books and an internal register of employment contracts. Explicitly outside
   standard corporate payroll scope, and monitored by the UGPP; the part-time contribution may **not** be
   used where the work is in fact full-time (art. 34 par. 2).

### How are contributions calculated for Board of Directors who are also employees?

Colombia distinguishes sharply between a board mandate and an employment relationship, and the payroll
treatment follows the **substance** of the relationship, not the title.

| Situation | Employment relationship? | Social security | Withholding tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Director who is also an employee** — an executive director holding an employment contract | **Yes** | Full employee treatment on the **whole** remuneration, salary and director's fees alike, where the fees are consideration for the service. Employer 12% + 8.5% + occupational risk + levies; employee 4% + 4% + solidarity fund | Art. 383 progressive table |
| **Non-executive director receiving only attendance fees** (*honorarios de junta directiva*) | **No** — this is a corporate mandate, not employment | **Not** an employee contribution. The director contributes as an **independent worker** on a base of **40% of the monthly fee**, with a 1-minimum-wage floor (Law 1955 of 2019 art. 244) | **Art. 383 table applies** — Tax Code art. 383 par. 2 extends the employment table to non-employment work income using *"pagos mensualizados"*: total contract value less mandatory health and pension contributions, divided by the months of the contract |
| **Statutory legal representative on an employment contract** | **Yes** | Full employee treatment; generally management staff excluded from the maximum-hours rules | Art. 383 table |

**Two engine consequences.**

1. Where a person is **both** an employee and a fee-earning director of the same or a related company, the
   two income streams have **different social-security bases** — full salary for the employment, 40% of
   fees for the mandate — but the same withholding table. They accumulate against the **shared
   25-minimum-wage ceiling**.
2. Directors' fees paid to a **non-employee** are outside payroll scope for social security and are handled
   as an independent-contractor payment. Mercans scope covers the employment relationship only; the
   fee-only director is descoped and the client retains responsibility.

### Are year-end adjustments required?

**No year-end reconciliation of social security is required.** Colombian contributions are definitively
settled month by month. There is no annual return, no annual true-up, no reconciliation filing and no
year-end adjustment mechanism for health, pension, occupational risk, the solidarity fund or the payroll
levies. Twelve monthly contribution returns are the complete annual record.

What **is** required at or around year end:

| Obligation | Timing | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Severance-savings liquidation** at 31 December and deposit to the employee's fund | By **14 February** | Not a contribution — a statutory benefit deposit. Late deposit costs **one day's salary per day of delay** |
| **Interest on severance savings** paid to the employee | During **January** | Paid **to the employee**, not to the fund |
| **Recalculation of the fixed withholding percentage** (Procedure 2 only) | **December** | Tax, not social security |
| **Reparameterisation for the new minimum wage** | 1 January | The floor, ceiling and all triggers move with the new minimum wage |
| **Annual income and withholding certificate** (Form 220) to each employee | Last business day of **March** | Tax |

**Corrections, not adjustments.** Where a past month was under- or over-declared, the fix is a **correction
planilla** (planilla type N), in which the employer reports paired records — an `A` group carrying the
originally settled value and a `C` group carrying the corrected value. The sum of the contribution bases
in the `C` records must be **greater than or equal to** that of the `A` records, and likewise for the day
counts. The correction may only be filed through the **same information operator** that carried the
original return, absent an authorisation from the financial superintendence. Under-declaration attracts
late-payment interest and UGPP assessment exposure.

## 5.3 Voluntary Contributions & Private Schemes

### Can employees and employers make additional voluntary contributions?

**Yes — through four distinct vehicles, each with its own tax character and its own cap.** Distinguishing
them correctly is essential, because two are non-taxable income and two are exempt income, and they
consume different caps.

| # | Vehicle | Who contributes | Tax character | Cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Voluntary contributions to the private individual pension account** (*cotizaciones voluntarias al RAIS*) | Employee, and employer on the employee's behalf | **Non-taxable income** (Tax Code art. 55) | **25% of annual labour income** and **2,500 tax units** = COP 130,935,000 |
| 2 | **Voluntary pension fund contributions** (*aportes a fondos de pensiones voluntarias*) | Employee and employer | **Exempt income** (Tax Code art. 126-1) | Shared 30%-of-labour-income / **3,800 tax units** = COP 199,021,200 annual cap |
| 3 | **Construction-savings and long-term-savings accounts** (*AFC / AVC*) | Employee | **Exempt income** (Tax Code art. 126-4) | Shares the same 30% / 3,800-tax-unit cap with vehicle 2 |
| 4 | **Additional contributions to the public defined-benefit regime** | Employee | Contribution, not a tax relief in itself | Within the 25-minimum-wage ceiling |

**Vehicle 1 — the individual-account voluntary contribution.** Tax Code art. 55 treats these as *ingresos
no constitutivos de renta ni ganancia ocasional* — non-taxable income deducted **before** the 40% and
1,340-tax-unit caps are applied, which makes them materially more valuable than exempt income. The cap is
25% of annual labour income, subject to an absolute 2,500 tax units. A withdrawal that does not meet the
qualifying conditions is **withheld at 35%**.

**Vehicles 2 and 3 — voluntary pension funds and long-term savings accounts.** Tax Code arts. 126-1 and
126-4 make these *rentas exentas* — exempt income deducted **inside** the 40% and 1,340-tax-unit caps, and
sharing a single combined annual limit of the lower of 30% of labour income or 3,800 tax units. Employer
contributions to a voluntary pension fund are deductible for the employer up to **3,800 tax units per
employee** (art. 126-1 first paragraph).

**Employer voluntary contributions** to a voluntary pension fund on the employee's behalf are treated as
the employee's own contribution for cap purposes and are exempt income in the employee's hands, within the
same shared cap.

**There is no voluntary top-up of health, occupational risk or the payroll levies.** Those three are fixed
statutory percentages with no voluntary element.

### Which employee contributions are allowed as pre-tax deductions?

Colombia does not use the term "pre-tax deduction". It uses two distinct legal categories, and the
distinction determines where in the depuration each item sits.

**Category A — non-taxable income (deducted at step B, before the 40% cap):**

| Item | Rate or amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mandatory **pension** contribution | 4% of the contribution base | Tax Code art. 55 |
| **Solidarity pension fund** contribution | 1% + 0.2%–1.0% | Tax Code art. 55 — confirmed by field 24 of the information return, which groups pension and solidarity contributions on one line |
| Mandatory **health** contribution | 4% of the contribution base | Tax Code art. 56 |
| **Voluntary contributions to the individual pension account** | ≤ 25% of annual labour income, cap 2,500 tax units | Tax Code art. 55 |
| **Third-party meal payments** within the thresholds | ≤ 41 tax units per month, employee salary ≤ 310 tax units | Tax Code art. 387-1 |

**Category B — deductions and exempt income (deducted at steps D and E, inside the 40% and 1,340-tax-unit
caps):**

| Item | Monthly cap 2026 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Housing-loan interest and monetary correction | 100 tax units = COP 5,237,400 | Tax Code arts. 119 and 387 |
| Prepaid health / health-insurance premiums | 16 tax units = COP 837,984 | Tax Code art. 387 |
| Dependants deduction — 10% of the month's gross labour income | 32 tax units = COP 1,675,968 | Tax Code art. 387 |
| Voluntary pension fund and long-term savings contributions | 30% of labour income, 3,800 tax units per year | Tax Code arts. 126-1, 126-4 |
| The 25% exempt-income deduction | 790 tax units per year = COP 41,375,460 | Tax Code art. 206 num. 10 |

**Category C — outside the caps entirely:**

| Item | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Additional dependants deduction | **72 tax units per dependant per year**, maximum 4 dependants = COP 3,770,928 each | Tax Code art. 336 num. 3, second paragraph: *"in addition to the limit established in the preceding paragraph"* |

**Not deductible against withholding.** The 1% / 240-tax-unit invoice-based deduction of Tax Code
art. 336 num. 5 is expressly excluded from the withholding calculation — *"shall not be taken into account for the
calculation of withholding at source"* — confirmed by **DIAN Concepto 015007 (int. 973) of 13 September
2023**. It is available only in the employee's own annual return.

**Post-tax deductions with no tax effect:** union dues, cooperative and savings-fund dues, payroll loans,
insurance premiums other than qualifying health cover, and court-ordered garnishments.

### Are there private insurance schemes with employer and employee contributions?

**Yes. Four are common in the Colombian market. None is a statutory scheme, and none replaces a statutory
contribution.**

| Scheme | Nature | Employer contribution | Employee contribution | Tax treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Prepaid medicine** (*medicina prepagada*) | Supplementary private health cover, on top of the compulsory health system | Common — often 50% to 100% | Common, by payroll deduction | Employee may deduct up to **16 tax units per month** (COP 837,984). The provider must be supervised by the National Health Superintendence |
| **Supplementary health plans** (*planes complementarios de salud*) | Sold by the employee's own health insurer | Common | Common | Same 16-tax-unit monthly cap |
| **Group life and personal accident insurance** | Private cover, often extending occupational-risk benefits | Common | Sometimes | Employer premiums are a deductible business expense; not a statutory employee deduction, and a taxable benefit to the employee where personal cover is provided |
| **Voluntary pension funds** | Third-pillar retirement savings, administered by the private pension administrators | Common | Common | Exempt income within the shared 30% / 3,800-tax-unit annual cap |

**Payroll treatment.** Employer-paid premiums are generally structured as **non-salary** under a written
Labour Code art. 128 pact, which keeps them out of the contribution base, the payroll-levy base and the
statutory-benefit base — subject always to the **40% clamp**. Employee-paid premiums are a **post-tax
payroll deduction** requiring the employee's written authorisation for each case (Labour Code art. 149),
which may separately generate a withholding deduction under Category B above.

**No occupational pension scheme exists in Colombia** in the sense of a defined-benefit or defined
contribution plan sponsored by the employer as an alternative to the state system. The mandatory pension
system is universal, and the voluntary pension fund is an individual product to which an employer may
contribute — not an employer-sponsored plan with its own trustees and rules.

### What documents or declarations are required from an employee?

| Item | Employee declaration | Supporting evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Health insurer, pension administrator, pension regime, severance-savings fund | Written election | Affiliation form |
| Change of health insurer or pension administrator | Written election, reported as a transfer change event | Transfer form |
| **Foreign pension-system waiver** | Written election not to affiliate to Colombian pension | **Proof of affiliation to a pension system abroad** (Decree 1072 of 2015 art. 2.2.1.2.1.4) |
| Voluntary individual-account contributions | Amount and period | Pension administrator certificate |
| Voluntary pension fund contributions | Amount and period | Fund certificate |
| Long-term savings account contributions | Amount and period | Financial-institution certificate |
| Prepaid health / health-insurance deduction | Amount paid | Provider certificate; the provider must be supervised by the National Health Superintendence or the Financial Superintendence |
| Housing-loan interest deduction | Amount paid | Lender certificate |
| Dependants | Number, **each dependant's identity document**, relationship, basis of dependency | Civil registration; for a dependent spouse, parent or sibling, a public accountant's certificate of annual income **below 260 tax units** (COP 13,617,240) or a certified state of physical or psychological dependence |
| Employee-requested higher withholding rate | Written request | Effective from the **month after** the request (Tax Code art. 383 par. 3) |
| Multi-employer status | Declaration of the other employer and the contribution base there | Needed for the proportional allocation against the shared ceiling |
| Active-pensioner status | Declaration | Pension award document |
| Any voluntary payroll deduction | **Written order signed for each case** | Labour Code art. 149 num. 1 |

**Who counts as a dependant — Tax Code art. 387 par. 2**, numerals 2 and 3 as amended by **Law 2411 of
2024 art. 2**: children up to 18; children aged 18 to 25 whose higher or technical education the taxpayer
finances; children over 18 in a certified state of physical or psychological dependence; a spouse or
permanent partner dependent through absence of income or annual income below **260 tax units**, or through
certified physical or psychological dependence; and parents and siblings on the same conditions.

**A benefit-stacking point.** Decree 2231 of 2023 art. 3 provides that a single dependant ordinarily gives
rise to only one of the two dependant deductions — *"except where the taxpayer has income from an
employment or statutory and regulatory relationship, in which case **both deductions may be applied for the same
dependant**"*. For an **employee**, therefore, the engine may take **both** the art. 387 dependant
deduction (10% of gross, capped at 32 tax units per month) **and** the art. 336 additional 72 tax units per
dependant, for the same dependant.
