        Overview
Tax year
Fiscal year starting on 1 January and ending on 31 December.


Tax Regions
There is only one tax region.


Tax Authority name
Danish Tax Agency (Skattestyrelsen).


Residency: 
Who is a resident:
An individual is considered a tax resident in Denmark if they meet any of the following conditions:
* Physical Presence:
   * They are present in Denmark for more than 183 days during a calendar year, either consecutively or intermittently.
* Place of Residence or Abode:
   * Their primary place of business, residence, or habitual abode is in Denmark.
* Employment or Activity:
   * They are employed or carry out their primary economic activity in Denmark.
Who is liable for tax in Denmark:
* The salary recipient is a resident of Denmark, regardless of the funding source.
* The income is generated from services performed within Denmark or services that benefit a company based in Denmark, even if the funding source is outside Denmark.
* The funding source is in Denmark, irrespective of the beneficiary's residence or the location of the services provided.
Non Residency:
* Generally, someone who comes to Denmark, stays less than 183 days in any 12-month period, and who does not have an ‘enduring relationship’ with Denmark, is taxed as a non-resident. A non-resident must pay tax on income for personal services performed in Denmark, and other income from Denmark sources. Non-residents are taxed only on Danish-source income.

	Rounding and Precision Rules


Statutory Rounding Rules
Statutory basis: Den Juridiske Vejledning A.B.4.1.1.2.6 (Nedrunding ved beregning af A-skat og AM-bidrag) presents the down-to-10 base method as one that "kan eksempelvis benyttes" — it is optional, not mandatory, and its "no øre on the payslip" requirement applies only if that method is used. 
This guide applies the precision rule — "rundes op til nærmeste hele kronebeløb" — i.e. AM-bidrag and A-skat are each rounded up to the nearest whole krone. Withholding basis: kildeskatteloven §46 and §49. Source: https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2292833 (v3.16)
Precision Rules
1. AM-Income Base: use the exact value to 2 decimals; do not round the base down. Determined separately per stream (other earnings; holiday pay).
2. AM-Contribution (8%): 8% of the exact AM-income base, rounded UP to the nearest whole krone. Two separate amounts — AM-bidrag on other earnings, AM-bidrag on holiday pay.
3. A-Income Base: exact income − exact AM-bidrag − tax-card allowance [fradrag], to 2 decimals; do not round down to 10. Separately per stream, using tax card.
4. A-Tax Amount: trækprocent × the exact A-tax base, rounded UP to the nearest whole krone. Two separate amounts — A-skat on other earnings, A-skat on holiday pay.
5. Payslip & eIndkomst: income, deductions and net pay shown to 2 decimals (øre); AM-bidrag and A-skat shown as rounded up whole krone. In eIndkomst, felt 13/14 (income) carry 2 decimals; felt 15/16 (A-skat/AM-bidrag) are rounded up whole krone. Present other-earnings and holiday-pay levies on separate lines; felt 16 = other-earnings AM + holiday AM, felt 15 = other-earnings A-skat + holiday A-skat, as the sum of the already-rounded per-stream components — do not re-round the totals.
Example. Inputs: taxable income 25,125.39; deductions 5,000.00; tax rate 38%.
* AM-income base = 25,125.39 (exact)
* AM-bidrag = 25,125.39 × 8% = 2,010.0312 → up = 2,011.00
* A-income = 25,125.39 − 2,011.00 = 23,114.39
* A-tax base = 23,114.39 − 5,000.00 = 18,114.39
* A-tax = 18,114.39 × 38% = 6,883.4682 → up = 6,884.00
Bases exact (no down-to-10); AM-bidrag and A-skat rounded up to whole krone. 
Statutory basis: Den Juridiske Vejledning A.B.4.1.1.2.6 (Nedrunding ved beregning af A-skat og AM-bidrag); kildeskatteloven §46 (duty to withhold) and kildeskattebekendtgørelsen. The round-up method below is the convention adopted for this guide. 
1. AM-Income Base: Round DOWN to the nearest amount divisible by 10 DKK (before AM contributions are calculated) - determined on each basis separately: other AM-income and holiday-pay AM-income 
2. AM-Contribution (8%): Round the result UP to the nearest whole Krone - as two separate amounts: AM-bidrag on ordinary earnings and AM-bidrag on holiday pay. 
3. A-Income Base: Round DOWN to the nearest amount divisible by 10 DKK (after deducting that stream's already-rounded AM-bidrag, before deducting the tax free deductions & calculating tax) - separately for holiday pay and other earnings, using that stream's tax card. 
4. A-Tax Amount: Round the result UP to the nearest whole Krone - as two separate amounts: A-skat on other earnings and A-skat on holiday pay 
5. Payslip: Show only whole numbers (no øre). Present other incomeand holiday-pay AM-bidrag / A-skat as separate lines; the eIndkomst totals are the sum of the already-rounded components -> felt 16 = other income AM + holiday AM, felt 15 = ordinary A-skat + holiday A-skat. Do not re-round the totals. 
Example: Example Calculation
Employee Data:
* Taxable Income (exact): 25,125.39 DKK
* Tax Card Deduction (Fradrag): 5,000 DKK
* Tax Rate (Trækprocent): 38%
Step 1: Calculate AM-bidrag (Labour Market Contribution)
First, establish the base for the 8% tax.
* AM Contribution Base: 25,125.39 DKK
* Rounding Rule: Round DOWN to nearest 10.
   * (25,125.39 becomes 25,120)
* AM Contribution Base (Rounded): 25,120 DKK
Now, calculate the 8%:
* Calculation: $25,120 \times 0.08 = 2,009.60 \text{ DKK}$
* Rounding Rule: Round UP to the nearest whole krone.
* AM-bidrag to Pay: 2,010 DKK
Step 2: Calculate A-Tax (Withholding Tax)
First, find the "Actual A-Income" (Gross minus AM-bidrag).
* Taxable Income: 25,125.39 DKK
* Less AM-bidrag: - 2,010.00 DKK
Additional step:
* Actual A-Income: 23,115.39 DKK
Note: Before you calculate the A-Tax base we need to round down the Actual A-income value which is taxable income minus AM contribution amount. and then A-Tax base is calculated i.e A-Income - tax free deduction. Tax free deduction amount we are neither rounding down nor rounding up.
Now, apply the rounding rule before calculating the A-tax base: 
* Rounding Rule: Round A-Income DOWN to nearest 10.
   * (23,115.39 becomes 23,110)
* A-Income (Rounded): 23,110 DKK.
Now, apply the Tax Card Deduction (Fradrag):
*  A Tax Base: 23,110 - 5,000.10 (Deduction) = 18,109.90. DKK
Finally, calculate the 38% tax:
* Calculation: $18,109.90 \times 0.38 = 6,881.76 \text{ DKK}$
* Rounding Rule: Round UP to the nearest whole krone.
* A-Tax to Pay: 6,882 DKK
Final Result (Payslip View)
* As you can see, even though the deduction had 10 øre (0.10), the final tax remained 6,882 DKK. This is because the rounding "up" absorbs the small differences.

Income Tax


Personal income tax (PIT) is levied on wages and salaries at progressive tax rates. 


When is pay subject to income tax
Income tax is calculated monthly, hourly or Biweekly(14days). Employees are typically paid at the last working day of the month.

Pay Structure


Working Hours:
* Standard: 37 hours per week depends upon the company, anything exceeding this hours will be considered as Overtime.
Calculation:
* Step 1: Standard Danish working hours per week = 37 hours
* Step 2: Number of weeks in a year = 52 weeks
So,
* 37 hours/week×52 weeks/year=1,924 hours/year
* Step 3: Divide by 12 months:
* 1,924÷12=160.33 hours/month
For Bi-Weekly
* Step 3: divide by 26
* 1,924÷26= 74 hours/bi weekly
For weekly 
* Step 3: divide by 52
* 1,924÷52= 37hours/weekly
Daily 
* Step 1: divide by 5 days
* 37 ÷5 = 7.40hours/day
Therefore:
* 160.33 hours/month = Average monthly working hours for a full-time employee in Denmark (based on 37-hour weeks).
Overtime:
* Regulated by collective bargaining agreements.
* May necessitate additional compensation or time off.
Pay Frequency:
* Monthly (Employees are typically paid on the last working day of the Month).
* Cut-off Dates: Cut-off dates for payroll data submission (e.g., timesheets, new hires, variable data) are a matter of company policy and are not statutorily defined. To ensure timely processing, validation, and payment by the end of the month, cut-off dates are typically set between the 10th and the 20th of the month
* Bi-weekly / Hourly paid employees are paid on the last working day of the Biweekly 
Bi weekly - Extra Pay Period Handling
The Week 53 Phenomenon
In a bi-weekly cycle, the standard year consists of 26 periods ($14 \text{ days} \times 26 = 364 \text{ days}$). Since a year is 365 or 366 days, the calendar drifts. A 27th pay period (often called Week 53) occurs approximately every 11 years.
* Statutory Handling: Denmark uses a cumulative withholding system where the tax card allocates a specific tax-free deduction (Fradrag) per period.
   * Standard Year: The employee receives 26 portions of their annual deduction.
   * Week 53 Year: The employee has likely exhausted their annual deduction by period 26.
* Configuration Requirement: The system must explicitly handle the 27th period.
   * Method A (Automatic): If the system tracks "Remaining Deduction," period 27 will naturally have zero deduction available, resulting in higher tax.
   * Method B (Forced): The system must aggressively apply the "Secondary Card" logic (tax rate only, no deduction) for the 27th period to prevent under-taxation.
* Communication: This effectively reduces net pay for the 27th period compared to the previous 26. It requires proactive communication to employees to explain that this is a mathematical necessity of the tax year, not an error.


Tax Cards


* In Denmark, the main, secondary, and exemption tax cards are collectively known as the combined tax card. This digital card is accessible through TastSelv, located at the top of the employee advance tax statement.
* All individuals liable to pay income tax must have a tax card (skattekort) issued by the Danish Tax Authority (SKAT). 
* The tax card determines how much tax the employer should withhold from an employee’s salary. 
* It ensures accurate deduction of Labour Market Contribution (AM-bidrag) and income tax based on the individual’s expected income and allowances.
* Each individual is entitled to a tax-free income allowance annually. This amount is DKK 54,100 for 2026 (DKK 51,600 for 2025) (DKK 49,700 in 2024). 
* A tax exemption card specifies this tax-exempt earning limit.
* Employees income is stated at the start of the year in their Primary income statement.
* Tax card details will be provided to our payroll software automatically through Skat.


Types of Cards:


* Primary Tax Card: 
   * Employees primary tax card states both the amount employee will not be taxed on (personal allowance) and the tax rate employee will have to pay.
   * When an employer uses this card, tax is only applied to income above the employee's personal allowance.
   * If an employee has more than one income its employees responsibility to inform the Employer to use the primary cards.


* Secondary Tax Card:
   * An employee's secondary tax card shows their tax rate and is for those with multiple employers.
   * If an employer uses this card, the employee is taxed on the full payable amount.
   * Employees must inform their employers if one is using the primary tax card so others can use the secondary tax card at the same time.


* Exemption Tax card:
   * For individuals whose annual income falls below the tax-free threshold (typically students or part-time workers).
   * Once the threshold is crossed, income becomes taxable as per the individual's tax rate.
   * If the employee manages to earn the corresponding to that of tax exemption card , then they will be using the secondary tax card for the rest of the year.
   * Employees will always have to pay labour market contributions (AM-bidrag).


NOTE: Employee can change make the adjustments in their preliminary income assessment (forskudsopgørelsen) during the Year, and this will be notified to employer by the tax authority

3. Allowances and Benefits in Kind (Personalegoder)
In Denmark, most non-cash benefits provided by employers are considered taxable income, typically valued at their market rate.
* Company Car (Fri bil): This is a highly regulated benefit. The taxable value is calculated annually and added to the employee's income in pro-rata installments each pay period.
   * To calculate company car value for taxation in Denmark, use the purchase or new-car price and fixed rates.
   * The minimum value is always DKK 160,000.
For Cars New or Up to 3 Years Old:
* The new-car price is the basis for calculation for the first 36 months from initial registration.
* After 36 months, the value is reduced to 75% of the new-car price (minimum DKK 160,000).
* Example: A car registered in December 2020 will have its calculation basis reduced to 75% of the new-car price from December 2023.
For Cars Older Than 3 Years:
* For second-hand cars bought more than three years after initial registration, the purchase price is the calculation basis (minimum DKK 160,000).
Minimum Taxable Base: The minimum taxable value for a company car is DKK 160,000.
        Calculating the environmental supplement (periodic tax multiplied by a rate)
   * The environmental supplement is calculated by multiplying the periodic tax (green tax, weight tax, or CO2 owner’s tax) by a specific rate.
   * The supplement increased from 150% to 250% on July 1, 2021, and is projected to reach 700% by 2025.
   * For diesel cars, the compensatory tax (udligningsafgift) must be deducted from the periodic tax before calculating the supplement.
   * For cars with "parrot plates" (business and private use), the private use supplement must be subtracted from the periodic tax before calculation.
   * As of July 1, 2021, the environmental supplement is calculated by multiplying the annual periodic tax by 205%.


* Telephone & Internet (Fri telefon og internet): If an employer pays for an employee's mobile phone or internet connection that can also be used privately, a fixed amount is added to their taxable income.
   * Taxable Value for 2025: DKK 3,500 (in 2026) per year (DKK 3,300 in 2025). This amount is fixed regardless of the actual cost and covers all such devices.
   * For computer : 
      * Taxability: If an employee uses a company-provided computer exclusively for private use, its value is taxable.
      * Employer's Role: The employer reports the computer's value but doesn't deduct the tax.
      * Employee's Tax Liability: The employee pays tax based on the rental cost of a similar computer.
      * Reporting Threshold: If the total value of such benefits exceeds DKK 1,400 in 2025 and 2026 (DKK 1,300 in 2024) annually and the employer hasn't reported it, the employee must disclose the full amount.


* Board and Lodging (Fri kost og logi): When an employer provides accommodation or meals, a standardized value is considered taxable income.
   * Coffee, tea, fruit arrangements and canteen subsidies are tax-free.
   * Fixed rates apply for board and lodging for specific roles, including agricultural helpers, domestic help, kitchen and serving staff, and private and hospital nurses.
   * Employers must calculate A-tax and AM contributions on the amount.
   * SKAT's Official Rates for 2025:
      * Full board and lodging: 4047.50 kr.
      * Full Board per day : 100 kr.
      * 2 Meals per day : 85 kr.
      * 1 Meal per day : 55kr.




4. Tax on Bonuses and Irregular Payments
In Denmark, the calculation for bonuses, or other lump-sum payments is straightforward.
* In Denmark, bonuses, and other lump-sum payments are added to the regular gross salary for the pay period in which they are disbursed.
* The standard tax calculation (Steps 1-7 from Primary tax card) is then applied to this new, higher total gross income for that specific pay period.
* There are no special formulas or annualization methods used for these types of payments.
NOTE: Any benefit in kinds will be direct input it will not be evaluated on the System
5. 53A Pension Scheme
* Understanding Pension Scheme § 53a (Denmark)
   * Pension Scheme § 53a is a special pension arrangement designed primarily for employees staying in Denmark for a limited period. It offers a unique tax structure that benefits international employees planning to leave Denmark after their assignment.
* Key Features of § 53a
   * Taxation Timing
      * Contributions to the pension are taxed monthly, not at the time of withdrawal. 
      * This means employees pay tax upfront, and when they leave Denmark and begin withdrawing their pension, no further Danish tax is applied
   * Eligibility
      * Only employees who are temporarily residing in Denmark are eligible.
      * Typically, employees under the § 48E tax scheme (often expatriates) qualify for § 53a.
      * For Employers the Tax rate is 11%.
      * for Employee the tax rate is 5%
      * Not applicable to CEO / CFO and Senior Management Employees

7. Holiday Pay
   1. For Salaried Employees: The amount is calculated as 12.5% of the employee's gross salary during the accrual period. Every month it is to be accrued and during the termination case Holiday payment is made.
Note: Each month, the employer accrues holiday pay at a rate of 12.50%. This accrued amount is neither paid to FerieKonto by the employer nor is it paid out to the employee by FerieKonto during the employment period.
Upon termination of employment, taxes and the AM contribution are calculated and deducted from the total accrued holiday pay. The resulting net holiday pay is then paid directly to FerieKonto by the employer.
   2. For Hourly-Paid Employees:In Denmark, The employer pays the 12.5% on top of the salary into the fund, separate from the employee’s normal wages, and is NOT deducted from the employee’s gross salary.
Key points:
* Payment in lieu of wages: Employees who are paid by the hour do not receive wages during their vacation. Instead, when they take a vacation, they "order" this accrued money to be paid out to replace their missing 
* wages.
* Taxation: The 12.5% is earned before tax. The employer deducts the tax before transferring the money to FerieKonto. Therefore, the amount displayed in the system is generally the net amount that will be paid to the employee by Feriekonto.
* Upon termination: Since the employer has been contributing 12.5% of the employee's wages throughout the employment, the termination process involves ensuring that all contributions are up-to-date.

8. Gross Salary Scheme 
A gross salary scheme, also known as salary restructuring or a flexible salary package, allows an employee to take a reduction in their cash salary in exchange for receiving one or more non-cash employee benefits from their employer (e.g., telephone, broadband, massage, training, or newspapers).
Key Principles:
* You can agree with your employer to substitute a portion of your salary with a benefit.
* Specific conditions must be satisfied for a gross salary scheme to be valid.
Conditions for Implementation:


For a gross salary scheme to be legally sound, the following conditions must be met:
1. Compliance: The agreement must adhere to all applicable regulations, including any relevant collective agreements that govern employment.
2. Genuine Salary Reduction: The agreement must entail a genuine, future decrease in the employee's cash salary.
3. Duration: The agreed-upon salary adjustment must typically be in effect for the entire salary agreement period, or for a minimum of 12 months as a general guideline.
4. Employer Provision: The employer must provide the benefit directly. The employee cannot purchase the benefit themselves and then be reimbursed for the cost.
5. Predetermined Amount: The reduction in cash salary must be a fixed, predetermined amount. This amount must not be adjusted based on the employee's actual consumption of the benefit or the employer's ongoing costs.
6. Exclusions: These specific rules do not apply to tax-free reimbursements for travel, meals, and lodging.

2. Minor Employee Benefits (Bagatelgrænse)
Certain small, work-related benefits are tax-free if their total value remains below an annual de minimis limit.
* General De Minimis Limit (2025): DKK 7,000 per year (DKK 7,600 in 2026). This covers benefits provided for work purposes, such as coffee, tea, fruit at the office, a company-paid bus pass, or a newspaper. If the total value of these benefits exceeds the limit, the entire amount becomes taxable.
* Christmas Gift Limit (2025): Gifts from an employer are generally taxable. However, a Christmas gift is tax-free if its market value does not exceed DKK 1400. If it exceeds this amount, the entire value is taxable.


3. Work-Related Clothing and Tools
* Uniforms and safety gear supplied by an employer for work purposes are considered non-taxable benefits, provided they are unsuitable for personal use (e.g., display a company logo).


4. Payments related to Health & Safety
Payments from an employer for specific health services are tax-free if they are intended to prevent or treat work-related injuries or illnesses.
* Examples: Flu vaccinations, addiction treatment, or counseling for work-related stress.
* This exemption does not cover general private health insurance premiums, which are taxable as explained above.

3. Key Differences: Savings, Payout, Holiday Pay, & Bonus
It is vital to distinguish between these four terms to ensure correct payroll configuration:
A. Free Choice Allowance Savings (Opsparing)
   * What it is: A balance sheet item (debt to the employee).
   * Timing: Accrues monthly.
   * Tax: Not taxed at this stage.
   * Base: Calculated on the qualifying salary.
B. Free Choice Allowance Payout (Udbetaling)
   * What it is: A payroll transaction (Gross Salary code).
   * Timing: Occurs upon request or fixed intervals (e.g., May/August).

   * Base: It is the result of the savings; it is not a base for other accruals.
C. Holiday Pay (Feriepenge)
   * What it is: The mandatory 12.5% accrual for vacation days (per the Holiday Act).

D. Holiday Bonus (Ferietillæg)
   * What it is: A specific supplement (usually 1%) paid to employees on paid holiday.
   * Difference: This compensates for the fact that you generally don't earn holiday pay while on holiday. It is a fixed statutory/contractual percentage, whereas Free Choice is a flexible negotiated percentage.
4. Technical Calculation: Is it calculated "outside" Payroll?
No, as a payroll software provider, I must clarify that while the balance might be tracked in a separate ledger, the payout and taxation must occur inside the standard payroll run.
Here is the workflow:
   1. Gross Up: The employee requests a payout (e.g., 5,000 DKK).
   2. Entry: You enter 5,000 DKK under the specific pay component for "Fritvalg Payout" in the payroll system.
   3. AM-Bidrag Calculation: The system strictly calculates 8% of the 5,000 DKK (400 DKK) and deducts it.
   4. A-Tax Calculation: The remaining amount (4,600 DKK) is added to the employee's total taxable income for that month. The system applies the employee's tax card (skattekort) rate and deductions (fradrag) to this total.
   5. Reporting: The system reports this to SKAT via eIndkomst.
Why it cannot be "outside":
If you calculated this outside the payroll system, you would fail to report the AM-bidrag and A-Tax correctly to SKAT in real-time. This would lead to non-compliance with the Danish Tax Agency.
Exception:
Sometimes, companies do a "separate payroll run" (an extra run/ ekstra lønkørsel) just for Free Choice payouts so it doesn't get mixed with the monthly salary slip. However, this is still a payroll run subject to standard tax rules, just processed on a different day.
Free Choice Allowance Pension 
What it is: This is when employees choose to direct the money directly into their pension fund.
Purpose: To boost employees retirement savings. Since this money goes straight to the pension provider, employees typically do not pay tax on it immediately (it is taxed upon payout during retirement), making it a tax-efficient way to save if employees don't need the cash now.
Summary: Employees earn the money into the "Savings." They can then choose to "Payout" (cash) or send it "to Pension." employees can also often use it to buy extra days off.
Is it Subject to Tax?
Short Answer: NO (on the payslip).
As a payroll provider, your system must handle the "Right of Disregard" (Bortseelsesret).
   * A-Tax (Income Tax): No.
   * Logic: The amount must be deducted from the gross income before A-tax is calculated. The employee pays 0% income tax on this amount at the time of payroll.
   * AM-Bidrag (Labor Market Contribution): No (on the payslip).
   * Logic: Your system must not deduct the 8% AM-bidrag. You transfer the full amount to the pension provider.
   * Note: The pension provider is legally responsible for deducting the 8% internally before investing the money. If your system deducts it, the employee pays it twice.
   * eIndkomst Reporting: You must map this to Field 148 (Employer-administered pension), not Field 13 (A-Income).
3. Is it Subject to Holiday Pay (Feriepenge)?
Short Answer: NO.
This is a critical trade-off the employee makes, and your system must calculate it correctly.
   * The Rule: In Denmark, employees earn Holiday Pay (12.5%) on their taxable income (Salary). They generally do not earn Holiday Pay on pension contributions.
   * The Payroll Impact:
   * If the employee takes the Free Choice money as CASH, your system MUST calculate 12.5% Holiday Pay on it.
   * If the employee takes the Free Choice money as PENSION, your system MUST NOT calculate Holiday Pay on it.
   * Provider Note: Your system logic needs to be dynamic. The "Holiday Pay Basis" (Ferieberettiget løn) must exclude any amount mapped to the "Free Choice to Pension" wage type.
4. Is it Subject to Holiday Bonus (Ferietillæg)?
Short Answer: NO.
   * The Rule: The Holiday Supplement (usually 1% or higher) is calculated based on the Holiday-Entitled Salary.
   * The Payroll Impact: Since pension contributions are removed from the Holiday-Entitled Salary (as explained above), no bonus is calculated on this specific amount.


G-days (G-dage)
These are compensation days paid by the employer if employees are terminated or sent home (e.g., the first 1-2 days of unemployment).
   * Impact: employees receive a specific daily rate (approx. DKK 973 for full-time insured).

Trade union dues (also referred to as union membership fees), along with related contributions such as unemployment insurance fund payments, early retirement scheme fees, and flexible benefits, are subject to automatic handling in payroll where applicable. These elements are typically deducted from an employee's net pay if the employer agrees to withhold and remit them on behalf of the employee (e.g., via direct payment to the union or fund). However, their primary impact is as a tax deduction, which affects income tax calculations rather than serving as a direct payroll input.
Key Characteristics and Maximum Limits
- Maximum Deduction: For 2025, the maximum tax-deductible amount for trade union dues is DKK 7,000 annually (consistent with 2024 and projected for 2026 based on current SKAT guidelines). This cap applies to regular union fees, additional strike fund contributions imposed by the union, and similar professional association fees that defend economic interests.
- Eligibility: Employees are entitled to this deduction if they pay fees to approved trade unions or professional associations. The deduction is only applicable to income taxable in Denmark and cannot exceed the employee's taxable income.
- Monthly Proration: For payroll purposes, if dues are deducted monthly, the annual maximum is typically prorated (e.g., divided by 12 for monthly pay, resulting in approximately DKK 583.33 per month). However, the actual deduction amount is based on the employee's reported payments, not a fixed proration.
Source of the Amount: Tax Card vs. Manual Input
- Tax Benefit via Tax Card: The tax deduction for trade union dues is not manually calculated or inputted in payroll for tax purposes. Instead, it is reported by the employee (or automatically by the union/association) to the Danish Tax Agency (SKAT). SKAT incorporates it into the employee's preliminary income assessment (forskudsopgørelse), which adjusts the tax card (skattekort). This appears as an increased tax-free allowance (fradrag) on the tax card, effectively reducing the A-tax base without requiring payroll to recalculate or input the deduction separately.
  - Payroll software should automatically retrieve and apply the updated tax card details (e.g., via eIndkomst integration), ensuring the fradrag reflects the union dues deduction.
  - No manual input is needed for the tax effect; attempting to manually adjust would risk double-counting or non-compliance.
- Cash Deduction Amount: If the employer handles the payment of dues (e.g., deducts from salary and remits to the union), the actual dues amount must be manually inputted by the payroll user (e.g., as a fixed amount or percentage per pay period). This is separate from the tax card and is treated as a cash outflow from net pay. If the employee pays directly (outside payroll), no manual input or deduction occurs in payroll.
  - Recommendation for Software Logic: Provide a configurable field for manual input of the dues amount (e.g., "Union Dues Deduction - Cash") with validation against the annual maximum (DKK 7,000). Flag if cumulative year-to-date exceeds the limit, but do not block processing as the tax authority handles final reconciliation.
Pre-Tax vs. Post-Tax Treatment
- Post-Tax Deduction: Trade union dues are treated as post-tax deductions in payroll processing. They do not reduce the base for AM-bidrag (Labour Market Contribution) or other pre-tax elements. Instead:
  - AM-bidrag is calculated first on the full taxable income (A-indkomst).
  - The tax card's adjusted fradrag (incorporating the dues deduction) is then applied to reduce the A-tax base after AM-bidrag.
  - The cash deduction (if applicable) is subtracted from net pay after all taxes and contributions.
- Why Not Pre-Tax?: Unlike pension contributions or other pre-tax deductions (which reduce the taxable base before AM-bidrag), union dues are an expense deduction applied after AM-bidrag. This ensures AM-bidrag is withheld on the full gross earnings, aligning with SKAT rules. Treating it as pre-tax would under-withhold AM-bidrag and lead to compliance issues.
- Bi-Weekly or Other Pay Frequencies: The post-tax behavior is consistent across frequencies. For bi-weekly payroll, prorate the manual cash input (e.g., annual max / 26 periods), but rely on the tax card for tax adjustments, which may use monthly/daily fradrag fields.
Subject to AM-Bidrag, A-Tax, Holiday Pay, and Holiday Bonus?
- Not Subject to AM-Bidrag or A-Tax: Trade union dues are not taxable income; they are an expense. Therefore, they are not subject to AM-bidrag (8%) or A-tax withholding. The dues themselves do not form part of the taxable base—in fact, they provide a tax benefit by reducing A-tax via the fradrag. (Note: An earlier version of this guide incorrectly stated they are "subjected to A-tax and AM-tax"; this refers to the income from which they are deducted, not the dues.)
  - Payroll Logic: Do not include dues in the AM-base or A-tax base calculations. The tax relief is handled implicitly through the tax card.
- Not Subject to Holiday Pay (Feriepenge) or Holiday Bonus: 
  - Holiday pay accrues at 12.5% of qualifying gross earnings (before deductions like union dues). Union dues do not reduce this base, as they are post-tax.
  - Holiday bonus (1% of gross salary) is calculated on the gross salary during the accrual period (September to August), excluding post-tax deductions like union dues.
  - Payroll Logic: Exclude union dues from the bases for holiday pay and bonus accruals. If dues are deducted, they reduce net pay but do not retroactively adjust accruals.
Payroll Processing Step-by-Step
1. Tax Card Side (SKAT/Skattestyrelsen):
   - Employee (or union) reports dues payments to SKAT.
   - SKAT adjusts the tax card: Increases monthly/annual fradrag (tax-free allowance) up to the maximum.
   - Updated tax card is fetched automatically by payroll software.


2. Payroll Calculation Side:
   - Calculate gross salary (taxable pay minus pre-tax deductions).
   - Calculate AM-bidrag base: Round down to nearest 10 DKK on full taxable income (no reduction for union dues).
   - Withhold AM-bidrag (8%, round up to nearest whole DKK).
   - Calculate A-tax base: Taxable income minus AM-bidrag, then apply tax card's fradrag (which includes union dues effect), round down to nearest 10 DKK.
   - Withhold A-tax (tax card rate, round up to nearest whole DKK).
   - If manual cash deduction: Subtract the inputted dues amount from net pay (post-tax).
   - Do not recalculate taxes based on dues; rely on tax card.
3. What Payroll Does Not Do:
   - Reduce AM-bidrag base for dues.
   - Treat dues as a pre-tax deduction for tax purposes.
   - Include dues in bases for holiday pay, holiday bonus, or other accruals.
Simple Example
- Employee Data: Gross salary: DKK 40,000. Union dues: DKK 500 (reported to SKAT; employer deducts cash).
- Payroll Steps:
  - AM-bidrag base: DKK 40,000 (rounded down if needed; no dues reduction) → AM-bidrag: DKK 3,200 (8%, rounded up).
  - A-tax base: DKK 40,000 - DKK 3,200 = DKK 36,800, minus tax card fradrag (e.g., DKK 5,000 including dues effect) = DKK 31,800.
  - A-tax: Apply tax card rate (e.g., 38%) → DKK 12,084 (rounded up).
  - Net pay before dues: Gross - pre-tax deductions - AM - A-tax = e.g., DKK 24,716.
  - Deduct cash dues: DKK 24,716 - DKK 500 = DKK 24,216 (final net pay).
- No Extra Tax on Dues: The DKK 500 is not taxed; the fradrag already reduces A-tax.


Leave Entitlement
Annual Leave (Holiday)
   * Eligibility: All employees are entitled to annual leave under the Danish Holiday Act (Ferieloven).
   * Accrual Methodology: Denmark uses a "concurrent holiday" system. Employees accrue 2.08 days of paid holiday for each month of employment. The accrual year runs from 1 September to 31 August. The accrued leave can be taken in the "holiday-taking period," which runs for 16 months, from 1 September of the accrual year to 31 December of the following year. This allows employees to take leave shortly after it has been earned.
   * For a complete fiscal year the employee is entitled for 25 days.
   * Only 5 days leave is card forward from the previous year.
   * When an employee's unused leave is carried forward to the next fiscal year, the employer must ask the employee if they prefer to take the carried-forward leave within the next six months or receive a payout for it.
   * if the employer does not pay to the FerieKonto at 16 months they have to bear the penalty from the FerieKonto. So the employer has to approach the employee to do the calculation for their accrued leaves.The employer is subject to penalties from FerieKonto if the accrued holiday allowance is not paid within 16 months. Therefore, the employer is responsible for calculating the employee's accrued leave pay(holiday pay)
   * To calculate the salary per day, multiply the gross salary by 4.8%. For each paid holiday day taken, the reduction of the Holiday Pay / Holiday Supplement accrual base is 4.8% of the monthly fixed and foreseeable salary (contractual monthly salary plus fixed contrac[a][b]tual allowances), applied per salary component — not 4.8% of gross salary. Variable pay (overtime, bonus, commission, one-off payments) is not reduced, because none of it is paid for the holiday day. Basis: Ferieloven §19(4) read with §17 and §18; the 4.8% per-day fraction is that prescribed in §15(3).
Sick Leave
   * Eligibility & Payment:
   * Salaried Employees (Funktionærer): Under the Salaried Employees Act, they are entitled to their full salary from the first day of illness.
   * Non-Salaried (Hourly) Employees: Entitlement to pay during sickness depends on the applicable collective agreement. If not covered, the Sickness Benefit Act applies. The employer must pay sickness benefits (not full salary) for the first 30 calendar days of illness, provided the employee has worked for the employer for at least eight weeks and for a minimum of 74 hours in that period.
   * Government Reimbursement: After the initial 30-day employer-paid period, the employee receives sickness benefits from their local municipality (kommune). The employer can also receive reimbursement from the municipality for salary paid beyond the 30-day period, up to the maximum sickness benefit rate.
   * 120-Day Rule: For salaried employees, if contractually agreed, the employer may terminate the employment with a shortened notice period of one month if the employee has received sick pay for 120 days within a 12-month period.

Scaling the Deduction
The Tax Card usually provides a Monthly Deduction (e.g., 4,000 kr.) and a Daily Deduction.
   * Bi-Weekly Conversion: The system must determine the deduction for 14 days.
   * Method 1: Daily Deduction * 14. (Most accurate).
   * Method 2: (Monthly Deduction * 12) / 26. (Common approximation).
   * Use of Daily Deduction: Skattestyrelsen recommends using the Daily Deduction field from the eSkattekort file multiplied by the number of days in the pay period (14). This handles odd periods (e.g., new joiner working 7 days) gracefully.
Mid-Month Changes
If an employee updates their preliminary income assessment (Forskudsopgørelse) mid-month:
   * The employer receives a notification to download a new tax card.
   * Rule: Apply the latest tax card available at the time of the payroll run. Do not retroactively adjust previous periods unless explicitly instructed (Reversal/Correction). The cumulative nature of the tax assessment (handled by the authorities via the card's dynamic adjustment) self-corrects over time.

Maximum threshold & Split Calculation Method for Mid - pay period:


Scenario 1: If the employee earlier has a tax free card and he has exhausted his maximum threshold limit in the mid pay period then we need to use the split calculation method. Below is detailed explanation

1. Purpose of the Frikort
The Frikort represents an annual tax-free allowance (e.g. DKK 54,100 for 2026), primarily for low-income earners. Payroll systems must ensure:
   * No income tax is withheld until the limit is reached
   * Tax is applied immediately to income exceeding the limit
   * Accurate reporting of the used balance to SKAT (eIndkomst)
2. Method of Calculation – Split Logic
Although SKAT guidance focuses on the end result, payroll systems handle this via a split calculation at the point of exhaustion:
   * Calculate A-income after statutory deductions (Pre tax deductions & AM-bidrag at 8%, except under-18s from 2026).
   * Compare current A-income (net taxable income) with the remaining Frikort balance.
   * Rounding Rule: The A-income base must be rounded down to the nearest 10 before comparing it to the Frikort balance.
   * Split the income into:
   * Portion A (within remaining Frikort balance): taxed at 0%
   * Portion B (exceeding the limit): taxed using:
   * the rate from the newly issued Hovedkort/Bikort, if available
   * otherwise, the statutory 55% emergency tax rate
3. Scenario Where No New Card Is Issued
If the Frikort limit is exhausted and SKAT has not yet provided a new tax card, payroll must apply the 55% rate by law on the taxable portion until a valid card is available.
4. Practical Considerations
   * The remaining Frikort balance must be reduced with every payroll run.
   * The exact used amount must be reported back to eIndkomst to stay aligned with SKAT’s tracking.
   * Overpayment of tax-free income may result in employer liability during reconciliation.
5. Summary
In practice, while SKAT may not explicitly describe a “split method,” payroll systems must apply it to ensure:
   * Compliance with tax law
   * Correct handling of mid-period threshold exhaustion
   * Immediate taxation of income exceeding the Frikort limit

Non-Resident and Foreign Employees - Special Income / Gross Tax Card
   * Foreign employees in Denmark are subject to the Danish tax system and must have a tax card.
   * Their tax calculation process is the same as for Danish citizens.
   * Favorable tax schemes, such as the Expat Tax Scheme, offer a flat rate of 27% no tax free deductions
   * Qualification for such a scheme will be indicated on the tax card issued by SKAT (the Danish tax agency).
   * Employers must apply the tax rates specified on the tax card.
Effective January 1, 2026, the minimum gross monthly salary requirement for the 27% flat expat tax scheme is being lowered
   * Current Requirement (2025): DKK 78,000 per month.
   * New Requirement (Effective Jan 1, 2026): DKK 65,400 per month.


Section 17 Benefits:
1. Purpose
The purpose of Section 17 (§17) tax card variants is to facilitate debt recovery and specific benefit withholding. Under Section 17 of the Danish Recovery Order (Inddrivelsesbekendtgørelsen), the Danish Tax Agency (Skat) can issue "Reserved Withholding Percentages."
   * It ensures that individuals receiving public benefits (who also owe public debt) have a specific, often higher, amount of tax withheld to settle those debts directly at the source.
2. Applicability (Public vs. Private Sector)
   * Public Sector: Primary User. These cards are almost exclusively used by public authorities and benefit payers (e.g., Udbetaling Danmark, SU Agency, Municipalities).
   * Private Sector: Rarely Applicable. Private employers do not typically receive §17 cards for standard wages. If a private employee owes debt, the authorities use Wage Attachment (Lønindeholdelse), which is a post-tax deduction, rather than a §17 tax card.
3. Requirement for Payroll Vendors
   * Do you need new logic? No. You do not need to build a "Section 17 engine." The mathematical calculation for a §17 card is identical to a standard card: (Taxable Income - Allowance) * Percentage.
   * Do you need to support it? Yes. As a vendor, your software must be able to import and recognize these card types from the e-Indkomst (eSkattekort) file so the system doesn't reject the data or crash when a user (e.g., a public sector client) processes a payroll.
4. Impact on Regulation
The regulation changes the Data Validation rules rather than the tax laws themselves:
   * Primary Rule: Any card labeled "Main" (§17 or Standard) must allow the tax-free deduction ($Fradrag$).
   * Secondary Rule: Any card labeled "Secondary" or "Bee" (§17 or Standard) must ignore the deduction.
   * Withholding Permit: Regulation dictates that only "authorized benefit payers" should use these cards. As a vendor, you simply provide the capability to use the data Skat provides.
5. Impact on Integration
The impact is strictly on the Mapping Layer of your Inbound Integration:
   * Mapping: Your integration must map Main card (§17) to your internal "Primary Card" logic and Secondary card (§17) to your "Secondary Card" logic.
   * Fields: You must ensure the Withholding Percentage and Deduction Amount fields are correctly pulled from the §17 segments of the XML/Data file provided by Skat.
Summary:
Feature
	Details
	Legal Basis
	Section 17 of the Danish Recovery Order (Debt Collection).
	Target
	Public benefits (SU, Unemployment, Pensions).
	Vendor Action
	Map the names in the integration; no new calculation code required.
	Key Logic
	§17 Main = Deduction Allowed; §17 Secondary = No Deduction.

   * Part-time, Casual, and Daily Employees: These employees are taxed similarly to full-time employees, with tax calculated on their earnings based on their tax card. Scenario 2: Special Scenarios - Errors, Backpay, and Retrospective Pay
Core Principle: Income in Denmark is taxed in the period it is paid, not when it was earned.

Overtime (March worked, April paid)

   * Overtime pay is included in April’s gross salary.
   * Tax is calculated on April’s total income (salary + overtime).

Underpayment (March missed, corrected in April)

      * The DKK 500 underpayment is added to April’s salary.
      * April’s tax is calculated on the combined amount (April salary + DKK 500).

Overpayment (August paid extra, corrected in September)

         * Common approach: Net overpayment from August is deducted from September’s net pay.
         * Gross-level correction is possible but more complex and rarely used.


Retrospective Increase (effective Dec 2024, paid in April 2025)

            * Scenario: Salary increase of DKK 500/month effective Dec 1, 2024.
            * April 2025 payroll includes:
            * New April salary: DKK 45,500
            * Back-pay lump sum (Dec–Mar): DKK 500 × 4 months = DKK 2,000
            * Total April gross pay = DKK 47,500
            * Tax is calculated on the full April gross (same treatment as a lump-sum/bonus).




Voluntary Increase of the Withholding Percentage (Frivillig forhøjelse af trækprocent) [Added v2.3.6]
An employee may ask the employer to withhold A-tax (A-skat) at a higher withholding percentage (trækprocent) than the one stated on the electronic tax card (eSkattekort). Where the employer agrees, the extra amount is ordinary provisional A-tax under Kildeskatteloven (Withholding Tax Act) §46 and §48, and is governed by Kildeskattebekendtgørelsen (Executive Order on Withholding Tax, BEK no. 839 of 24 June 2025) §24, as implemented in SKAT's eIndkomst guidance “Vejledning om eSkattekort” §5.7.


Statutory conditions (kildeskattebekendtgørelsen §24; SKAT §5.7): 
(1) the request must be in writing; 
(2) it may be made once per calendar year; 
(3) the increase must be a whole number of percentage points and the resulting withholding percentage must be a whole number; 
(4) it applies prospectively only (no retroactive effect) and lapses at the end of the calendar year. 
The employer is not obliged to comply, and not all payroll systems support it.
Why voluntary tax is a percentage, not a fixed amount
The only voluntary-tax mechanism that runs through the employer — and therefore into eIndkomst and the employer's A-skat remittance — is an increase of the withholding percentage. Kildeskattebekendtgørelsen §24 defines it strictly as withholding “at a higher percentage than stated on the tax card”, increased “by a whole number”; SKAT §5.7 repeats this. The Danish state payroll system (SLS) implements it the same way (wage element “Forhøjet trækprocent”), adding the requested percentage to the card rate. There is no provision for the employer to withhold a fixed kroner amount of voluntary A-tax.


A fixed amount does exist, but as a different route that is not a payroll wage element: the employee's own voluntary payment (frivillig indbetaling) made directly to the Tax Account (Skattekonto) via TastSelv/E-tax. That is employee-initiated, paid to SKAT directly, not withheld by the employer, not reported in eIndkomst, and outside this catalogue. Consequently, a request for a “flat voluntary tax amount” as a reportable payroll element has no statutory basis and should be redirected. Because §24 applies a percentage to a base that varies each period, an exact fixed kroner figure cannot be guaranteed through payroll.
Official sources: kildeskattebekendtgørelsen §24 — https://www.elov.dk/kildeskattebekendtgorelsen/paragraf/24/ ; SKAT eIndkomst “Vejledning om eSkattekort” §5.7 — https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=1745935 ; Kildeskatteloven §46/§48 — https://danskelove.dk/kildeskatteloven/46 and /48.
Payroll mechanics and report impact
Effective withholding percentage = tax-card percentage + whole-number voluntary uplift (resulting percentage a whole number). AM-bidrag (8%) is unaffected — it is computed on the AM-base independently of the withholding percentage. The extra A-tax is reported aggregated in eIndkomst Field 0015 (Withheld A-Tax); there is no separate field or income type for voluntary tax. Field 0013 (A-income) and Field 0016 (AM-bidrag) do not change. Two implementation modalities: (A) rate override — base A-tax computed at the uplifted rate, no separate element; or (B) base A-tax at the card rate plus a separate “Voluntary Additional A-Tax” element. Under either modality Field 0015 must not double count the uplift.

1.2 Who is Covered
The Holiday Act covers all employees who receive wages for work performed under an employer. It does NOT cover:
            * Freelancers and self-employed persons
            * Directors/board members not classified as employees under the Salaried Employees Act
            * Employees working in the Faroe Islands or Greenland (separate legislation applies)
            * State employees (covered under separate civil service rules)


Special Terms for CEO / CFO and Senior Management Employees
Individuals who have a decisive influence over the company (such as registered CEOs, CFOs, owner-managers, and equivalent senior executives) are subject to special terms that differ from standard employees covered by the Danish Holiday Act (Ferieloven) and the Salaried Employees Act (Funktionærloven). The following provisions apply to this category:
1. Who is Covered by These Special Terms
•  Registered CEOs and CFOs who exercise decisive managerial authority over the company
•  Owner-managers with controlling ownership interest in the company
•  Other senior executives contractually excluded from the Salaried Employees Act and Holiday Act
2. Holiday Pay Provisions
Not Holiday Entitled: The salary of CEO / CFO employees is not holiday-entitled. Their remuneration does not qualify as the basis for holiday pay accrual under Ferieloven.
No Holiday Day Accrual: The standard accrual of 2.08 days per month does not apply. No holiday days (paid or unpaid under Ferieloven) are accrued in the payroll system for these employees.
No Holiday Supplement (Ferietillæg): The 1% holiday supplement (ferietillæg) payable to salaried employees in May and August does not apply to CEO / CFO employees. Their salary is not a qualifying earnings basis for this supplement.
3. Great Prayer Day (Storebededag) Compensation
CEO / CFO employees are not entitled to the Great Prayer Day (GPD) compensation. The statutory GPD compensation (equivalent to approximately 0.45% of annual salary, representing one extra day’s pay) is payable only to employees covered by Ferieloven and applicable collective agreements. Since CEO / CFO employees fall outside this scope, no GPD compensation is calculated or paid. The GPD-related fields in payroll must be set to zero / not applicable for this employee category.
4. Free Choice Earnings and Similar Benefits
CEO / CFO employees are not entitled to free choice earnings (fritvalgsbidrag / fritvalgsopsparing) or similar collective agreement-based benefit contributions. These benefits arise from collective bargaining agreements (overenskomster) to which CEO / CFO employees are not party. No free choice contributions, special bonuses linked to collective agreements, or equivalent benefit accruals are to be set up in payroll for this category.
Summary: Payroll Configuration for CEO / CFO Employees
•  Holiday day accrual (2.08 days/month): NOT applicable
•  Holiday pay 12.5% accrual / FerieKonto transfer: NOT applicable
•  Holiday supplement (Ferietillæg) 1%: NOT applicable
•  Great Prayer Day (GPD) compensation: NOT applicable
•  •  Free choice earnings / fritvalgsbidrag: NOT applicable


NOTE: The Act applies regardless of nationality. All foreign nationals employed in Denmark on standard contracts are subject to Ferieloven.

3. Accrual Calculation Logic
3.1 Standard Accrual - Full Month of Employment
For any complete calendar month of employment, regardless of full-time or part-time status, seniority, or type of work:


Formula:  
Days Accrued = Number of Full Months in a Holiday Year × 2.08
Maximum per holiday year: 25 days (12 × 2.08 = 24.96, rounded to 25)


Holiday Pay Accrued = Monthly Qualifying Taxable Earnings × 12.5% 
Holiday Pay Supplement Accrued (only for salaried employee)


The amount-based system tracks the FINANCIAL LIABILITY; the day balance tracks the LEGAL ENTITLEMENT. 
	

Accrual continues during the following absence types - holiday days still earned:
            * Weekends and public holidays
            * Sick days (with full or partial pay)
            * Days off / holiday
            * Maternity, paternity, and adoption leave (if employer pays full salary)
Accrual during unpaid sickness (sygeferiegodtgørelse, §5(3) and §20): if an employee is absent due to sickness without a right to full pay, holiday still accrues as sygeferiegodtgørelse from the 2nd day of each period of sickness absence. The rate is 12.5% of the employee’s usual pay in the 4 weeks before the absence; where the employer pays partial pay, it is the difference between that 12.5% and the 12.5% already applied to the reduced pay. In very long-term sickness (more than 52 sick-absence periods with the same employer in the holiday year), accrual runs from the first sick day so that at least 4 weeks accrue (§5(4)).


3.2 Partial Month Accrual (Mid-Month Joiner or Leaver)
When employment begins or ends part-way through a calendar month:


Formula:  
Days Accrued in a month = Number of Calendar Days Employed in a month × 0.07 per day


Amount Accrued = Prorated Monthly Qualifying Taxable Earnings × 12.5% 


	

NOTE: The 0.07/day rate applies only to any employment period of less than one complete calendar month. A full month always uses 2.08. For a mid-month joiner, the first partial month uses 0.07/day; all subsequent full months use 2.08.

8. 5th Week / Excess Holiday (Overskydende Ferie)
8.1 Entitlement Threshold
Every employee accrues all 25 days — including the 5th week (days 21–25) — at 2.08 days per month (§5); there is no minimum-months threshold to be entitled to, or to take, the 5th week. The 9½-month rule is different: it is a condition for paying the 5th week out in cash to an actively employed employee (this protects the EU-guaranteed first 4 weeks), not a condition for accruing or taking it. The days need not have been accrued with the same employer.
Holiday in advance (forskudsferie, §7): the employer and employee may agree that the employee takes paid holiday before it has been earned. On termination, any advance holiday taken but not yet earned is offset against the employee’s final pay; a shortfall that the final pay cannot cover is not otherwise reclaimable.

8.8 Sickness Before or During Holiday — Replacement Holiday (Erstatningsferie)
Sick before the holiday begins: an employee who is sick when the holiday is due to start is not obliged to begin it (§12); they report sick under the normal rule and may take the holiday later.
Sick during the holiday: an employee who falls sick during holiday has a right to replacement holiday (erstatningsferie), but only after 5 waiting days (karensdage) of sickness during holiday per holiday year (§12). If fewer than 25 days have been accrued, the waiting period is reduced proportionally (accrued days × 1/5). The employee must report sick on the first day and provide a medical certificate at their own cost.
This applies to both salaried and hourly employees. It does not create new money — it moves when the holiday is taken: for salaried employees it is an internal balance adjustment; for hourly employees the affected days are simply re-drawn from FerieKonto.
9. Termination: Step-by-Step Holiday Pay Calculation
9.1 Overview
On termination (dismissal or resignation), the salaried (full-time) employee's holiday model switches from "salary continuation" to the 12.5% calculation model for any untaken days. All untaken holiday accrued in the current holiday year must be reported to and transferred to FerieKonto.
Garden leave (fritstilling): if the employee is released from duties during the notice period, holiday is automatically treated as held during the fritstilling period, to the maximum the notice rules allow — provided the period is long enough to contain both the required notice (3 months for main holiday, 1 month for remaining holiday) and the holiday itself, and the days are accrued. Only holiday that cannot be accommodated in the period is settled as holiday pay at termination.

9.6 Death of an Employee
On the death of an employee, accrued but untaken holiday pay is settled to the estate (dødsbo), not paid to the employee (§34 b, stk. 6: the feriegodtgørelse is paid to the estate).
The employer calculates the holiday pay as for a termination (12.5% of qualifying earnings for each open holiday year), withholds AM-bidrag and A-tax using the deceased employee’s tax card (bikort), and reports it. For hourly employees the amount already held in FerieKonto is released to the estate; for salaried employees the employer settles the untaken holiday to the estate. This applies to both employee categories.
