Module 1 — Country Overview
Iraq operates two separate payroll regimes. Federal Iraq applies the federal Income Tax Law and the 2023 social-security law; the Kurdistan Region (KRI) applies its own income-tax rules and a separate, legacy social-security framework. This guide details each regime and compares them where they differ.
1.1 Jurisdictions, currency and language
* Currency: Iraqi Dinar (IQD). Wages are paid in IQD; foreign-currency salaries are converted to IQD at the payment date for reporting and statutory calculation.
* Language: Arabic for statutory documents in Federal Iraq; Arabic and Kurdish in the KRI. Bilingual (Arabic + English) payslips are common practice but the Arabic version is authoritative.
* Tax year: the Gregorian calendar year (1 January – 31 December) for payroll withholding in both regions.
1.2 Pay frequency, pay dates and the working week
Monthly pay is the prevailing private-sector practice; weekly pay is also permitted. Friday is the statutory weekly rest day (at least 24 consecutive hours at full pay, Art. 70); a Friday–Saturday weekend is common private-sector practice. Where a pay date falls on the weekend or a public holiday, payment is brought forward to the preceding working day. Statutory reporting is monthly regardless of pay frequency.
Typical cadence: cut-off 20th–25th, calculation 25th–28th, sign-off 27th–29th, net pay on the last working day (or the first working day of the next month).

* Individual components are computed at full precision.
* Final income tax, social-security contributions and net pay are rounded to the nearest whole Iraqi dinar (the fils is obsolete).
* Statutory returns are filed in whole dinars.
1.6 Employee master data for payroll
The minimum payroll attributes are:
* Region — Federal Iraq or KRI (drives the entire tax and SS treatment).
* Residency — Resident or Non-resident only. A person shall be considered as a ‘resident’ if the period of their stay in Iraq is for a continuous duration of four months, or for a duration of six months scattered during the fiscal year. 
* Nationality flag — Iraqi or foreign. This is separate from residency and drives only the Federal SS treatment (the state 8%).
* Marital status — single; married with a non-earning/income-combined spouse; married with a working spouse; widow/divorcee. This drives the personal allowance (see 3.1).
* Children and age — qualifying-children count and whether t  inputs).
Contract type does not change tax or social-security rates; it is a labour-law attribute only.

Module 2 — Social Security Contributions
Federal Iraq and the KRI run separate social-security systems. Rates are similar (employee 5% / employer 12%) but the ceiling and the foreign-worker treatment differ materially.
2.1 Federal Iraq
Governing law: Workers’ Retirement and Social Security Law No. 18 of 2023 (in force in Federal Iraq since December 2023). Administered by the Workers’ Retirement and Social Security Department via the Daman platform.

Contribution base, floor and ceiling
* Base: the actual wage, allowances and benefits of the contribution month — basic salary; fixed and recurring allowances (housing, transport, meal, mobile, etc.); recurring salary supplements; leave pay; bonuses; and benefits in kind (taxable medical/medical care, in-kind housing/food, and equity/stock at FMV) — clamped to the floor and 5x ceiling (wage and allowances of every kind, not basic pay alone; Art. 15(First)/(Second), Art. 1(11)). Excluded from the base: variable, hours-based pay (overtime, shift/night premium); genuine expense reimbursements (relocation, travel, per diem); exempt items (end-of-service/severance, employer medical-insurance premium, death/injury compensation); salary advances; and contractor payments.
* Base period (registration). The contribution wage is the actual wage and allowances (al-ajr wa-l-mukhassasat) the worker receives in the contribution month, clamped to the floor and the 5x minimum-wage ceiling (Law 18/2023, Art. 15(First)); for a worker joining an employer for the first time it is the full monthly wage actually paid (Art. 15(Second)). Law 18/2023 does not fix the Federal contribution wage annually - the January-registered-wage method is the Law 39/1971 (KRI/legacy) basis and does not apply in Federal Iraq. The consolidated contribution is remitted through the Daman platform in the following month (Art. 16). 
* Floor: the minimum wage (IQD 350,000).
* Ceiling: 5× the minimum wage = IQD 1,750,000/month. Pay above this is not contributory.
Step-by-step (Federal)
* Consider the registered social security contribution base.
* Apply the floor, then cap at the 5×MW ceiling → insurable base.
* Employee 5%, employer 12%, state 8% (employer-borne for foreign workers).[a]
* Withhold the 5% from pay; remit the consolidated contribution (17%/25%) by the end of the following month.
Joiners, leavers and special cases
* Joining month: full-month contribution (Art.15(Second)/(Third)); register the worker on Daman within 30 days (Art.93).
* Leaving month: contributed on the part of the month worked (pro-rated) - contributions are due 'for the part of the month in which the service ends' (wa-tastahiqqu 'an juz' al-shahr alladhi tantahi fihi al-khidma) (Law 18/2023, Art. 15(Third)(a)). The joining month is contributed in full (Art. 15(Second)/(Third)(a)).
* Suspended contracts (illness, maternity, arrest): the employer continues full contributions [b][c](Art.18).
Note on Expatriate Registration Fees: A mandatory one-time registration fee of 2,000,000 IQD applies to any new foreign worker registered with the Pension and Social Security Organization (PSSO). This is kept out of payroll scope as it is a one-time government levy imposed strictly on the employing entity. It should be processed as an HR onboarding cost, global mobility expense, or legal compliance overhead.
2.2 Kurdistan Region (KRI)
Governing law: Social security in the KRI is governed by the Pension and Social Insurance Act No. 39 of 1971 (with Amendment No. 4 of 2012); the Federal Social Security Law No. 18 of 2023 has not been adopted in the Region and does not apply there. The scheme is administered by the KRG Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs (MoLSA), through its Department of Social Security, and contributions are paid into the regional Social Security Fund.

Module 3 — Monthly Income Tax
Federal Iraq applies a progressive tax with personal allowances on a discrete monthly method — each month is taxed independently on monthly-equivalent brackets and allowances (the annual Art. 13 brackets and Art. 12 allowances ÷ 12), with no in-year cumulative tracking or annualised projection, and an annual reconciliation on Form ض.د/4أ (D-4A). The KRI applies a flat 5% over a monthly threshold with no personal allowances.
3.1 Federal Iraq
Governing law: Income Tax Law No. 113 of 1982 (as amended; CPA Order 49/2004 rates). Withholding (PAYE/direct deduction) by the employer to the GCT – Direct Deduction Division.
(a) What goes into gross taxable pay
Taxable employment income (Art.2(5)) is salaries, wages, bonuses, allowances and benefits-in-kind — including the cash or estimated value of housing, food and accommodation. There is no separate fringe-benefits tax; benefits-in-kind are valued at fair market value and taxed through payroll.[f]
(b) Exemptions relevant to payroll (Art.7)
* End-of-service indemnity (Art.7(6)(c)) up to statutory limit (Module 5)
* Lump-sum compensation to the family of a deceased employee, and injury/death compensation (Art.7(9)).
* Eligible-allowance exemption — the eligible-allowance basket (housing, accommodation, transport, food, clothing and risk/hazard allowances) is exempt from income tax up to an aggregate 30% of basic salary (the lower of the basket total and 30% of basic); the balance is taxable. Income-tax only — the allowances remain fully sociable in the social-security base. Basis: GCT Direct Deduction Instruction No. 1 of 2007, Art. 6(Thirteenth); confirmed by PwC.
* Geographical-allowance exemption (resident expatriates) — where a resident expatriate (meeting the residency test) is paid a contractual geographical allowance, that allowance is exempt from income tax up to 25% of basic salary, additive to the 30% eligible-allowance basket. Federal Iraq only; income-tax only — the geographical allowance remains fully sociable in the social-security base. Basis: Federal PIT treatment for qualifying resident expatriates, confirmed by PwC.
(UN salaries, foreign-diplomatic and other special exemptions are out of payroll scope.)
(c) Allowed deductions (before tax)
* Employee social-security contribution — fully deductible (Art.8(7)). Voluntary scheme contributions are likewise deductible, though rarely relevant for standard employees.
* Life-insurance premiums up to IQD 5,000,000/year and other insurance up to IQD 3,000,000/year (Art.8(10), as raised by Law 48/2015);cArt. 8(10)). Because each calendar month is computed as a standalone period with no in-year cumulative tracking or salary projection, annual insurance premium reliefs are applied through fixed monthly limits and normalised in the year-end.
The statutory annual limits — life-insurance premiums IQD 5,000,000 and other insurance IQD 3,000,000, as raised by Law 48/2015 — are divided by 12 to set fixed monthly deduction ceilings. Each month the payroll engine deducts the actual premium paid for that month, up to the relevant monthly ceiling, from taxable income before the progressive monthly bands are applied.

#Working spouse and clubbing of couple’s income: 
1. A spouse who earns is an independent taxpayer with their own 2,500,000 allowance — the 4,500,000 married allowance applies only when the spouse is non-earning. Two working spouses therefore get 2,500,000 each, not a shared 4,500,000. 
2. Couples may elect to combine incomes in the husband’s name. Where the couple elects to merge (club) a working wife's income with the husband's, and election is operated through the monthly payroll, the husband's employer applies the married allowance of IQD 4,500,000/year (IQD 375,000/month) in the monthly direct-deduction computation through his primary employer, while the wife's primary employer withholds at a nil personal allowance on the merged income (she does not separately claim her own IQD 2,500,000). Where the couple does not merge, each spouse is an independent taxpayer and each payroll applies the single allowance of IQD 2,500,000 (IQD 208,333/month). The annual Form ض.د/4أ (D-4A) reconciliation trues up the combined position, since neither employer sees both incomes in-year. 
Declaration and proof. The allowance the employer applies is driven by the employee's personal-status declaration and is reconciled on the annual Form ض.د/4أ (D-4A). The employer captures and evidences: 
* marital status
* the spouse's employment and income status (a working spouse is an independent taxpayer, so the 4,500,000 married allowance is not given)
* the count, dates of birth of qualifying children and legal guardianship in case of divorced couple
* for concurrent employment, the employee declares which employer is primary: only the primary employer applies the personal allowances and deduction for insurance premiums, the secondary withholds at a zero allowance, and the position is reconciled on the annual return (ITL Art. 12; GCT Direct Deduction Instruction No. 1 of 2007). No relief is allowed on secondary work other than retirement deductions and social-security contributions. 
(e) Mid-year change of personal circumstances (Art.12(7))
This is settled by primary law and is proportional within the same tax year from the next month — never retrospective to January and never deferred to next year:
* Marriage or a child’s birth: the spouse/child allowance is added for the complete months remaining in the year from the event.
* Separation or a child’s death or a child ceasing to qualify: the allowance is reduced by the complete months remaining.
* The taxpayer’s own allowance is unaffected — only the spouse/child portion moves.

(g) Monthly PAYE — discrete monthly method (each month standalone)
Federal withholding uses the discrete monthly method prescribed by the GCT Direct Deduction Instruction No. 1 of 2007 (made under Income Tax Law Arts. 60–61 — the “manner prescribed” under Art. 17(1)): each month is a standalone period. The annual Art. 13 brackets and the Art. 12 personal allowances are each divided by 12 into monthly equivalents, and the month’s tax is computed on that month’s taxable income. No in-year cumulative tracking or annualised projection is used; the annual return (Form ض.د/4أ / D-4A) reconciles the year[h][i].
Each pay run, the employer:
1. Builds the month's taxable income = month's basic salary + taxable allowances and benefits-in-kind − the employee social-security contribution (Art. 8(7)) − the monthly personal allowance.
2. Applies the monthly tax bands with cumulative addition: monthly tax = (monthly taxable − the lower bound of its band) × the band rate + the cumulative tax carried from the lower bands.
3. Carries intermediate values at two decimals and rounds only the final monthly tax to the whole dinar.
Because each month stands alone, an employee on a constant wage already bears one-twelfth of the annual liability each month and needs no true-up. Where pay varies, a personal-allowance declaration changes mid-year, or the worker joins or leaves mid-year, any residual difference is settled on the annual reconciliation return (Form ض.د/4أ / D-4A, due 31 March); the within-year / year-end adjustment authority is Art. 17(2). Annual reconciliation happens in the month of resident employee’s separation or in the last month of year (December).
A one-off bonus is taxed in the month it is paid: it is added to that month’s taxable income and the monthly bands are applied, so the bonus bears its own marginal monthly tax in that month (the income arises then — Art. 3(2)). It is not multiplied across months or spread forward. 
(h) Joiners, leavers and rounding
* The employer runs a standalone monthly calculation for each calendar month worked (including partial months without day-proportion of monthly tax slabs, allowances or other monthly limits).
* Mid-month joiner/leaver: the month’s salary is pro-rated on a 30-day month (days worked ÷ 30; Labour Law Art. 15). The monthly personal allowance and the monthly tax bands are applied in full to the partial month — they are not day-pro-rated.
* Rounding: Intermediate values to two decimals; the final monthly and annual tax figures to the nearest whole dinar.
There is no statutory previous-employment-income declaration in Iraqi payroll; each employer applies allowances to the pay it makes. Multiple employments, if reconciled at all, are reconciled via the employee’s own annual return.
(i) Year-end adjustment (Form ض.د/4أ)
At the close of the fiscal year or at the time of separation, the employer reconciles the tax withheld month by month against the tax due on the employee’s total income for the period of employment, and settles the difference on the second page of Form ض.د/4أ by 31 March (GCT Direct Deduction Instructions No. 1 of 2007, Arts. 10(Fifth), 11(First)(b); ITL Art. 17(2)). 
The computation is as follows.
Step 1 — Annual taxable income (ATI)
ATI = Σ monthly taxable income over the active months, where each month's taxable income is the (day-adjusted, 30-day month) gross plus taxable allowances and benefits-in-kind, less the exempt incomes/portion (e.g. the 30% allowance exemption) and less the deductions (employee social-security/pension, insurance premiums up to the monthly ceiling).
Step 2 — Annual legal allowance allowed (LA)
Resident: 
LA = the annual legal allowance for the employee's status (e.g. IQD 2,500,000 single; 4,500,000 married/clubbed; 5,000,000 married-woman sole earner; 3,200,000 widow/divorced woman plus IQD 200,000 per qualifying child and IQD 300,000 if aged 63+) — granted proportionate to the months of employment. 
Step 3 — Annual tax due (ATD). Apply the annual progressive scale - proportionate to the months of employment - to the annual tax base B = max(ATI − LA, 0). Annual tax slab application in case of 12 months’ worked:
* B ≤ 250,000 → ATD = B × 3%
* 250,000 < B ≤ 500,000 → ATD = 7,500 + (B − 250,000) × 5%
* 500,000 < B ≤ 1,000,000 → ATD = 20,000 + (B − 500,000) × 10%
* B > 1,000,000 → ATD = 70,000 + (B − 1,000,000) × 15%
Step 4 — Tax withheld (TW). TW = Σ monthly tax withheld over the active months (the sum of the discrete monthly computations actually deducted during the year).
Step 5 — Adjustment (ADJ). ADJ = TW − ATD.
* ADJ > 0 → over-withholding: refund due to the employee, refunded by the GCT 
* ADJ < 0 → under-withholding: additional tax payable, remitted by the employer.
Because each month stands alone with no in-year cumulative tracking, the employee is withheld with allowance and bands for the active/working months only; the unworked months carry no allocation. The employer settles in-year over/under-withholding on the second page of Form ض.د/4أ where annual tax slabs, annual allowances and other annual limits (like insurance deduction) are pro-rated for the number of months worked with the current employer. 
3.2 Kurdistan Region (KRI)
Governing law: KRG Income Tax Law No. 26 of 2007 and KRG Regulation No. 2 of 2017.
* Flat 5% on monthly income above IQD 1,000,000; the first 1,000,000/month is effectively tax-free.
* Employee social-security contribution is fully deductible.
* Fixed and recurring allowances (housing, transport, utilities) are exempted up to 30% of basic wage before the flat 5% calculation occurs.
* No progressive bands and no personal allowances (marital status / children do not reduce KRI tax).
* Filing is quarterly, by the 21st of the month following the quarter.
The KRI 5% applies to net income (gross less the employee social-security contribution), per the KRG General Directorate of Taxation; following the Federal model in practice (no KRG statutory cap), allowances are treated as exempt up to 30% of basic salary, with amounts above that added to taxable pay (market practice).

Federal Iraq and the Kurdistan Region run separate income-tax and social-security systems. How a moving employee is handled depends on (i) whether the move crosses the Federal/KRI boundary and (ii) whether it crosses a tax/social-security registration.

A Federal→Federal or KRI→KRI relocation does not change the tax method or the social-security scheme: income tax stays national to the region and contributions stay with the same fund. Only location-driven pay elements — hardship, transport, site allowances — change, and they feed the taxable and SS bases under the normal rules.
2. Move across the Federal/KRI boundary
The applicable tax method and social-security scheme change from the effective date of the move, because the two regions are separate jurisdictions.
* Income tax — earnings for the months worked in each region are taxed under that region's method: Federal progressive/discrete-monthly for the Federal period; KRI flat-5%-on-net (over the monthly exemption) for the KRI period. The single Federal annual personal allowance applies only to the Federal-source months.
* Social security — the worker is covered by the scheme of the region where the work is performed: deregistered from the source fund and registered with the destination fund as of the move, with each month's contribution going to the fund of the region worked..
3. Same organisation, different branches/locations
Entity-level determination: Whethe[j]r an entity files under the Federal regime, the KRI regime or both follows the registrations it holds and the region in which the work is performed. A Federal filer holds a GCT tax file number; a KRI filer holds a KRG General Directorate of Taxation registration and, normally, a KRG company licence. An entity may hold both, in which case each registration files for its own period and each worker follows the region of the work location. The payroll attributes that carry this are Tax Regime (Entity), the KRG registration identifiers and Work Location Governorate (see the Data Dictionary).
* One legal entity, single tax & SS registration (same IDs), both sites in the same region — treat as one continuous employment on one payroll registration; no split, no re-registration.
* One entity, separate registrations (e.g., a Federal entity with a separately-registered KRI branch — different tax/SS IDs) — treat the move as a transfer between registrations: close the period on the source registration and open it on the destination; each registration files for its own period. This is the expected position for an organisation operating across the Federal/KRI boundary, since the KRI administers its own tax and social security.
* Different legal entities (intra-group transfer) — treat as a leaver at the source entity (end-of-service settlement, deregistration) and a joiner at the destination (fresh registration; Federal full-month SS on joining).

Module 5 — Gratuity / End-of-Service
End-of-service entitlements arise from the labour law of the relevant region and the social-security framework. The lump-sum indemnity is treated as tax-exempt (Art.7(6)(c)).
Federal Iraq
Entitlement
End-of-service indemnity is governed by Labour Law No. 37 of 2015. It is payable on termination by the employer (including redundancy/economic dismissal, Art. 43-2-d), on the employee’s resignation (Art. 43-1-h), on retirement (Art. 43-2-c), on expiry of a fixed term, and to the family on death in service. It is not payable where service ends for serious fault — for example a final custodial sentence exceeding one year (Art. 43-1-b), breach of an essential contractual obligation (Art. 43-2-e), or failure during probation (Art. 43-2-g).
Amount. 2 weeks’ wage for each year of service, with any fraction of a year paid pro-rata. On the statutory 30-day month (Art. 15), two weeks = 14/30 of one month’s wage, so each completed year accrues about 0.467 of a monthly wage.
Wage basis. The indemnity (and any leave paid out) is computed on the wage of the last six months of service, excluding transport, food and risk allowances (Art. 76-2).
Death in service. If the worker had at least one year’s service, the family receives two months’ wage (Art. 43-1-a).
Unfair / unlawful dismissal. Compensation is double the end-of-service entitlement (Art. 47-2).
Tax treatment. The end-of-service indemnity is exempt from income tax to the extent of the statutory entitlement (ITL Art. 7(6)(c)); any ex-gratia or enhanced severance above the statutory formula is ordinary taxable income through payroll, as is a payout of untaken annual leave on termination.
Social-security interaction. Where the worker qualifies for a retirement/old-age pension or end-of-service bonus under the Social Security Law No. 18 of 2023, that benefit arises from the fund and is separate from the employer’s Labour-Law indemnity.[m][n][o]
KRI
End-of-service entitlements arise under Law No. 71 of 1987 (Labour Law 37/2015 is not adopted in the KRI) and the KRI social-security framework (Law 39/1971 + Amendment 4/2012); they are administered and disbursed through the regional Social Security Fund  where the worker is registered, with a direct employer-funded severance only where the worker was not registered [p][q].

Module 6 — Labour-Law Module (payroll-relevant)
The applicable labour statute differs by region: Federal Iraq applies Labour Law No. 37 of 2015; the KRI applies the 1987 Labour Code (Law No. 71 of 1987). Only payroll-relevant points are summarised; where the KRI differs, it is noted.
6.1 Leave
* Annual leave: Federal — broadly 21 days/year (more for hazardous work); KRI — 20 days/year. Untaken leave is generally paid out on termination.
* Sick leave: employer-paid for an initial period, then social-security-supported; the KRI provides for extended sick leave up to ~180 days.
* Maternity and other statutory leave per the applicable regional labour law and social-security provisions.
Annual leave (Federal). 21 days’ paid leave a year, rising by 2 days for every 5 years’ service; 30 days for arduous/hazardous work; pro-rated for any fraction of a year; leave may not be waived for cash (Art. 75). The leave wage follows the last-six-months basis, excluding transport, food and risk allowances (Art. 76).
Sick leave (Federal). 30 days a year at full pay; unused days accumulate up to 180 days; beyond 30 days in a year the Social Security fund reimburses the employer, and continued incapacity passes to the pension/SS regime (Art. 80). A medical certificate is required beyond a short self-certified period.
Maternity (Federal). At least 14 weeks at full pay — up to 8 weeks may be taken before the due date and not less than 6 weeks after delivery — extendable by the competent medical authority to up to 9 months for difficult or multiple births or complications; a nursing mother is entitled to a paid daily nursing break; she may not take other employment during the leave (Art. 88) and may take up to one year’s unpaid child-care leave (Art. 89). There is no statutory paternity leave; either parent may take up to 3 days’ unpaid leave for a sick child under 6 (Law 37/2015, Arts. 87–89).
Other paid leave (Federal). Marriage 5 days, a child’s marriage 1 day, bereavement of a close relative 5 days, iddat for a widow 130 days; pilgrimage (hajj) leave is unpaid (Art. 82).
Public holidays. Iraq observes the official national and religious holidays; Islamic-calendar dates are fixed each year by official announcement (Council of Ministers / KRG), so the precise Gregorian dates must be taken from the annual decree. Work on an official holiday or the weekly rest day is paid at 200%; a compensatory rest day is given for work on the weekly rest day (Art. 74).
KRI deltas. Under Law 71/1987: annual leave is 20 days, rising by 2 days for every 5 years' service; paid maternity leave is 62 days plus up to one year's unpaid child-care leave; sick leave is 30 days a year at full pay, accumulable to 180 days, with the Social Security Fund reimbursing the employer beyond 30 days. The Region applies its own holiday calendar (e.g. Nowruz, Kurdistan Uprising anniversary), set by annual KRG decree (per secondary legal sources).
6.2 Working hours and overtime
Standard hours and overtime premiums are set by the applicable labour law; work on official holidays attracts premium pay plus a compensatory day.
Working hours (Federal). 8 hours a day and 48 a week (Art. 67), excluding meal/rest breaks (Art. 68); reduced hours apply to arduous or health-harmful work. The weekly rest is Friday, at least 24 consecutive hours on full pay (Art. 70).
Overtime (Federal). Overtime is paid at not less than 150% of the normal wage for day work and 200% for work at night, on the weekly rest day, on official holidays or in arduous conditions (Art. 71(4)); overtime is subject to the statutory caps in Art. 71. A worker required to work on the weekly rest day also receives a compensatory rest day.
KRI. The 1987 code caps normal hours at 8/day and 48/week; overtime is paid at 150% for day work and 200% for night work, the weekly rest day and official holidays, with a compensatory rest day for work on the weekly rest day (per secondary legal sources).
6.3 Termination and notice
Notice, severance and end-of-service indemnity follow the applicable regional labour law; final pay includes pro-rated salary, untaken-leave payout (taxable) and the indemnity (tax-exempt).
Notice (Federal). An indefinite contract is ended on 30 days’ written notice; in default the employer pays 30 days’ wage in lieu (Art. 44; Art. 37). Probation may not exceed 3 months and a probationer is given at least 7 days’ notice (Art. 37(2)).
Grounds and protection. Summary termination is limited to the serious-fault grounds in Art. 43; an unfair or unlawful dismissal carries double the end-of-service entitlement (Art. 47). A worker who has lodged an official complaint is protected from dismissal.
Final settlement. All terminal entitlements — pro-rated salary, payout of untaken leave (taxable) and the end-of-service indemnity (tax-exempt) — are paid within 7 days of the end of the relationship (Art. 60). End-of-service mechanics are in Module 5.
KRI. During the three-month probation an employer may terminate without notice or payment in lieu (Law 71/1987, per secondary legal sources).
6.4 Wage deductions, garnishments and records
* Federal: court-ordered deductions are processed under Labour Law Art.57, subject to the percentage caps (20%/30%), with net pay protected at the minimum wage (Art.62).[r][s]
* Statutory tax and social-security withholding take priority and sit outside the post-tax deduction cap.
* Wage records (Federal). The employer maintains a wage register and pay records (Art. 61); the payslip shows gross pay, each statutory withholding (income tax and social security) and any capped post-tax deductions.
6.5 Employment types — tax and social-security treatment
Income-tax and social-security rates do not vary by contract type; what varies is the labour treatment and, for some categories, who bears the contribution. The base is always the actual wage for tax and the actual wage clamped to the SS floor/ceiling for social security.
Permanent / full-time. Standard PAYE and SS (employee 5% / employer 12%, plus the 8% State branch — funded by the State for Iraqi workers and borne by the employer (20% total) for foreign workers).
Part-time (Art. 39). A contract of 12–24 hours a week; pay, leave and SS run on the actual (pro-rata) wage; tax and SS rates are unchanged.
Daily / casual. Taxed and insured on actual wages earned; workers engaged by the day or week are paid weekly (maximum 5 days’ delay in wage payment).
Trainees / apprentices (Art. 27). Covered for work-injury and death; where an accident or death occurs, the Vocational Training Service pays the SS contribution computed on the minimum wage. Ordinary PAYE applies to any taxable stipend.
Freelancers / independent contractors. Outside the Labour-Law employment relationship — no employer PAYE withholding and no employer SS; the individual self-assesses income tax. A contractor working under the employer’s direction and control carries a re-characterisation (mis-classification) risk [practice point].
Secondary / multiple employment. Each employer withholds on the pay it makes and applies the personal allowance only against its own payroll (only if elected as primary employer for personal allowances); there is no statutory prior-employer year-to-date declaration. Any cumulation across employers is settled on the employee’s annual return.
