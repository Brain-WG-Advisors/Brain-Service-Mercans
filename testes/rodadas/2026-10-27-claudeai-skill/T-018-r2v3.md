## Resposta

**Use the RPN-driven Threshold → Base → Amount triplet, not a single Differential regulation.** Ireland is the one documented exception to the "progressive tax = one Differential" rule. The 2 July correction says exactly this: splitting progressive tax into three steps was specific to the RPN, wrong for other countries, and right for Ireland. The production IE pattern (CT-A-192, 52 regs) is: rates via RPN in 52/53xxx, the triplet, Paid regulations, and reconciliation at ordinal 0 and at the end.

## Why a Differential doesn't fit

A Differential's band edges are annual constants in the band table, and the engine divides them by frequency. In Ireland the 20%/40% boundary is not a constant. It is the employee's SRCOP from the RPN, and it varies by employee for three reasons:

- **Personal status.** It can be €44,000, €48,000 or €53,000, and for two-earner couples it can be anything up to €88,000.
- **Revenue changes it.** Revenue splits it across employments and reduces it mid-year through revised RPNs, for example to tax Illness Benefit or Jobseeker's Benefit.
- **No PPSN.** Under the emergency basis it drops to nil.

Selecting one of four static tables with a flag would still miss most real RPNs. Credits and the basis (`CUMULATIVE` / `WEEK_1` / `EMERGENCY`) also come from the RPN. So the boundary has to be computed per employee in a formula, and that is what the triplet does.

## Structure

**Flags, before regulation 1 (582xx, values from 1, routing with `LESS`/`GREATER`):**
- Basis flag: 1 = Cumulative, 2 = Week 1, 3 = Emergency.
- PPSN-present flag.
- Emergency pay-count, for the escalation (monthly: first month gets 1/12 of €44,000 = €3,666.67, then nil; weekly: weeks 1–4 get €846.15, then nil). Frequencies other than weekly, fortnightly, four-weekly, twice-monthly and monthly get the higher rate only.
- Exclusion Order flag.
- Week 53 flag, which forces non-cumulative treatment for that payment.

**Pay for Income Tax base (581xx):**
- Taxable series go in the matrix.
- Employee superannuation, PRSA, RAC, AVC and PHI contributions, plus Travel Pass and Cycle to Work sacrifice, go on the credit side as negatives.
- My Future Fund stays out entirely; it gets no relief.
- Add a `_period` copy crediting 99998, because `PayForIncomeTax` (item 25) is reported.

**The triplet:**

| Step | Computes | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold | Available cut-off C: cumulative for periods to date, one period's worth under Week 1, or the emergency figure | Formula (RPN/HR input) |
| Base | Standard portion `MIN(P;C)` and higher portion `MAX(SUBTRACT(P;C);0)`, where P is YTD under the cumulative basis | Flat, `_post_formula`, `$band_amount_raw` |
| Amount | 20% × standard + 40% × higher − credits (both rates from 52xxx). Under cumulative: YTD tax due = `MAX(0; gross − cumulative credits)`, then minus YTD tax deducted = this period's PAYE | Flat, `_post_formula` |

**Tail:**
- A Paid regulation posts debit 2610 and credit 2552.
- Reconciliation runs at ordinal 0 (Flag Reconciliation MTD) and again at the end.

## Points to settle while building

1. **Refunds.** Negative period PAYE is a normal cumulative outcome, and `IncomeTaxPaid` (item 26) can be negative. Matrix sums clamp at 0, so the refund must come from a formula over earlier outputs, never from a matrix sum. Confirm the engine accepts a negative regulation result.
2. **Tax Rule Group strings.** The "YTD due minus YTD deducted" step probably composes `simple_tax` with `_ytd` and `_negate_ytm`, following the Canada precedent. Take the exact strings from CT-A-192 rather than guessing.
3. **Credits.** Production carries three separate credit regulations, and our v1.0 missed them. Decide between the Credit Amount band field and the formula, following production.
4. **Does the RPN give yearly or already-scaled figures? This is a research gap.** The report spec (§6) and the DD say the RPN cut-off is "cumulative or period, per the basis". That wording matches payroll-submission items 20–21. As far as I know, the RPN itself carries yearly cut-off and yearly credits, and the employer does the scaling. Check this against *RPN: Data Items* (28 Nov 2025). If the RPN is yearly, the Threshold formula must do the scaling: × periods to date ÷ periods in year for cumulative, and ÷ periods in year for Week 1.
5. **Keep out of the engine.** Marginal relief and exemption limits are applied by Revenue through the RPN.

## Validation target

The design should reproduce CCG §7, which is a €5,000 month-1 cumulative case with C = €3,666.67:
- €733.33 at the standard rate plus €533.33 at the higher rate gives gross tax of €1,266.67.
- Less credits of €333.33, PAYE is **€933.33**.

After that, run a month-2 cumulative case and a Week-1 case. Then run an emergency month 1 → month 2 to prove the escalation, and a refund case to prove negative PAYE comes through.

USC follows the same reasoning, since its bands and exemption also arrive through the RPN. The one difference is emergency USC: a flat 8% with no cut-off, so it needs no escalation logic.
