## Resposta

**Short answer:** annual maximums are not divided across pay periods. You deduct at the full rate every period and track year-to-date (YTD) amounts. When YTD reaches the annual maximum, the final period takes only the remaining balance, and deductions stop for the rest of the year. The one per-period element is the CPP basic exemption.

**2026 parameters (outside Quebec)**

| | Rate (employee) | Earnings ceiling | Annual employee max |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPP (base) | 5.95% | YMPE $74,600, minus $3,500 exemption | $4,230.45 |
| CPP2 | 4% | YMPE $74,600 to YAMPE $85,000 | $416.00 |
| EI | 1.63% | MIE $68,900 | $1,123.07 |

Sources: CRA rate tables as republished by Carleton HR, CPP2 formula of 4.00% × ($85,000 − $74,600) = $416.00, and ESDC's 2026 EI notice.

**CPP (base).** The employer deducts CPP each pay period until the maximum pensionable earnings or the maximum employee contribution for the year is reached. No contributions are taken on the $3,500 annual basic exemption, which is split across the pay periods in the year. CRA publishes the per-period exemption, for example $291.66 for monthly pay.

Two details tend to cause errors:
- The per-period exemption is truncated, not rounded. For biweekly pay, $3,500 / 26 = $134.6153, and the part-penny is dropped, so the correct exemption is $134.61.
- Only regular earnings get the exemption. Regular earnings qualify only if they are both worked and paid in the same pay period, and bonuses don't qualify for any CPP basic exemption.

**CPP2.** The $3,500 basic exemption applies only to base CPP. CPP2 has no basic exemption. CPP2 starts if and when the employee's annual income passes the YMPE. In practice, CPP2 begins in the same period where YTD earnings cross $74,600. Split that period: only the portion above the YMPE is charged CPP2. CPP and CPP2 must be tracked independently, with separate annual maximums and separate stop points.

**EI.** EI has no exemption and starts from the first dollar. The employer must deduct $1.63 for every $100 paid until the maximum annual premium is reached. In Quebec, the rate is $1.30 per $100, with a maximum of $895.70. The employer pays 1.4× the employee premium, up to a maximum of $1,572.30 per employee.

**Maximums apply per employer.** When an employee changes employers, CPP, QPP and EI deductions restart from zero, even if the employee already reached the maximum with the previous employer. Any excess is refunded to the employee when they file their income tax return. The exception is a successor employer, which can carry over the previous employer's YTD amounts. CRA rules govern this, and payroll systems require a CPP/EI ruling from CRA before applying successor treatment.

**Prorated CPP maximum for part-year contributors.** CPP and QPP are prorated for the year when an individual becomes or ceases to be eligible to contribute, for example by turning 18 or 70 or by electing out. To prorate, multiply the annual maximum contributory earnings by the number of pensionable months in the year (divided by 12). EI has no equivalent proration.

**Worked example: $120,000/year, paid monthly at $10,000**
- **CPP:** each month is ($10,000 − $291.66) × 5.95% = $577.65. Months 1–7 total $4,043.55. Month 8 takes the remaining $186.90. Months 9–12 are $0.
- **CPP2:** month 8 is when YTD earnings reach $80,000, so CPP2 is ($80,000 − $74,600) × 4% = $216.00. Month 9 takes the remaining $200.00. Months 10–12 are $0.
- **EI:** each month is $10,000 × 1.63% = $163.00. Months 1–6 total $978.00. Month 7 takes the remaining $145.07. Months 8–12 are $0.

**Payslip:** show current-period and YTD amounts for CPP, CPP2 and EI on separate lines. That makes the stop point visible and explains the jump in net pay once a maximum is reached.

For Quebec employees, QPP/QPP2 and QPIP replace CPP and part of EI. Revenu Québec sets those, and the 2026 QPP first-tier rate is 6.40%.
