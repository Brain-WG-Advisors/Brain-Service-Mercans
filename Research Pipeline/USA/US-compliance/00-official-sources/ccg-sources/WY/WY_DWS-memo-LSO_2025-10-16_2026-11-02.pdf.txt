This memo offers an overview of the Wyoming Unemployment Insurance (UI) program, 
intended for the Joint Labor, Health and Social Services Committee as it reviews the proposed 
draft bill. Similar legislation has been introduced and discussed in previous years. The main 
purpose of this memo is to address questions that have arisen in past discussions.  
 
Conformity with Federal Unemployment Insurance Laws 
UI is a federal-state partnership based on Federal law that is administered by state employees 
under state law. Federal law establishes UI requirements, including broad coverage and benefit 
provisions, as well as the federal taxable wage base, tax rate, and administrative requirements. 
Businesses in states with UI laws conforming to federal law, such as Wyoming, receive credits 
for the federal unemployment tax. Currently, this tax is 6% on the first $7,000 of wages ($420). 
However, businesses in conforming states receive a 5.4% credit, effectively reducing the tax 
from $420 to $42. 
 
States must meet several requirements to comply with federal UI law. Key requirements include, 
but are not limited to, the following: 
●​ Benefit Payments: All funds withdrawn from the federal unemployment trust fund are 
exclusively for UI benefit payments. 
●​ Equal Treatment: UI benefits for federal, state, and local government employees must be 
equivalent in amount, terms, and conditions to those for private sector employees.

Claimant Benefit Eligibility Requirements for Wyoming Unemployment Insurance 
The key UI benefit eligibility criteria include: 
●​ Monetary Eligibility: The base period is the first four of the last five completed calendar 
quarters immediately preceding the effective date of the claim. Claimants must have 
earned at least 2 quarters of wages in the base period, have earned at least 8% of the 
statewide average annual wage (for 2025, this is $4,900) in the base period, and the total 
base wages must be at least 1.4 times the highest quarter’s wages. See, W.S. 
27-3-306(d)(i) and (iii). 
●​ Separation from Employment: The claimant’s separation must not be for misconduct or 
voluntary. 
●​ Able and Available for Work: Claimants must be physically and mentally capable of 
performing suitable work and must be available to accept such work if offered.2 
●​ Work Search Requirements: Claimants must be actively and diligently seeking suitable 
employment by making at least two verifiable job contacts each week. These contacts 
must demonstrate a reasonable effort to secure new employment and are subject to review 
by the Department of Workforce Services. Every five weeks, UI specifically verifies the 
contacts.3

The Reemployment Services and Eligibility Assessment (RESEA) program 
RESEA is a proactive program aimed at accelerating the reemployment of UI claimants and 
ensuring continuing weekly eligibility. This initiative fosters collaboration between our UI 
Claims Center and Workforce Centers. RESEA actively involves selected claimants, offering 
them individualized and targeted support to overcome reemployment obstacles and secure new 
jobs swiftly.

an in-depth assessment to identify specific challenges they might face in their job search, 
such as skill gaps, resume deficiencies, or other personal circumstances. Based on this 
assessment, a personalized reemployment plan is developed.

UI Tax Rates and CARES Act Funding 
Wyoming's UI taxes are paid solely by the employer. The tax rates are based on a combination of 
factors: 
●​ A base rate or benefit ratio is individualized for the company.  
○​ For employers with at least three years of taxable wage history, a benefit ratio is 
calculated by dividing the company’s benefit charges by its taxable wages for the 
three most recent fiscal years. 
○​ For employers with less than three years of taxable wages, a base rate is assigned 
based on the industry's average rate.  
●​ Fund Balance Factor is:  
○​ Positive when the UI Trust Fund is less than 3.5% of the state's total payroll; or 
○​ Negative when the fund is more than 4% of the state’s total payroll. 
●​ Non-Charge/Ineffective Charge Adjustment Factor is calculated by dividing all 
noncharges and ineffective charges by the taxable wages for the preceding fiscal year.

In 2022, 2023, and 2024, Wyoming had a minimum UI tax rate of 0%.  
 
The 2025 minimum UI rate is .14%. With the current taxable wage base of $32,400, this equates 
to a maximum of $45.36/year/employee (as not all employees make $32,400/year). For 2025, 
employers with the minimum tax rate (those who have not incurred any benefit charges for three 
years) will pay no more than $45.36 in State UI tax per employee.  
  
In 2025, 13,066 employers had the .14% tax rate, 396 employers had the statutory maximum tax 
rate of 10% and the other 12,878 had a tax rate between .14% and 10%. 
 
UI Trust Fund 
The balance of the federal UI Trust Fund at the end of August 2025 was $555,739,973. 
Quarterly, the federal government sends interest payments for the trust fund. On October 1, 2025, 
the quarterly interest payment was $4.4 million. All interest payments are deposited into the trust 
fund. 
 
The Unemployment Insurance Commission 
The Unemployment Insurance Commission’s primary function is to review appeals from both UI 
claimants and employers. Please note that W.S. 27-3-605 states that the Department of 
Workforce Services shall administer the “unemployment compensation program” in this state.

Wyoming Unemployment Insurance Benefit Scenarios 
 
The scenarios below outline various potential Unemployment Insurance (UI) benefit outcomes 
for workers, based on the hours worked and wages received each quarter during the base period. 
These examples highlight how each situation is unique. In each scenario below, the worker is 
monetarily eligible for UI benefits having already met the criteria of at least 2 quarters of wages 
in the base period, earnings of at least 8% of the statewide average annual wage (for 2025 is 
$4,900) in the base period and the total base wages are equal to at least 1.4 times the highest 
quarter’s wages. See, W.S. 27-3-306(d)(i) and (iii).  
 
Per W.S. 27-3-303(a), the weekly benefit amount is determined as follows: 
●​ Four percent (4%) of total wages payable for insured work in the quarter of the base 
period in which wages were highest.  
●​ However, the weekly benefit amount shall not exceed 55% of the statewide weekly wage, 
which is $651 for July 6, 2025 – July 5, 2026.  
 
Per W.S. 27-3-304, the maximum UI payment for a benefit year is the lesser of:  
●​ 26 weeks at the individual’s weekly benefit amount; or 
●​ Thirty percent (30%) of wages for insured work in the base period.
