Onboarding


Employment Contract
Hiring employees in Taiwan should start with an employment contract. Taiwan’s Labour Standards Act (LSA) recognizes 2 types of employment contracts — fixed-term and non-fixed-term. Fixed-term contracts work for temporary or short-term work of up to 6 months, seasonal work of no more than 9 months, and specific work with a defined duration. 


Work Permits
Foreign workers can be hired in Taiwan, but need an advance work permit to work legally. Normally, the employer will take care of this for the worker by lodging the necessary documents with the Ministry of Labor. The paperwork includes verification of employee qualifications and a job description outlining duties.


Minimum Wage
Employee salary must not be lower than minimum wage. In the case of 2025 minimum monthly salary is NT$28,590 (minimum hourly wage is NT$190).


Social security
Overview of Social Security Types in Taiwan.
In Taiwan, the structure of social security encompasses several types of insurance programs designed to provide protection and benefits to employees and their dependents. These programs are governed by national regulations, and the standard monthly duration for contribution calculations is fixed at 30 days.


Categories of Social Security Programs:


1. National Health Insurance (NHI)
A mandatory health insurance program that provides comprehensive medical coverage to all citizens, residents and foreign non-residents employee of Taiwan.


* National Health Insurance – Dependent Addition : An extension of the NHI that includes coverage for eligible dependents of the insured person.
* Supplementary National Health Insurance (SNHI) : This covers additional income or compensation not included in the standard NHI contributions, such as bonuses or part-time income. In case of bonuses the total amount of the year that exceeds 4 times the monthly insured amount. In the case of a part-timer, monthly salary income exceeds the minimum basic salary of the year.


2. Labor Insurance
Offers coverage for illness, maternity, disability, death, and old-age benefits for employees.
* Ordinary Insurance : A subcategory under labor insurance that covers general employment-related risks.
* Employment Insurance : Provides unemployment benefits and employment assistance to individuals who have lost their jobs.
* Occupational Accident Insurance : Specifically targets incidents related to workplace injuries or occupational diseases.


3. Overdue Wage Payment Fund
A government-managed fund used to compensate employees when their employer is unable to pay wages due to bankruptcy or financial difficulty.


4. Labor Pension System
Divided into two categories based on the employee’s hire date:
* Old Pension System: Applicable to employees hired before July 1, 2005.
* New Pension System: Applicable to employees hired on or after July 1, 2005.


5. Welfare Fund (Optional)[a][b][c][d]
A voluntary program that may be offered by employers to provide additional benefits to employees, such as subsidies or recreational activities.


Calculation of Insurance Contributions (National Health Insurance ,Labor Insurance and Employment Insurance)


Each insurance type has its own contribution base, which is defined by government-regulated contribution level tables. 
The method for calculating premiums falls under two primary types:


Type 1: Formula-Based Calculation


The premium is calculated as: Contribution Base × Insurance Rate × Contribution Rate


Type 2: Band-Based Calculation[e][f]
Instead of using a formula, the insurance amount is determined directly from an official premium table. This method is generally applied to:
* National Health Insurance
* Labor Insurance (Except OAI)
* Employment Insurance




System Implementation Note
The Mercans system adopts the Type 2 calculation method. Therefore, users must manually select the appropriate insurance band when setting up or managing insurance contributions in the system.




Income tax
The employer is required to withhold tax for employee’s salary income during each payroll.


Resident: An foreign individual is considered a tax resident in Taiwan if they stay in the country for 183 days or more in a calendar year.


Non-Resident: An foreign individual who stays in Taiwan for less than 183 days in a calendar year is considered a non-resident.


Tax Method
There are 4 types of tax methods applicable in Taiwan.


Tax Methods for resident employees:
* Recurring income
   * Tax Table  
      * Monthly salary less than 500,000: Employees may be taxed using the monthly tax tables, this allows the employee to apply for tax dependents credit/relief.
      * Monthly salary more than 500,000: Use annual table and apply exemption items.
   * Fixed Tax Rate - Resident employees may elect to be taxed at a fixed rate of 5% (2025 year)
* Additional income
   * Variable Tax Rate - Resident employees are taxed on 5% on the variable income they received, example of variable income can be bonuses. ( When the taxable variable income reaches 88,501 (in the year 2025) and the number of tax dependent is 0, 5% withheld is required.)


Tax Method for non-resident employee:
* non-resident tax rate - Non resident are taxed at either 6% or 18% depending on whether their income exceeds 1.5 times of the minimum wage

Leave entitlement
Annual Leave
According to the employee's length of service, annual leave days are decided.
6 months or more but less than 1 year: 3 days
1 year or more but less than 2 years: 7 days
2 years or more but less than 3 years: 10 days
3 years or more but less than 5 years: 14 days
5 years or more but less than 10 years: 15 days
More than 10 years: 1 additional day per year, up to a maximum of 30 days.
Unused leave must be paid out if not taken within 2 years.


Sick Leave
Ordinary sick leave for those not hospitalized is limited to a maximum of 30 days per year. For those who are not hospitalized, the total sick leave within two years must not exceed one year.


If sick leave within the first 30 days, paid 50% of salary. No payment if exceeding 30 days.
If hospitalized within 1 year, 50% of salary should be paid in the first 30 days.


Menstrual Leave
One day of menstrual leave may be taken each month. If the total number of menstrual leave days taken in a year does not exceed three days, they are not counted as part of sick leave.
Any additional days beyond that will be included in the sick leave calculation.


Paid at half salary.


Maternity Leave
There are 2 kinds of maternity leave.
Fully paid leave: 8 weeks for employees with a minimum of 6 months of service.
Pregnancy checkup leave: 7 days of paid leave for pregnancy checkups.


Paternity Leave
7 days of fully paid leave for spouses during childbirth.


The employer covers the first 5 days, and the remaining 2 days can be covered by a government subsidy.


Parental Leave
Up to the child’s 3rd birth day
Unpaid, but labor insurance benefits (wage replacement) can be applied for
60% of average monthly insured salary for up to 6 months.


Bereavement Leave
Bereavement leave dates vary depending on relationship to the deceased, ranging from 3 to 8 days.
* 8 days for parents, foster parents, step-parent or spouse.
* 6 days for grandparents, children, wife’s parents, wife’s adoptive parents or step-parent.
* 3 days for great-grandparents, siblings, grandparents in law.


Severance Payment
Eligibility
Termination by Employer: If the employer initiates the termination of the employment contract.
Layoffs Due to Business Reasons: Such as business closure, restructuring, or transfer of ownership.
Involuntary Termination: If the employee is dismissed for reasons not related to misconduct.

Offboarding
Offboarding an employee in Taiwan involves legal, administrative, and procedural steps governed primarily by the Labor Standards Act and related regulations. 
Employers must provide advance written notice or compensation in lieu of notice:
Employment Duration
	Notice Period
	Less than 3 months
	None required
	3 months to under 1 year
	10 days
	1 to under 3 years
	20 days
	3 years or more
	30 days
	

Documentation and exit procedures
Ensure all offboarding steps are properly documented:
* Termination notice (with reason)
* Final paycheck, including unused leave and bonuses
* Severance pay (if applicable)
* Certificate of employment (upon request)
* Return of company property
* Exit interview (optional but helpful)
* Involuntary resignation Certificate or resignation Certificate.
Labor Insurance and Pension Reporting 
* Report employee's termination to the Bureau of Labor Insurance and Health Insurance.
* If enrolled in the New Labor Pension System, ensure final contribution is made
