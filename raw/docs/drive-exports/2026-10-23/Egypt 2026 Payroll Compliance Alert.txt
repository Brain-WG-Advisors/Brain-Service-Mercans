2. Social Insurance: The Core Engine Update (Covered in last update)
2.1. The 2026 Insurable Wage Parameters
Effective January 1, 2026, the National Organization for Social Insurance (NOSI) enforces new floors and ceilings.:
* Minimum Insurable Wage (SI_MIN): EGP 2,700 per month.
* Maximum Insurable Wage (SI_MAX): EGP 16,700 per month.
2.2. Contribution Rates and Classifications
2.3.1. Standard Employees
For the vast majority of the workforce, the contribution structure is as follows:
* Employee Share: 11% of the Insurable Wage.2
* Employer Share: 18.75% of the Insurable Wage 2
2.3.2. Managers and Board Directors [c][d][e]
A critical "edge case" logic exists for company directors and managers whose names appear in the Commercial Register.
* Logic: These individuals are insured as "Employers" rather than "Employees."
* Rate: A flat 21% employer contribution.2
* Base: Crucially, this rate is applied to the Maximum Insurable Wage (EGP 16,700), regardless of their actual drawing. Even if a board member takes a nominal salary of EGP 5,000, their social insurance liability is calculated on EGP 16,700.
2.4. Handling Salary Components (Basic vs. Variable)
Add Basic + Recurring Allowances + Overtime + Commissions to determine the "Total Insurable Wage" before comparing it against the SI_MIN and SI_MAX limits.
3. Emergency Benefit Fund: The Decoupling Risk[f]
3.1. 2026 EBF Parameters
The Emergency Benefit Fund is an employer-only contribution designed to aid workers in distressed companies. For 2026, the parameters are:
* Basis: Basic Salary Only.
* Contribution Rate: 1%.4
* Minimum Basic Wage Cap: EGP 440.3
* Maximum Basic Wage Cap: EGP 2,720.3
* Applicability Threshold: Companies with > 30 Employees.
4. The New Labor Law No. 14 of 2025[g][h]
The enactment of Labor Law No. 14 of 2025, which replaces the 2003 legislation, introduces profound changes to payroll logic. While the law was published in May 2025 and effective September 2025, the 2026 fiscal year is the first full year where these rules apply comprehensively, particularly the new annual Training Fund cycle.
4.1. Training Fund: A Complete Paradigm Shift
The most technically complex update for 2026 is the Training Fund. Under the old law (Law 12/2003), this was a finance-driven calculation: 1% of the company's annual Net Profit. This meant it was often handled outside the payroll engine, typically by the Finance team during audit.
The New Labor Law moves this squarely into the Payroll Domain. It transforms the levy from a profit tax to a payroll headcount tax.5
4.1.1. The New Formula (Effective Jan 1, 2026)
* Trigger: Employers with > 30 Employees.5 (Old law was > 10 employees).
* Basis: The National Minimum Social Insurance Wage (SI_MIN).
   * Note: It is calculated on the statutory minimum (EGP 2,700), NOT the employee's actual salary.
* Rate: 0.25% of the Basis.
* Constraints:
   * Minimum Floor: EGP 10.00 per employee.
   * Maximum Ceiling: EGP 30.00 per employee.
* Exemption: Employers who provide robust internal training programs may apply for an exemption, but the payroll system must accrue the liability by default until the exemption is granted.8
4.1.2. Logic Walkthrough for 2026
Let's calculate the liability for a single employee in 2026:
1. Get Basis: SI_MIN = EGP 2,700.1
2. Apply Rate: 2,700 * 0.0025 = 6.75 EGP.
3. Apply Floor: Max(6.75, 10.00) = 10.00 EGP.
4. Apply Ceiling: Min(10.00, 30.00) = 10.00 EGP.
Result: The contribution for 2026 is EGP 10.00 per employee
4.2.  Employer termination of definite term contracts:
Under the New Labour Law, employees are entitled to a gratuity equal to one month's salary for each year of service to be paid if the employer ends a definite term employment contract pre-maturely.
4.3 Leave Entitlement Updates
The New Labor Law alters leave logic, impacting "paid days" calculations and potential termination payouts.
* Annual Leave: Employees will be entitled to 15 workdays of employer-paid leave in the first year of service (currently eligible only after six months of work, pro rata based on 21 workdays); individuals with disabilities will be entitled to 45 days of paid leave per year
* Sick Leave: The new structure provides more generous terms. Employees are entitled to 100% of pay for the first 3 months of sick leave (previously lower), then 85% for the next 6 months, and 75% for the final 3 months.12
* Maternity Leave: Increased from 90 days to 120 days (4 months).11
   * Eligibility: Can be taken up to 3 times (previously 2)
* Paternity Leave: A new entitlement of 1 day of paid leave for fathers upon the birth of a child.11
5. Universal Health Insurance (UHI): Geographic Expansion[i]
5.1. Phase 2 Rollout
The UHI system is a mandatory health coverage scheme replacing the old system incrementally. Deductions are triggered based on the location of the employee. For Jan 1, 2026, the system expands into Phase 2 Governorates.6

5.2. Deduction Logic & Rates
Once an employee is identified as being in an active zone (based on Work Location or SI Office Location), the following rates apply:
* Employee Share: 1% of Total Insurable Wage.
* Employer Share: 3% of Total Insurable Wage.
* Basis: Unlike SI (capped at 16,700), the UHI Law technically applies to the total income. However, usually, it aligns with the SI base for administrative simplicity. Critical check: Snippet 18 mentions "maximum annual calculation base for establishing health insurance... will enter into force 1 Jan 2026." This suggests UHI might have its own cap or align formally with the SI Cap. Recommendation: Apply the deduction on the Total Insurable Wage up to the SI Max (16,700) unless specific executive regulations specify a higher ceiling for UHI. Most implementations utilize the SI Cap.
5.3. Dependent Coverage Logic
Employees are liable for dependents if they are the sole breadwinner:
* Non-working Spouse: 3% deduction.
* Children: 1% per child.
* Payroll Implication: This is often a direct payment by the individual to the UHI Authority, but some employers facilitate it. The software should support an Optional_UHI_Dependents_Deduction field, but it should not be mandatory unless the employer policy dictates.
6. Taxation: Personal Exemption & Martyrs Fund
6.1. Personal Exemption Thresholds
For the 2026 tax year, the calculation of the "Personal Exemption" (the amount deducted from taxable income before applying tax brackets) is as follows:
* Standard Employee: EGP 20,000 per year.15
   * Note: Some snippets mention 15k rising to 20k. Snippet 15 confirms 20k is the current standard.
* Disabled Employee: EGP 30,000 per year.3
   * Logic: Disabled persons receive a 50% uplift on the standard exemption (20,000 * 1.5 = 30,000).[j]
6.2. Martyrs Fund (Solidarity Levy)
The "Martyrs and Victims Fund" levy remains a mandatory deduction.
* Rate: 0.0005 (0.05% or "Five Ten-Thousandths").16
* Basis: Gross Salary (Total Monthly Income).
* Placement: This is deducted from the Net Pay. It is a "Net-to-Net" reduction or a post-tax deduction depending on interpretation, but standard practice in Egypt is to calculate it on the Gross and deduct it as a separate line item.[k]
* Example: Gross Salary EGP 50,000. Levy = 50,000 * 0.0005 = 25.00 EGP.
7. Payroll Engine Impact Simulation
To visualize the cumulative effect of these changes, the following simulation breaks down the 2026 payroll calculation for a high-income employee situated in a newly active Phase 2 governorate.
Scenario:
* Employee: Senior Engineer.
* Location: Damietta (UHI Phase 2).
* Gross Salary: EGP 20,000 per month (Basic: 10,000, Variable: 10,000).
* Marital Status: Single.


Analysis of Simulation:
1. SI Deduction: The salary (20k) exceeds the cap (16.7k). Deduction is 16,700 * 11% = 1,837.
   * 2025 Comparison: In 2025, the cap was 14.5k, so deduction was 1,595. The employee pays EGP 242 more in SI this year.
2. UHI Deduction: Because Damietta is now active, the employee pays 16,700 * 1% = 167.
   * 2025 Comparison: EGP 0.
3. Net Impact: The combined effect of the SI cap increase and UHI activation significantly reduces take-home pay compared to 2025. Communications to employees should clearly attribute this to "Statutory Compliance Updates."
9. Comprehensive Test Strategy (QA/UAT)
The following test scenarios are mandatory for the "Egypt 2026 Compliance Release."
9.1. Test Case A: The "Floor" Employee
* Inputs: Basic Salary EGP 2,000. Location: Cairo.
* Logic: Salary < SI Min (2,700).
* Expected Outputs:
   * SI Base: 2,700 (Deemed).
   * SI Employee Deduction: 2,700 * 11% = 297.
   * SI Employer Deduction: 2,700 * 18.75% = 506.25.
   * EBF Deduction: 2,000 * 1% = 20 (Uses actual basic, as it's > EBF Min 440).
   * Training Fund: 10 (Floor).
9.2. Test Case B: The "Split Cap" Executive
* Inputs: Basic Salary EGP 25,000. Location: Minya (Phase 2).
* Logic: Salary > SI Max (16,700) AND Salary > EBF Max (2,720).
* Expected Outputs:
   * SI Base: 16,700.
   * SI Employee Deduction: 16,700 * 11% = 1,837.
   * EBF Deduction: 27.20 (Capped at 2,720 * 1%). CRITICAL CHECK: Ensure system does not calculate 167.00.
   * UHI Deduction: 167.00 (1% of SI Max).
   * Training Fund: 10 (Floor).
9.3. Test Case C: The "Profit-to-Payroll" Transition (Training Fund)
* Inputs: Company with 50 employees.
* Logic: Run payroll for Jan 2026.
* Expected Output: Total Training Fund Liability = 50 * 10 = 500 EGP.
* Negative Test: Verify that the system does not prompt for "Net Profit" or attempt to calculate 1% of any profit figure.
9.4. Test Case D: The "3% Increment" Audit
* Inputs: Employee Salary in Jan 2025 = 10,000. Salary in Jan 2026 = 10,200.
* Logic: Increase is 200. Required 3% of 10,000 = 300.
* Expected Output: System flags a "Compliance Warning: Increment below statutory minimum (3%)."
