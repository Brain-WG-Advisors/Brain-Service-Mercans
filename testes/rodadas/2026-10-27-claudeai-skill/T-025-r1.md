## Resposta

The rule is simple: **each period's deduction is the lesser of the remaining annual maximum and the normal rate applied to that period's earnings.** You never use a flat slice like "annual maximum ÷ 12." The two only give the same answer in the first pay of the year with zero year-to-date. Everything therefore depends on reliable YTD accumulators kept per calendar year and per payroll program account.

**The per-period formulas (2026, T4127)**

```
CPP  = lesser of (i) 4,230.45 × (PM/12) − D
                 (ii) 0.0595 × [PI − (3,500 ÷ P)]                      floor 0

CPP2 = lesser of (i) 416.00 × (PM/12) − D2
                 (ii) (PI_YTD + PI − W) × 0.04,
                      W = greater of PI_YTD or 74,600 × (PM/12)       floor 0

EI   = lesser of (i) 1,123.07 − D1
                 (ii) 0.0163 × IE                                      floor 0
```

D, D2 and D1 are the YTD employee CPP, CPP2 and EI. PI is the period's pensionable earnings and IE its insurable earnings. P is the number of pay periods in the year. PM is the number of months CPP is required.

The employer amounts follow the employee amounts. Employer CPP and CPP2 match the employee dollar for dollar. Employer EI is 1.4 × the employee premium, so it stops when the employee premium stops.

**Points that commonly go wrong**

The CPP basic exemption is $3,500 ÷ P, **truncated** rather than rounded (monthly 291.66, semi-monthly 145.83, bi-weekly 134.61, weekly 67.30). It is given in full for any period with pay. It is never prorated for days worked, and it is applied only once per period even when a bonus is paid in the same period. In a 53-week or 27-bi-weekly year, the per-period exemption is recalculated (66.03 or 129.62), but the annual maximums stay the same.

CPP2 is not a flat 4% from the first dollar. It starts only after YTD pensionable earnings pass the YMPE of $74,600, and it is capped at $416 on the band up to the YAMPE of $85,000. It needs its own accumulator (D2), separate from base CPP.

EI has no exemption and no PM proration. Its ceiling is the MIE of $68,900 per employer.

PM/12 reduces the CPP and CPP2 maximums in years when the employee turns 18 or 70, dies, becomes disabled, or files or revokes a CPT30 election.

**Worked example: $10,000/month salary, Ontario, PM = 12**

| Month | CPP | CPP2 | EI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–6 | 577.65 each | 0.00 | 163.00 each |
| 7 | 577.65 | 0.00 | **145.07** (remaining max) |
| 8 | **186.90** (remaining max) | 216.00 (YTD PI crosses 74,600) | 0.00 |
| 9 | 0.00 | **200.00** (remaining max) | 0.00 |
| 10–12 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| **Total** | **4,230.45** | **416.00** | **1,123.07** |

The regular CPP figure is 0.0595 × (10,000 − 291.66) = 577.65. The partial amounts in the capping months are what an engine using "annual ÷ 12" gets wrong.

**Multiple employers, transfers and go-live**

Each employer, and each payroll program account, applies the full annual maximums to its own payments. Payroll does not combine contributions across employers. Prior-employer YTD figures are recorded for information only, and the employee recovers any over-deduction on the T1 (or TP-1 in Québec). A move between legal entities mid-year also restarts the maximums. In contrast, when migrating from another provider under the **same** payroll account, you must load the YTD CPP, CPP2, EI, tax, pensionable and insurable figures at go-live, or the remaining-maximum calculation will over-deduct. An employer can correct an over-deduction on a later pay within the same year, but not after the year has closed.

**Income tax interaction**

Once the contributions are maxed, the K2 credit still uses the maximum base CPP ($3,519.45) and the maximum EI ($1,123.07). Federal tax therefore doesn't jump in the periods where CPP and EI drop to zero. CPP2 and the 1% enhanced portion go through F5 as an income deduction, never through K2.

**Québec**

The calculation works the same way with different constants. QPP is 6.30% with a maximum of $4,479.30, and QPP2 has a maximum of $416.00. Reduced EI is 1.30% with a maximum of $895.70 on the $68,900 MIE. QPIP runs on its **own** $103,000 ceiling with a $442.90 employee maximum and no exemption. A Québec employee therefore has two insurable ceilings at once, and pays QPP instead of CPP, never both.

**Payslip**

Show each deduction's period amount next to its YTD (CPP, CPP2, EI, QPP, QPP2, QPIP, income tax). In the capping month the line shows the reduced amount, and afterwards it shows 0.00 while the YTD stays at the maximum. This lets the employee see that the deduction stopped because the maximum was reached, not because of an error. The Canada Labour Code Part III requires each deduction to be shown for federally regulated employers. Provincial employment-standards rules otherwise apply.

**One thing to check in the reference pack:** CCG Example D sets PM = 6 for an employee **hired** on 1 July. The CCG's own rules elsewhere (each employer applies the full maximum, and PM changes only for age 18/70, death, disability or CPT30) and CRA guidance say a mid-year hire is **not** prorated: PM stays 12 and the remaining-maximum calculation handles the rest.
