## Resposta

**Default DD values (GB, 2026/27):** Employer **3%**, Employee **5%**, Total **8%**, applied to qualifying earnings.

The statute sets an employer minimum of 3% and a total minimum of 8% of qualifying earnings. The 5% employee rate is not a separate statutory figure. It is the balance needed to reach 8% when the employer pays only the minimum. The employer must contribute, the total contribution must be at least 8% of the jobholder's qualifying earnings in the relevant pay reference period, and the employer's share must be at least 3%. The legal basis is the Pensions Act 2008, s.20 (quality requirement for UK money purchase schemes). Because of this, the DD should keep the employee rate as a configurable field rather than a fixed statutory constant: if the employer chooses to pay the full 8%, the employee doesn't have to contribute anything.

**Basis fields to set alongside the rates (2026/27):**

| Threshold | Annual | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| Lower level of qualifying earnings | £6,240 | £520 |
| Earnings trigger | £10,000 | £833 |
| Upper level of qualifying earnings | £50,270 | £4,189 |

Monthly figures are from the 2026/27 per-period table. DWP confirmed the UEL stays at £50,270 for 2026 to 2027, and the trigger and LEL are also unchanged from 2025/26 (gov.uk: *Review of the AE Earnings Trigger and Qualifying Earnings Band for 2026/27*, Table 1).

**Points to flag in the DD:**

1. **Tax relief method changes the employee deduction.** Under relief at source, payroll deducts 4% net and HMRC adds 1%, which is the 3% employer / 4% worker / 1% government tax relief split. Under a net pay arrangement, the full 5% is deducted gross. The DD needs a scheme-level field for this.
2. **Certification sets use different defaults** if the scheme isn't on the qualifying-earnings basis. Per TPR's phasing guidance:
   - Set 1 (basic pay): 4% employer, 9% total.
   - Set 2 (pensionable pay that is at least 85% of earnings): 3% employer, 8% total.
   - Set 3 (all earnings): 3% employer, 7% total.
3. **2023 Act reforms are not in force.** The Pensions (Extension of Automatic Enrolment) Act 2023 only gives the government powers to lower the age to 18 and remove the LEL. Implementation needs regulations, and the 2026/27 review kept the LEL at £6,240. So the current 22-to-SPA age band and the LEL stay in the DD for now.
