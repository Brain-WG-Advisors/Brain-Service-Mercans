Title: Child Labor (Tennessee Department of Labor and Workforce Development)
Tennessee's Child Labor Law includes protections for youth ("minors") 14 to 17 years old ... Employers are generally subject to both state child labor laws and the federal child labor provisions of the FLSA, 29 U.S.C. 212(c), and 29 CFR Part 570. Employers must follow whichever provisions (state or federal) are "more restrictive".
Working Hours - 14/15-year-olds: While school is in session: no more than 3 hours per day; no earlier than 7:00 am and no later than 7:00 pm; no more than 18 hours per week. While school is not in session: no more than 8 hours per day; no earlier than 6:00 am and no later than 9:00 pm; no more than 40 hours per week.
16/17-year-olds: may not be employed during hours the minor is required to attend class (T.C.A. 50-5-105(b)(1)); not between 10:00 pm and 6:00 am Sunday through Thursday preceding a school day (50-5-105(b)(2)); with a valid Parental Consent Form in the personnel file, may work until midnight but no more than three nights per week Sunday-Thursday (50-5-105(b)(2)(A)); form valid until end of school year, end of employment or majority; original kept at the minor's work location. Church-related school / home-school exception with director's letter (50-5-105(c)).
Break or Meal Periods: a minor must be provided a 30-minute unpaid break or meal period if scheduled to work six consecutive hours; not during or before the first hour of the work day (T.C.A. 50-5-115). The ample-opportunity exemption (50-2-103(h)(1)(A)) and break waiver (50-2-103(h)(2)) do not apply to employees under 18.
