Title: Tax Rates | Missouri Department of Labor and Industrial Relations

"Until eligible for an experience rate, regular employers ... are assigned an annual tax rate that is the average tax rate computed during the preceding year of all employers within the industrial classification division to which the employer is assigned (prior to CRA), or 2.7 percent, whichever is the highest."

"Rates could range from 0.0% to 6.0%, not including maximum rate surcharge and/or contribution rate adjustment."
Maximum Rate Surcharge: "If an employer has been at the maximum experience rate for two consecutive years, a surcharge of one-quarter percent is added ... up to one percent ... an additional surcharge of one-half percent ... In no case shall the surcharge exceed one and one-half percent in any given year."
Contribution Rate Adjustment (CRA): "A contribution rate adjustment is a percentage increase or decrease to the tax rate that is based on the average cash balance of the Unemployment Trust Fund. Rates may be increased by 10 percent, 20 percent or 30 percent, or reduced by 7 percent or 12 percent."
Automation Adjustment: "Effective January 1, 2023, state law (House Bill 2168) allows the Division ... to redirect to the Unemployment Automation Fund ... No additional tax amounts are due in the first quarter. ... not to exceed 0.020% of an employer's total taxable wages ..."
Federal Interest Assessment: "... Missouri has paid off the Federal loan. A Federal Interest Assessment is not expected to be necessary for calendar year 2026."
Credit Reduction: "Missouri is currently not a credit reduction state."
Taxable Wage Limitations: 2026 $9,000; 2025 $9,500; 2024 $10,000; 2023 $10,500; 2022 $11,000.
