## Resposta

Don't model Irish PAYE as a fixed progressive bracket table. The 20%/40% rates are national, but the band (Standard Rate Cut-Off Point, SRCOP) and the tax credits are set per employee by Revenue and delivered on the RPN. So the regulation should be a two-rate formula that takes employee-level inputs and runs cumulatively.

**Calculation (cumulative basis, the default)**

1. Cumulative taxable pay = previous-employment pay from the RPN + year-to-date taxable pay including the current period.
2. Cumulative SRCOP = annual SRCOP ÷ 52 × income tax week (or ÷ 12 × month).
3. Gross tax = 20% × min(cumulative pay, cumulative SRCOP) + 40% × the excess.
4. Subtract cumulative credits (annual credits prorated the same way).
5. Tax this period = cumulative tax payable − tax already deducted, including prior-employment tax from the RPN. A negative result is a refund.

Revenue states the rule as: apply the standard rate up to the cut-off point, apply the higher rate on the balance above it, add the two amounts, and subtract the tax credits. Under the cumulative basis, the tax for any pay period is the cumulative tax payable less the tax already deducted during the year. When the RPN shows earlier income in the year, it includes previous pay and the tax paid on it, and the employer must factor these into the cumulative calculation.

**Inputs the regulation needs**

- Per employee, from the RPN: annual SRCOP, annual tax credits, tax basis flag, previous pay, and previous tax.
- Country-level, effective-dated: the 20% and 40% rates, plus the single-person SRCOP used for emergency tax.

**Branches by tax basis**

- **Week 1 / Month 1:** the same formula, but using only the current period's share of the band and credits, with no year-to-date amounts and no refunds.
- **Emergency, PPSN provided but no RPN:** the employer gives the employee the single-person cut-off point for their first four weeks, taxes at the standard rate up to week 4, and taxes everything at the higher rate from week 5.
- **Emergency, no PPSN:** tax at the higher rate with no tax credits.
- **Pay frequency:** non-weekly frequencies use specific cumulative week numbers. For example, fortnightly pay uses the cumulative position at week 2 for payments in week 1 or 2, week 4 for weeks 3 or 4, and so on.

**Effective-dated values (needed for the emergency default and for validation)**

- 2026: the single band stays at €44,000, unchanged from 2025, and the Personal and Employee credits are €2,000 each.
- 2027 (Budget 2027): the single band rises to €46,500, single parent to €50,500, married one-income to €55,500, and married two-income to up to €93,000. The Personal and Employee credits each rise to €2,125.

USC is a separate regulation, and it is the one that actually looks like a classic multi-band table. Its cut-offs also come on the RPN.

**Sources**

- Revenue, Methods of calculating tax: the "Rule for calculating tax" page and the "Cumulative basis" page (Example 2 is a worked cumulative case).
- Employers' Guide to PAYE, TDM Part 42-04-35A, Chapter 9: §9.3 Cumulative Basis and §9.5 Emergency Basis. Both sections were updated per eBrief 043/26.
- Income Tax (Employments) Regulations, Regulation 4. It empowers Revenue to determine each employee's tax credits and SRCOP, which is why these are inputs to the regulation and not configuration.
