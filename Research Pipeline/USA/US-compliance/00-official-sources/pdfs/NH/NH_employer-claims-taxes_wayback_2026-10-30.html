Employer Claims & Taxes

Reporting and tax requirements for New Hampshire employers

In New Hampshire, every employer must file a Tax and Wage Report for every calendar quarter while providing employment. The report serves two purposes: to provide wage information about individual employees to be used in determining potential entitlement to benefits; and to establish the amount of taxable wages paid each quarter so that taxes due may be calculated and paid timely. These taxes support the payment of unemployment benefits as well as employer services and programs to assist with recruitment and training.

Tax Rates

All private for-profit employers in New Hampshire, and some public and private non-profit employers, must pay unemployment compensation taxes when they file their quarterly tax and wage report. Each new employer starts with a new employer tax rate of 2.7% which is paid on the first $14,000 in annual wages paid to each employee. Following your first year as an employer, your tax rate will be adjusted based upon your prior experience, both in terms of paying taxes timely as well as how much in unemployment benefits the state has paid out to your former employees.

  • The New Hampshire new employer tax rate is 2.7 % Minus any Fund Balance Reduction in place for the applicable quarter
  • See the Tax Rate Chart to determine the current quarter applicable Fund Balance Reduction

Tax Audits

New Hampshire is required by the Federal Government to conduct random and targeted employer audits to ensure compliance with the Unemployment Compensation program. These audits are conducted by the department's field agents.

Employer Support

New Hampshire employers may contact department field agents for help with tax and reporting related questions.
