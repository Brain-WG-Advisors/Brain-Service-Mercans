# Module 4: Gross Compensation

*Purpose: To define all components of gross pay and their calculation rules.*

## 4.1 Minimum Wage

### Is there a minimum wage? Who is eligible?

**Yes.** Colombia sets a single national statutory minimum monthly wage (*Salario Mínimo Mensual Legal
Vigente*, SMMLV), fixed annually by government decree under Law 278 of 1996 art. 8.

> **2026: COP 1,750,905 per month.**

Eligibility is universal across the private sector. Labour Code art. 145 defines the minimum wage as that
which *"every employee is entitled to receive in order to meet their normal needs and those of their
family"*. It applies to every employee under the Substantive Labour Code, including:

| Category | Entitlement |
|---|---|
| Full-time employees | Full monthly minimum wage |
| Part-time employees | **Proportional** to time worked — there is no separate part-time minimum rate |
| Daily-rated and piece-rate workers | The minimum wage converted to the applicable period. Circular 101 of 2025 §2.3–2.4 confirms expressly that piece-rate workers, including in agriculture, are **not** excluded from minimum-wage or maximum-hours rules |
| Domestic workers, family drivers, day workers, farm workers | Covered — Labour Code art. 306 par., as amended by Law 1788 of 2016 |
| Apprentices | **Not** on the minimum wage as such. They receive a maintenance support payment set as a percentage of it — see Module 4.2 |
| Employees on all-inclusive salary | Necessarily above, since the floor is 13 minimum wages |

**There is no lower youth, trainee or probationary minimum wage.** Labour Code art. 143, as amended by
Law 1496 of 2011, prohibits salary differences by age, gender, sex, nationality, race, religion, political
opinion or trade-union activity, and presumes any differential treatment unjustified until the employer
demonstrates objective differentiating factors.

**The legal instrument for 2026 requires a specific note.** The 2026 minimum wage of COP 1,750,905 — a 23%
increase over the 2025 figure of COP 1,423,500, a nominal rise of COP 327,405 — was fixed by **Decree 1469
of 29 December 2025**. That decree was **provisionally suspended** by the Council of State (Section Two,
Subsection A) in annulment proceeding 11001-03-25-000-2026-00004-00 by order of 12 February 2026, and the
Government complied by issuing **Decree 0159 of 19 February 2026**, which re-fixed the identical amount
transitorily. On **9 July 2026** the Council of State **revoked** the provisional suspension — reported
17 July 2026 — on the ground that the interim decree was materially equivalent to the suspended one, so
the injunction had neutralised no concrete harm. **Decree 1469 of 2025 therefore recovered full force and
is the operative instrument as at the date of this guide**, with Decree 0159 of 2026 spent.

**The amount COP 1,750,905 has been continuously in force since 1 January 2026 and has never been in
dispute** — only the instrument supporting it changed. The underlying annulment action on the merits
remains pending, so the value stays litigation-exposed and must be re-verified before any 2027 cut-over.

### Is the minimum wage applicable at a national level or does it vary by province/state?

**Single national rate.** COP 1,750,905 applies in all 32 departments and the Capital District, in urban
and rural areas alike, in every economic sector. There is:

- no departmental or municipal minimum wage;
- no sectoral or occupational minimum wage;
- no cost-of-living zone differential;
- no separate rural minimum wage — Labour Code art. 146 num. 2 allows the *fixing* of the wage to take
  account of employer-provided housing, crops and fuel that reduce rural living costs, but this informs
  the national negotiation, it does not produce a second rate.

The only companion national figure is the **statutory transport allowance**, also uniform nationally, and
payable in every place where public transport is available (see Module 4.2).

### Does the minimum wage impact any tax or social security calculations?

**Yes — pervasively. The minimum wage is the index for nearly every social-security parameter in
Colombia**, while the tax unit indexes the tax parameters. The two are entirely separate calibers and must
never be mixed.

| Parameter | Rule | 2026 value |
|---|---|---|
| **Contribution-base floor** | *"In no case may the contribution base be lower than one minimum wage"* (Law 100 art. 18) | 1,750,905 |
| **Contribution-base ceiling** | 25 minimum wages (Law 100 art. 18) | 43,772,625 |
| **Solidarity pension fund trigger** | Base ≥ 4 minimum wages → 1% (Law 100 art. 20) | 7,003,620 |
| **Subsistence sub-account bands** | 16–17, 17–18, 18–19, 19–20, above 20 minimum wages | 28,014,480 upward |
| **Payroll-levy and employer-health exemption** | Employee earns **less than** 10 minimum wages (Tax Code art. 114-1) | 17,509,050 |
| **All-inclusive salary floor** | Ordinary salary above 10 minimum wages; total not less than 13 (Labour Code art. 132 num. 2) | 22,761,765 |
| **Transport allowance eligibility** | Up to 2 minimum wages (Decree 1470 of 2025) | 3,501,810 |
| **Work-clothing eligibility** | Up to 2 minimum wages (Labour Code art. 230) | 3,501,810 |
| **Dismissal-indemnity tier** | Below vs at/above 10 minimum wages (Labour Code art. 64) | 17,509,050 |
| **Salary-in-kind cap** | Maximum 50% of total salary, or **30%** where the employee earns the legal minimum wage (Labour Code art. 129) | — |
| **Late-payment penalty split** | Employees earning **less than 1 minimum wage** stay on the uncapped original rule (Labour Code art. 65 par. 2) | 1,750,905 |
| **Apprentice contribution base** | Must in every case be at least 1 minimum wage (Decree 0223 of 2026 art. 2.2.6.3.3.4 par. 2) | 1,750,905 |

By contrast, **no income-tax threshold is expressed in minimum wages.** The withholding table, the
exemption caps, the deduction limits and the meal-payment thresholds are all in tax units (COP 52,374 for
2026). This is the one-caliber discipline: minimum wage for social security and labour; tax unit for tax.

### How are deductions handled if they cannot be made due to minimum wage constraints?

Labour Code art. 149 num. 2 is the controlling rule:

> *"Nor may a withholding or deduction be made **without a court order, even where there is written
> authorisation from the employee**, whenever it would affect the legal or collectively agreed **minimum wage** or the
> portion of salary declared **exempt from attachment** by law."*

So a deduction that would take pay below the minimum wage — or into the non-attachable portion — is
prohibited **even with the employee's written authorisation**, unless a court has ordered it.

**Deduction priority and the protected floor.** The engine applies deductions in this order and stops when
the protected floor is reached:

| Priority | Deduction | May breach the minimum wage? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mandatory social security (health, pension, solidarity fund) | **Yes** — these are compulsory contributions under Labour Code art. 150, applied to the contribution base which is itself floored at 1 minimum wage |
| 2 | Withholding tax | **Yes** — a statutory withholding, not a "deduction from salary" in the art. 149 sense. In practice an employee at the minimum wage falls in the 0% band, so this is theoretical |
| 3 | Court-ordered garnishments (maintenance, judicial attachment) | **Yes**, within the attachability rules below — a court order is exactly the exception art. 149 contemplates |
| 4 | Union dues, cooperative and savings-fund dues, disciplinary sanctions under approved work rules, the higher-education solidarity contribution | **No** |
| 5 | Employee-authorised voluntary deductions: payroll loans, employer advances, insurance, voluntary pension | **No** |

**The non-attachable portion — Labour Code arts. 154–156.**

| Rule | Effect |
|---|---|
| Art. 154 | Salary is **not attachable** as a general rule |
| Art. 155 | The **excess over the minimum wage** is attachable up to **one fifth (20%)** of that excess |
| Art. 156 | **Exception:** for **maintenance obligations** and for debts owed to cooperatives, up to **50% of the whole salary**, including the minimum wage, is attachable |

**Handling an unpayable deduction.** Where the protected floor is reached:

1. **Do not partially apply a court-ordered garnishment beyond the statutory cap** — apply it to the cap
   and record the shortfall against the order.
2. **Suspend the voluntary deduction for the period** and carry the unpaid instalment forward. The
   employer must **not** unilaterally reschedule the loan; Labour Code art. 151 requires the instalment and
   amortisation period to be agreed in writing, and unilateral variation entitles the employee to go to
   the labour inspector.
3. **Notify the employee** of the suspended deduction on the payslip, as an explicit informational line
   with a zero or reduced amount, so the arrears are visible.
4. **Never** recover the shortfall by set-off in a later period without a fresh written authorisation, and
   never at a rate that breaches the floor again.

Note the asymmetry the engine must implement: the **minimum-wage floor protects net pay against voluntary
deductions**, but the **contribution base is separately floored at 1 minimum wage**, so an employee working
a partial month still contributes on a full minimum wage even though the actual salary is lower. These are
two different floors doing two different jobs.

*Legal basis: Substantive Labour Code arts. 129, 132, 143, 145, 146, 149, 150, 151, 154, 155, 156, 230,
306; Decree 1469 of 29 December 2025 art. 1; Decree 1470 of 2025 art. 1; Law 100 of 1993 arts. 18 and 20;
Tax Code art. 114-1; Law 2466 of 2025 arts. 21, 22, 53; Decree 0223 of 5 March 2026 art. 2.2.6.3.3.4;
Ministry of Labour Circular 101 of 2025 §§2.3, 2.4; Council of State, proceeding
11001-03-25-000-2026-00004-00, orders of 12 February 2026 and 9 July 2026.*

## 4.2 Salary Components

### What are the mandatory and common salary components?

| Component | Mandatory? | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic salary** | Mandatory | The agreed remuneration for the ordinary working day. May be by unit of time, by task, by piece or by output (Labour Code art. 132 num. 1) |
| **Transport allowance** | **Mandatory** where the employee earns up to 2 minimum wages and public transport is available | COP 249,095 per month for 2026 |
| **Connectivity allowance** | **Mandatory** for a teleworker earning less than 2 minimum wages, **in place of** the transport allowance | COP 249,095. Never paid together with the transport allowance |
| **Overtime** | Mandatory where worked | Hours beyond the ordinary or maximum working day |
| **Night premium** | Mandatory where worked | 35% on the value of day work, for hours between 19:00 and 06:00 |
| **Obligatory rest-day / holiday premium** | Mandatory where worked | 80% to 30 June 2026, 90% from 1 July 2026 |
| **Statutory service bonus** | Mandatory | 30 days' salary per year, half by 30 June and half by 20 December |
| **Severance savings** | Mandatory | One month's salary per year of service |
| **Interest on severance savings** | Mandatory | 12% per year on the 31 December balance |
| **Vacation pay** | Mandatory | 15 working days per year of service |
| **Work clothing and footwear** | Mandatory in kind, for employees up to 2 minimum wages with more than 3 months' service | Three deliveries per year |
| **Commissions and sales percentages** | Common, contractual | **Salary** by express statutory definition (Labour Code art. 127) |
| **Habitual bonuses** | Common, contractual | **Salary** where habitual, unless a written non-salary pact applies |
| **Occasional gratuities** | Common | **Not** salary (Labour Code art. 128) |
| **Extra-statutory bonuses** — vacation, service, Christmas | Common, contractual | Not salary where the parties have **expressly** so agreed in writing |
| **Salary in kind** — food, housing, clothing | Common | **Salary** where ordinary and permanent; capped at 50% of total salary, 30% at the minimum wage |
| **Representation expenses, work tools, means of transport** | Common | **Not** salary — given to enable the work, not to enrich the employee |
| **Meal payments to third parties** | Common | Special tax regime under Tax Code art. 387-1 |
| **Apprentice maintenance support** | Mandatory for apprentices | 75% or 100% of the minimum wage by phase |

### What is the general definition and pay cycle for each component?

| Component | Pay cycle |
|---|---|
| Basic salary | Each payroll period (semi-monthly or monthly in practice) |
| Transport / connectivity allowance | Each payroll period, alongside salary; prorated for days worked |
| Overtime, night premium, rest-day premium | With the salary of the period worked, or at the latest the **following** period (Labour Code art. 134 num. 2) |
| Commissions | As contractually agreed; enter the period in which they fall due |
| Service bonus | **Two instalments** — by 30 June and by 20 December |
| Severance savings | Liquidated at 31 December; **deposited to the employee's fund by 14 February**. Optionally, the employer may deposit 8.33% monthly as an advance deposit (Law 2466 of 2025 art. 64) |
| Interest on severance savings | Paid **to the employee** during January. Optionally monthly at 1% by written agreement (Law 2466 of 2025 art. 64) |
| Vacation pay | When leave is granted, or on termination |
| Work clothing | **30 April, 31 August, 20 December** (Labour Code art. 232) |

### Are components uniform across the country or do they differ by state/province?

**Uniform.** Every component above is set by national statute and applies identically in all 32
departments and the Capital District. There is no departmental variation in rates, thresholds or
entitlements.

Three narrow points of non-uniformity, none of which is a component definition:

1. The **occupational-risk class and rate** vary by *work centre* (not by region), so two employees of the
   same company in different plants can carry different employer risk rates.
2. The **family compensation fund** is organised by department, so the receiving institution code differs
   even though the 4% rate does not.
3. Small-business payroll-levy scales under Law 1429 of 2010 carry a **special regime for Amazonas,
   Guainía and Vaupés**, visible in the contribution-return rate tables. This affects only qualifying
   micro and small enterprises in those departments, not the standard corporate payroll.

### What is the calculation logic for each salary component?

Let `S` = contractual monthly salary, `D` = hourly divisor (220 to 14 July 2026, **210** from 15 July
2026), `H` = hours.

| Component | Formula |
|---|---|
| Basic salary, full month | `S` |
| Basic salary, partial month | `S × days worked ÷ 30` |
| Ordinary hour value | `S ÷ D` |
| Transport allowance | `249,095 × days worked ÷ 30`, if salary ≤ 2 minimum wages |
| Night premium | `(S ÷ D) × 0.35 × H` for hours 19:00–06:00 |
| Overtime, day | `(S ÷ D) × 1.25 × H` |
| Overtime, night | `(S ÷ D) × 1.75 × H` |
| Rest-day / holiday premium | `(S ÷ D) × 0.80 × H` to 30 June 2026; `× 0.90` from 1 July 2026 — **in addition to** the paid rest day itself and the hours actually worked (see Module 4.4) |
| Service bonus | `(salary + transport allowance) × days in the semester ÷ 360`, capped at 15 days per semester |
| Severance savings | `(salary + transport allowance) × days of service ÷ 360` |
| Interest on severance savings | `severance savings balance × 12% × days ÷ 360` |
| Vacation pay | `(ordinary salary **excluding** overtime and rest-day work) × working days of vacation ÷ 30`; variable pay → average of the preceding year |
| Commissions | Contractual; enter the base of the period in which they fall due |
| Salary in kind | Contractually valued, or by expert appraisal; capped at 50% / 30% of total salary |

**The hourly divisor is the single biggest 2026 calculation change.** Ministry of Labour Circular 101 of
22 September 2025 publishes the table:

| From | Weekly hours | Hours per working day | **Monthly divisor** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 July 2023 | 47 | 7.83 | 235 |
| 15 July 2024 | 46 | 7.66 | 230 |
| 15 July 2025 | 44 | 7.33 | **220** |
| **15 July 2026** | **42** | **7** | **210** |

The legacy divisor of 240 is obsolete and understates every premium by about 14.3%. A **July 2026 payroll
spans both divisors** and must be split at 15 July.

### Deliverable: Define the primary earnings base for social security contributions — comprehensive Inclusion/Exclusion analysis

**The primary earnings base is the *Ingreso Base de Cotización* (contribution base), defined by Law 100 of
1993 article 18 as substituted by Law 797 of 2003 article 5**, and constituted by the payments that are
*salary* under Labour Code arts. 127 and 128.

**INCLUDED in the contribution base**

| Item | Reason |
|---|---|
| Basic salary, fixed or variable | Labour Code art. 127 — "remuneración ordinaria, fija o variable" |
| Overtime | Art. 127 — "the value of supplementary work or of overtime hours" |
| Work on obligatory rest days | Art. 127 — "the value of work on mandatory rest days" |
| Night premium | Consideration for the service |
| Commissions and sales percentages | Art. 127 — "porcentajes sobre ventas y comisiones" |
| Habitual bonuses without a written non-salary pact | Art. 127 — "bonificaciones habituales" |
| Extra-salary payments (*sobresueldos*) and salary premiums | Art. 127 — "primas, sobresueldos" |
| Salary in kind — food, housing, clothing provided as ordinary and permanent consideration | Art. 129, subject to the 50% / 30% caps |
| **Vacation pay** | Paid rest, not a statutory benefit; contributions continue during leave |
| **Paid statutory leave** — bereavement, voting, medical appointments, domestic calamity | Paid rest days |
| **Non-salary payments exceeding 40% of total remuneration** | Law 1393 of 2010 art. 30 forces the excess back in |
| **70% of an all-inclusive salary** | Law 100 art. 18 final paragraph |

**EXCLUDED from the contribution base**

| Item | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Transport allowance** | Non-salary statutory allowance (Law 15 of 1959; Decree 1470 of 2025 recitals) |
| **Connectivity allowance** | Expressly *"does not constitute salary"* (Law 2466 of 2025 art. 53) |
| **Service bonus** | Labour Code art. 307 — *"is not salary, nor shall it be counted as a salary factor in any case"* |
| **Severance savings and the interest on them** | Statutory benefits under Titles VIII–IX, excluded by art. 128 |
| **Work clothing and footwear** | Provided to enable the work (art. 128) |
| Occasional and purely gratuitous sums, occasional bonuses and gratuities | Art. 128 |
| Profit participation and solidarity-economy surpluses | Art. 128 |
| Representation expenses, means of transport, work tools, protective equipment | Art. 128 — not to enrich the employee |
| Habitual or occasional benefits with an **express written** non-salary pact — food, housing, clothing, extra-statutory bonuses | Art. 128, **but only up to the 40% clamp** |
| Sickness, maternity and paternity benefits paid by the health insurer | Not employer salary; contributions continue on the underlying base |
| Third-party meal payments within the tax-law thresholds | Not employee income (Tax Code art. 387-1) |
| Reimbursement of documented business expenses | Not consideration for the service |
| The 30% of an all-inclusive salary above the contribution base | Law 100 art. 18 |
| Amounts above the **25-minimum-wage ceiling** | Law 100 art. 18 |

**Contrast with the payroll-levy base.** For the family compensation fund, apprenticeship training levy and
family welfare levy, the base is the *"nómina mensual de salarios"* of **Law 21 of 1982 art. 17**: *"the
totality of the payments made in respect of the various constituent elements of salary … and in addition,
those made for statutory and collectively agreed or contractual **paid rest periods**."* Two differences
follow, and the engine must model them separately:

1. The payroll-levy base **has no ceiling** — nothing is capped at 25 minimum wages.
2. The payroll-levy base **expressly includes paid rest days**, statutory and contractual.

## 4.3 Proration

### Are fixed wages prorated for the number of working days?

**Yes.** Fixed salary is prorated on a **30-day commercial month**, irrespective of the actual number of
calendar days in the month. February and March are both 30-day months for payroll purposes. This is the
convention the Labour Code applies throughout, and it is the basis on which the electronic payroll XML
carries a *days worked* attribute.

The rule and its consequences:

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Full month worked | Full monthly salary, whether the month has 28, 30 or 31 calendar days |
| Mid-month hire or termination | `S × days worked ÷ 30` |
| Unpaid absence | Deduct `S × days absent ÷ 30` |
| A 31-day month worked in full | Still 30 days for payroll; the 31st is absorbed |
| February worked in full | Still 30 days; days 29 and 30 are notional |
| Transport allowance | Prorated on the same 30-day basis |
| Statutory benefits | Prorated on a **360-day** year — the annual analogue of the same convention |

**Not prorated:** the **contribution-base floor** of 1 minimum wage. A partial month still contributes on
a full minimum wage where the prorated salary falls below it, unless a contribution-return change event
(hire, termination, unpaid leave, sick leave) legitimately reduces the contributory days.

### Describe the statutory or standard business practice for prorating fixed salary components for mid-period hires and terminations.

There is **no single Labour Code article prescribing the 30-day divisor**; it is the settled convention
derived from art. 249 (severance savings "for each year of service and proportionally for any fraction of a
year"), art. 306 (service bonus "proporcionalmente al tiempo trabajado") and art. 186 (vacation), all of
which prorate on a 360-day year of twelve 30-day months. The Ministry of Labour, the contribution-return
system and the tax authority all operate on that convention.

**Mid-period hire.**

1. Count days from the hire date to month end **inclusive of the hire date**.
2. Where the hire date is the 31st, the day is counted as day 30 — a 31-day month never produces 31
   payroll days.
3. Prorate basic salary and the transport allowance at `× days ÷ 30`.
4. Fixed non-salary allowances follow the same proration unless the contract says otherwise.
5. Variable pay (overtime, commissions) is not prorated; it is paid as earned.
6. Contributions run on the prorated base, floored at 1 minimum wage, with a *hire* change event on the
   contribution return carrying the contributory day count.
7. Withholding tax under Procedure 1: for a period shorter than 30 days, divide the payments by the days,
   multiply by 30, read the table percentage, then apply that percentage to the actual payments of the
   period (Tax Code art. 385).

**Mid-period termination.**

1. Count days from the 1st to the termination date **inclusive**.
2. Prorate basic salary and the transport allowance.
3. Settle all accrued statutory benefits to the termination date — service bonus, severance savings,
   interest, unused vacation (Module 9.1).
4. Contributions run on the prorated base with a *termination* change event.
5. Deliver the **social-security payment evidence for the last three months within 60 days**, failing
   which the termination does not take effect (Labour Code art. 65 par. 1).

**Mid-period salary change.** Split the month at the effective date and prorate each rate over its own
days. The contribution base is the sum of both segments, and a *salary variation* change event is reported.

**A 2026-specific split.** July 2026 contains **two mid-month cut-overs two weeks apart** — the rest-day
premium steps from 80% to 90% on **1 July**, and the 42-hour week with the 210 divisor starts on
**15 July**. A July 2026 payroll must be split at both dates. Do not collapse them.

### Provide the proration formula and a worked example.

**Formulae**

```
Prorated salary            = monthly salary × days worked ÷ 30
Prorated transport allowance = 249,095 × days worked ÷ 30       (if salary <= 2 minimum wages)
Ordinary hour value        = monthly salary ÷ 220   (to 14 Jul 2026)
                           = monthly salary ÷ 210   (from 15 Jul 2026)
Service bonus              = (salary + transport allowance) × days in semester ÷ 360
Severance savings          = (salary + transport allowance) × days of service ÷ 360
Interest on severance      = severance savings × 12% × days ÷ 360
Vacation accrual           = salary (ex overtime and rest-day work) × days ÷ 720
                             [15 working days per 360 days = days ÷ 24 of a month]
```

## 4.4 Overtime

### Detail the statutory rules for overtime, including eligibility criteria and mandatory rates.

**The maximum ordinary working week.** Labour Code art. 161, as substituted by Law 2466 of 2025 art. 11:
a maximum of **8 hours per day** and **42 hours per week**, distributable by agreement over **five or six
days**, always preserving the rest day and **without reducing salary**. Law 2101 of 2021 art. 3 sets the
gradual path; the final step to 42 hours takes effect on **15 July 2026**.

| Period | Maximum ordinary week |
|---|---|
| 15 July 2025 – 14 July 2026 | 44 hours |
| **From 15 July 2026** | **42 hours** |

Where the week is distributed over five days so that the employee rests on Saturday and/or Sunday, the
ordinary daily shift may be extended by **up to two hours without paying supplementary work** (Labour Code
art. 164, confirmed by Circular 101 of 2025 §2.1).

**Eligibility.** Overtime is due to every employee whose hours exceed the agreed ordinary day or, absent
agreement, the statutory maximum. Exclusions and special regimes:

| Category | Position |
|---|---|
| Management, trust and confidential staff (*empleados de dirección, confianza y manejo*) | Excluded from the maximum-hours rules. **Law 2466 of 2025 art. 70 repealed Labour Code art. 162 literal b)** — the exclusion for intermittent-activity workers and domestic staff is gone; only the direction/trust/management exclusion of literal a) survives |
| Employees on all-inclusive salary | Overtime is compensated in advance by the salary itself (Labour Code art. 132 num. 2) — **except vacation**, which is never absorbed |
| Employees on 6-hour / 36-hour-week successive shift systems | **No** night premium and **no** rest-day premium apply (Labour Code arts. 161(d) and 168 num. 1) |
| Flexible daily shifts within 42 hours per week over up to 6 days, 4 to 9 continuous hours per day | **No** supplementary-work premium (Labour Code art. 161(a)) |
| Minors 15–17 | 6 hours per day, 30 hours per week, daytime only |
| Minors over 17 | 8 hours per day, 40 hours per week |
| Piece-rate and agricultural workers | **Included** — Circular 101 of 2025 §§2.3–2.4 is explicit that they are not excluded |

**Volume limits.** Labour Code art. 22 of Law 50 of 1990, as substituted by **Law 2466 of 2025 art. 13**:
*"In no case may overtime hours, whether daytime or night-time, exceed two (2) hours per day
and twelve (12) per week."* Exceptions: private security (Law 1920 of 2018) and the health sector. Circular
101 derives the resulting maxima of ordinary plus overtime hours: **56 hours per week** to 14 July 2026 and
**54 hours per week** from 15 July 2026.

**Ministry authorisation abolished; a register imposed instead.** Law 2466 of 2025 art. 12 removed the
requirement for Ministry of Labour authorisation to work overtime, and replaced it with an **employer duty
to keep a supplementary-work register** recording, as a minimum: the activity performed, the total
supplementary hours, the split between day and night hours, and evidence of payment. It must be available
on demand to employees and to administrative and judicial authorities. Failure to keep it, or failure to
pay supplementary work, allows inspectors to **order the suspension of all supplementary work for six
months**.

**The night window moved.** Labour Code art. 160, as substituted by **Law 2466 of 2025 art. 10**: day work
is 06:00 to 19:00 and night work is **19:00 to 06:00**. Article 160 par. 2 delayed entry into force by six
months from the 25 June 2025 sanction, so the 19:00 boundary has applied **since 25 December 2025** and
governs the whole of 2026. Two extra hours per day now attract the night premium compared with the
pre-reform 21:00 boundary.

**Rates — Labour Code art. 168** (Law 50 of 1990 art. 24, **not amended** by the labour reform):

| Type | Premium | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Night work, by reason of being night work | **+35%** on the value of day work | Excludes the 36-hour shift system |
| Day overtime | **+25%** on the ordinary day rate | |
| Night overtime | **+75%** on the ordinary day rate | |

Article 168 num. 4 is critical: *"Each of the aforementioned premiums arises exclusively, that is to
say, **without being cumulated with any other**."* The night, day-overtime and night-overtime premiums are
alternatives, not cumulative with one another. The obligatory-rest-day premium of art. 179 is a separate
statutory head and **is** added to them.

**Work on an obligatory rest day or public holiday — Labour Code art. 179, as substituted by Law 2466 of
2025 art. 14.** The article sets the surcharge at 100%, with a transitional paragraph phasing it in:

| Period | Rest-day / holiday premium |
|---|---|
| 1 July 2025 – 30 June 2026 | **80%** |
| **1 July 2026 – 30 June 2027** | **90%** |
| From 1 July 2027 | 100% |

An employer may voluntarily apply 100% from the outset.

**The three-value structure.** Circular 101 of 2025 §4 is explicit that working on an obligatory rest day
generates **three independent payment obligations**, not one:

1. the paid rest day itself, at one day's ordinary salary (Labour Code art. 173) — *first value*;
2. the hours actually worked on that day — *second value*;
3. the additional premium on the hour value at 80% / 90% / 100% (art. 179) — *third value*.

*"Payment is made for the rest day, payment is made for the hours worked, and an additional premium is paid for working
on Sundays or public holidays."* An engine that pays only the hours plus the premium underpays by one day.

**Occasional versus habitual rest-day work.** Article 179 par. 1: work on up to **two** obligatory rest
days in a calendar month is *occasional*; **three or more** is *habitual*. Habitual rest-day work gives the
employee the right to a compensatory rest day or to the corresponding remuneration, at the employee's
election (Labour Code art. 180).

**Combined 2026 percentages** (art. 168 premiums applied exclusively, plus the art. 179 premium where the
day is an obligatory rest day):

| Hour type | Jan–Jun 2026 | Jul–Dec 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary day hour (06:00–19:00) | 100% (base) | 100% (base) |
| Night premium (19:00–06:00) | +35% | +35% |
| Day overtime | +25% | +25% |
| Night overtime | +75% | +75% |
| Rest-day / holiday premium, day hours | **+80%** | **+90%** |
| Night premium on a rest day / holiday | +35% +80% = **+115%** | +35% +90% = **+125%** |
| Day overtime on a rest day / holiday | +25% +80% = **+105%** | +25% +90% = **+115%** |
| Night overtime on a rest day / holiday | +75% +80% = **+155%** | +75% +90% = **+165%** |

**A defect in the official guidance, recorded for traceability.** Circular 101 of 2025 §5.1 contains an
internal inconsistency: after correctly stating the 80/90/100 phase-in in §4, it then states *"supplementary
work on Sundays and public holidays with a 75% premium (Labour Code art. 179)"* — the pre-reform figure. The
80/90/100 schedule in §4, which tracks the statutory text of art. 179 as substituted by Law 2466 art. 14,
governs. The 75% reference in §5.1 is a residual error and must not be configured.

### Explain how overtime is treated for income tax and social security calculations.

| Aspect | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Salary character** | Overtime and rest-day work are **salary** by express statutory definition — Labour Code art. 127 lists *"the value of supplementary work or of overtime hours"* and *"the value of work on mandatory rest days"* |
| **Social-security contribution base** | **Fully included.** No exemption, no cap other than the general 25-minimum-wage ceiling. Both employee (4% + 4% + solidarity fund) and employer (12% + 8.5% + occupational risk) contributions apply |
| **Payroll-levy base** | **Fully included** — Law 21 of 1982 art. 17 covers "the various constituent elements of salary" |
| **Statutory-benefit base** | **Included** in the service-bonus, severance-savings and interest bases. **Excluded from the vacation base** — Labour Code art. 192 num. 1 excludes *only* rest-day work and supplementary overtime hours from the vacation calculation. The **night premium stays in** the vacation base |
| **Withholding tax** | **Fully taxable.** Overtime enters the ordinary monthly taxable payments under both Procedure 1 and Procedure 2. There is **no** preferential or separate rate for overtime in Colombia, and no exempt overtime allowance |
| **Threshold effects** | A large overtime month can push the employee across the 10-minimum-wage payroll-levy exemption line, the 4-minimum-wage solidarity-fund trigger, or into a higher withholding band — all tested on that month's actual figures |
| **Payment timing** | With the salary of the period worked, or at the latest the **following** period (Labour Code art. 134 num. 2) |
| **Electronic payroll reporting** | Reported as discrete overtime and premium elements with a quantity, a start and end time, a percentage and an amount, under the annex's hour-type code table |

**A reporting conflict, now resolved by tax-authority doctrine.** The electronic payroll Technical Annex
§5.5.5 (still at version 1.0, February 2021) hard-codes the pre-reform rest-day percentages — 100 / 75 /
150 / 110 for the four rest-day hour types — while the statute requires 80% in the first half of 2026 and
90% in the second. **DIAN Concepto 013840 (int. 1568) of 1 October 2025** resolves the point: the annex's
published percentage *"must be read in accordance with the rules in force for each specific case"*,
and the coexistence of different premium percentages within a single month *"does not constitute any obstacle
to the generation, transmission and subsequent validation"* of the document. Employers must consolidate all
earnings of the period **including premiums settled at the statutorily applicable percentage**. The engine
therefore reports the real statutory percentage (80% or 90%), not the annex's legacy literal.

*Legal basis: Substantive Labour Code arts. 127, 134, 158, 159, 160, 161, 162, 164, 168, 169, 172, 173,
179, 180, 192 (as substituted by Law 2466 of 2025 arts. 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 70); Law 50 of 1990 arts.
22 and 24; Law 2101 of 2021 arts. 3 and 4; Ministry of Labour Circular 101 of 22 September 2025 §§2.1,
2.3, 2.4, 3, 4, 5, 5.1; DIAN Concepto 013840 int. 1568 of 1 October 2025; Law 21 of 1982 art. 17.*

## 4.5 Bonuses

### Is there a statutory requirement to pay bonuses? If so, how are they calculated?

**Yes — the statutory service bonus (*prima de servicios*) is mandatory for every private-sector
employee.**

Labour Code art. 306, as amended by **Law 1788 of 2016 art. 1**:

> *"The employer is obliged to pay to its employee or employees the social benefit known as the service
> bonus (prima de servicios), which shall correspond to **30 days of salary per year**, payable in two instalments, as follows: one
> half **by 30 June at the latest** and the other half **no later than the first twenty days of December**. It
> shall be recognised for the whole half-year worked or in proportion to the time worked.*
> *PARAGRAPH. This economic benefit includes domestic-service employees, family-service
> drivers, day workers and farm workers…"*

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Amount | **30 days' salary per year** — 15 days per semester |
| Instalments | Half by **30 June**, half by **20 December** |
| Proration | For the whole semester worked or proportionally to the time worked |
| Base | Salary **plus the transport allowance**. Variable pay enters at the average of the semester |
| Monthly accrual | **8.3333%** (30 ÷ 360) of the base |
| Legal character | **Not salary** — Labour Code art. 307: *"The annual bonus is not salary, nor shall it be counted as a salary factor in any case"* |
| Contribution base | **Excluded** |
| Payroll-levy base | **Excluded** |
| Coverage | Universal, including domestic workers, family drivers, day workers and farm workers |
| All-inclusive salary | Absorbed — the all-inclusive salary compensates statutory bonuses in advance (Labour Code art. 132 num. 2) |

**Set-off — Labour Code art. 308.** Contractual or Christmas bonuses are set off against the statutory
service bonus; where the statutory bonus is larger, the employer pays the difference. An employer already
paying a 13th-month equivalent does not pay twice.

**Formula and worked example.**

```
Service bonus = (monthly salary + transport allowance) × days worked in the semester ÷ 360
```

Employee on COP 2,400,000 plus the transport allowance of COP 249,095, having worked the full first
semester of 2026 (180 days):

```
(2,400,000 + 249,095) × 180 ÷ 360 = 2,649,095 × 0.5 = COP 1,324,547.50   payable by 30 June 2026
```

**Other statutory payments that are commonly but wrongly called bonuses.** Severance savings, the interest
on severance savings and vacation pay are separate statutory heads with their own rules, covered in
Modules 8 and 9. There is **no** statutory Christmas bonus, no statutory profit-sharing and no statutory
performance bonus in Colombia.

**Non-statutory bonuses** — performance, sign-on, retention, referral, extra-statutory Christmas and
vacation bonuses — are entirely contractual. They are **salary** if habitual, and therefore contributory,
**unless** the parties have expressly agreed in writing that they are not salary under Labour Code
art. 128, subject always to the 40% clamp.

### Is there a specific tax treatment for bonuses?

Yes, and it differs sharply between the two withholding procedures. This is one of the most commonly
mis-implemented rules in Colombian payroll.

| | **Withholding Procedure 1** | **Withholding Procedure 2** |
|---|---|---|
| **Statutory minimum service bonus** | Withheld **separately**, on its own read of the art. 383 table, **not** added to the month's salary. Tax Code art. 385: *"In the case of the statutory minimum service bonus in the private sector … the 'amount to withhold' is the one shown against the bracket into which the respective bonus falls"* | Folded into the **ordinary monthly base**; the fixed percentage is applied to the combined figure. The 13 divisor in the semi-annual calculation already prorates the bonus effect |
| **Any excess above the statutory minimum bonus** | Added to the ordinary monthly base | Ordinary monthly base |
| **Non-statutory bonuses** | Ordinary monthly base | Ordinary monthly base |
| **Severance savings and interest** | **Excluded** from the monthly base under both procedures — they have their own art. 206 num. 4 treatment | Same |

**Everything else about a bonus's tax treatment is ordinary.** Bonuses are taxable employment income,
enter the depuration of Tax Code art. 388, and benefit from the 25% exempt-income deduction within the
combined 40% and 1,340-tax-unit annual caps. There is **no** preferential bonus rate, **no** separate
annualisation method and **no** tax-free bonus allowance in Colombia.

**Worked example — Procedure 1, June 2026.** Employee on COP 8,000,000, receiving the first-half service
bonus of COP 4,000,000 in the same month, with no declared deductions.

| Line | Salary read | Service bonus read |
|---|---|---|
| Taxable payments | 8,000,000 | 4,000,000 |
| Less employee pension 4%, solidarity fund 1%, health 4% (on the salary only — the bonus is not contributory) | (720,000) | 0 |
| Subtotal | 7,280,000 | 4,000,000 |
| Less 25% exempt income | (1,820,000) | (1,000,000) |
| 40% cap check | 40% × 7,280,000 = 2,912,000 → ok | 40% × 4,000,000 = 1,600,000 → ok |
| Taxable base | 5,460,000 | 3,000,000 |
| In tax units (÷ 52,374) | 104.2502 | 57.2802 |
| Band | > 95 and ≤ 150 → 19% marginal | ≤ 95 → **0%** |
| **Withholding** | **92,049.30** | **0** |
| **Total June withholding** | | **92,049.30** |

Under **Procedure 2** the same facts would produce a single base of COP 12,000,000 less contributions and
the 25% exemption, to which the June-set fixed percentage is applied — a materially different and usually
higher result in that month, offset by lower withholding in others.

*Legal basis: Substantive Labour Code arts. 128, 132, 306, 307, 308 (art. 306 as amended by Law 1788 of
2016 art. 1); Tax Code arts. 206, 383, 385, 386, 388; Law 1393 of 2010 art. 30.*

## 4.6 Wage Classification (Country-Specific)

### Are there country-specific classifications for wages critical for social security or tax calculations?

**Yes — four, and they are the defining feature of Colombian payroll.** Getting any one of them wrong
mis-states either the contribution base, the levy base, the benefit base or the withholding base.

| # | Classification | Statutory source | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Salary vs non-salary** (*salario* vs *no constitutivo de salario*) | Labour Code arts. 127 and 128 | Whether a payment enters the contribution base, the payroll-levy base and the statutory-benefit base |
| 2 | **The 40% non-salary clamp** | Law 1393 of 2010 art. 30 | How much validly non-salary pay may nonetheless be excluded from the **contribution** base |
| 3 | **Ordinary salary vs all-inclusive salary** (*salario integral*) | Labour Code art. 132 num. 2–3; Law 100 art. 18 | Whether the contribution base and the payroll-levy base are 100% or **70%** of the salary, and which statutory benefits are absorbed |
| 4 | **Statutory benefits** (*prestaciones sociales*) vs salary vs paid rest | Labour Code Titles VIII–IX; art. 128 | Which payments are excluded from the contribution base, and which — like vacation — are not |

### Provide a detailed definition for each classification.

**1. Salary vs non-salary.**

Labour Code art. 127 (Law 50 of 1990 art. 14) defines salary as *"everything the employee receives in
money or in kind as direct consideration for the service, whatever the form or designation
adopted, such as bonuses, salary supplements, habitual gratuities, the value of supplementary work or of
overtime hours, the value of work on mandatory rest days, sales percentages and commissions."*

Article 128 (Law 50 of 1990 art. 15) excludes: occasional and purely gratuitous sums; profit participation
and solidarity-economy surpluses; amounts given *"not for the employee's benefit, nor to enrich their estate,
but in order to perform their duties properly"* — representation expenses, means of transport, work tools;
the statutory benefits of Titles VIII and IX; and *"habitual or occasional benefits or allowances
agreed collectively or contractually … where the parties have **expressly** stipulated that they do not
constitute salary"*, such as food, housing, clothing and extra-statutory vacation, service or Christmas
bonuses.

**Three tests for a valid non-salary pact:** it must be (a) **express**, (b) **written**, and (c) attach to
a *benefit or allowance*, not to consideration for the service itself. A "non-salary" label on what is
plainly remuneration for work will not survive a contribution audit.

**2. The 40% non-salary clamp.**

Law 1393 of 2010 art. 30, verbatim:

> *"Without prejudice to what is provided for other purposes, for the purposes related to articles 18 and 204
> of Law 100 of 1993, the labour payments of private-sector employees that do not constitute salary
> may not exceed 40% of total remuneration."*

A valid non-salary pact removes a payment from the labour-law salary base **permanently** — it never enters
the statutory-benefit base or the overtime base. But it removes the payment from the **social-security
contribution base only up to 40% of total remuneration**. Everything above that line is added back for
health, pension and occupational risk while remaining non-salary for the benefits. **Two divergent bases,
permanently.** The clamp bites on articles 18 and 204 of Law 100 only — it does not reach the payroll
levies, which have their own base.

**3. Ordinary salary vs all-inclusive salary.**

Labour Code art. 132 num. 2 (Law 50 of 1990 art. 18): where the employee earns an *ordinary* salary above
**10 minimum wages**, the parties may agree **in writing** a salary that additionally compensates in
advance the statutory benefits, premiums and allowances — night work, overtime, rest-day and holiday work,
statutory and extra-statutory bonuses, severance savings and their interest, subsidies and in-kind supplies
— **except vacation**.

| Element | Rule | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum | 10 minimum wages + a benefit factor of at least 30% | **13 minimum wages = COP 22,761,765** |
| Contribution base | **70%** of the all-inclusive salary (Law 100 art. 18 final paragraph; Decree 1072 art. 2.2.4.3.3 for occupational risk) | At the floor: 15,933,235.50, rounded up |
| Payroll-levy base | Also **70%** — Labour Code art. 132 num. 3: *"the contributions shall be reduced by thirty percent (30%)"* | |
| Vacation | **Still owed in full**, 15 working days per year, on the **whole** all-inclusive salary | |
| Tax | The former exemption of the benefit factor was replaced by the 25% exempt-income deduction (Tax Code art. 206 par. 2) | |

**4. Statutory benefits vs salary vs paid rest — and why vacation is the trap.**

Statutory benefits (*prestaciones sociales*) are employer-borne entitlements under Titles VIII and IX:
the service bonus, severance savings, the interest on them, work clothing and sickness assistance. They
are excluded from salary by art. 128 and therefore from the contribution base.

**Vacation is not a statutory benefit.** It is *paid rest*. The consequences are counter-intuitive and must
be configured deliberately:

- Vacation pay **enters the contribution base** — contributions continue during leave.
- Vacation pay **enters the payroll-levy base**, because Law 21 of 1982 art. 17 expressly covers
  *"descansos remunerados"*.
- Its own calculation base **excludes overtime and rest-day work only** (Labour Code art. 192 num. 1) — the
  night premium and commissions stay in.
- An all-inclusive salary does **not** absorb it (art. 132 num. 2 expressly excepts it), so vacation is owed
  on the **full** all-inclusive salary while contributions run on 70%.
