## Resposta

**Short answer:** for Ireland, don't use the default single Differential regulation. Use the RPN-driven **Threshold → Base → Amount triplet** that the production IE design (CT-A-192) uses. Ireland is the one documented exception to the "progressive tax = one Differential" rule.

## Why Ireland is the exception

The rulebook rule (TRG, Mohit 2 Jul) is that a progressive tax is one Differential regulation with annual bands, never split into threshold, base and amount steps. That rule assumes the band edges are statutory constants. In Ireland they aren't:

- **The 20%/40% boundary comes from the RPN for each employee.** It is the `RateCutOff` at Index 1, and it is €44,000, €48,000, €53,000 or up to €88,000 depending on status. Revenue also reallocates it across employments and can reduce it.
- **The RPN gives the cut-off already scaled.** On a cumulative basis it is the cumulative cut-off, and on Week 1 it is the cut-off for the period. Differential band columns are annual figures that the engine divides by frequency, so they would apply a second scaling to a number that is already cumulative or periodic.
- **Credits also come from the RPN** (`TaxCredits`), and so does the basis: `CUMULATIVE`, `WEEK_1` or `EMERGENCY`.

This is what the 2 July correction recorded. Learning #2 (splitting progressive tax into threshold, base and amount) was specific to the RPN. It was wrong to apply it to other countries, but it is right for Ireland. The production IE pattern is: rates via RPN in 52/53xxx, the triplet, Paid regulations, and reconciliation at ordinal 0 and at the end.

## Structure

**Flags (before regulation 1)**
- **IT basis flag (582xx):** 1 = Cumulative, 2 = Week 1, 3 = Emergency, bound to the RPN field. Values start at 1 and routing uses `LESS`/`GREATER`, never `EQUALS(x;0)`.
- **Emergency sub-cases:**
  - No PPSN: 40% on all pay.
  - PPSN provided, first period(s): a fraction of €44,000 (1/12 for the first month; 1/52 for weeks 1–4).
  - After that: nil cut-off.
  - Pay frequencies other than weekly, fortnightly, four-weekly, twice-monthly and monthly: higher rate only.
- **Exclusion Order flag:** suppresses PAYE.
- **Week 53:** forces the non-cumulative path for that one payment.

**Pay for Income Tax base (581xx/585xx)**
- Taxable series go into the matrix.
- Employee approved superannuation, PRSA, RAC and AVC contributions, plus Travel Pass and Cycle to Work salary sacrifice, go on the **credit side** as negatives.
- **My Future Fund stays out.** It gets no relief and is deducted from net pay.
- Keep a separate `_period` copy with credit 99998, because `PayForIncomeTax` (item 25) is reported.

**The triplet**

| Step | Does | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold | Available cut-off: RPN Index 1 (cumulative or period, per the basis flag), or the emergency cut-off | Formula (HR/RPN input) |
| Base | Splits Pay for Income Tax (YTD under the cumulative basis) into `MIN(P;C)` at the standard rate and `MAX(P−C;0)` at the higher rate | Flat, `_post_formula`, `$band_amount_raw` |
| Amount | 20% × standard portion + 40% × higher portion − RPN credits. Under the cumulative basis, minus tax already deducted YTD to get this period's PAYE | Flat, `_post_formula`, rates from 52xxx |

**Tail**
- A Paid regulation posts PAYE with debit 2610 and credit 2552.
- Reconciliation runs at ordinal 0 (Flag Reconciliation MTD) and at the end.

## Points to settle before building

1. **Exact Tax Rule Group strings.** The cumulative "YTD tax minus YTD paid" step probably composes `simple_tax` with `_ytd` and `_ytm` or `_negate_ytm` (the Canada precedent). Take the exact composition from CT-A-192 rather than guessing it.
2. **Refunds.** Negative period PAYE is a normal cumulative outcome in Ireland. The Amount step must not run the refund through a matrix sum, because matrix sums are clamped to 0. Confirm that the engine accepts a negative regulation output.
3. **Credits.** Decide whether they go in the Credit Amount band field or in the formula. Production carries three separate credit regulations, and our v1.0 design missed this.
4. **No engine logic for marginal relief or exemption limits.** Revenue builds these into the RPN.
5. **USC follows the same reasoning.** Its bands also come from the RPN, so it needs the same treatment rather than a static Differential.
